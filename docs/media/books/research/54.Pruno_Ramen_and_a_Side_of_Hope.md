# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Pruno, Ramen, and a Side of Hope: Stories of Surviving Wrongful Conviction

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Pruno, Ramen, and a Side of Hope: Stories of Surviving Wrongful Conviction
- **Author(s)**: Courtney B. Lance and Nikki D. Pope
- **Publication Year**: 2015 (February 24, 2015)
- **Publisher**: Post Hill Press
- **Format**: Paperback, audiobook (narrated by Whoopi Goldberg and Bill Kurtis)
- **Focus/Theme**: Life during and after wrongful incarceration; how exonerees maintained hope and survived prison
- **Key Statistics**: 281 pages

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
The book features multiple exonerees telling their stories of survival during wrongful incarceration and their experiences after release. While specific names of all featured exonerees are not explicitly identified in the available search results, the book includes:

- Multiple wrongfully convicted men and women sharing their personal stories
- Family members of exonerees describing how they maintained strength while their loved ones were incarcerated
- Stories spanning various types of cases from robbery to murder to convictions where no crime had been committed

The book focuses on the human experience of wrongful conviction rather than just the legal aspects, highlighting how exonerees:
- Maintained hope during incarceration, sometimes for more than 20 years
- Survived prison life and in some cases thrived despite overwhelming odds
- Faced challenges after exoneration when returning to a changed world

### Research Notes
"Pruno, Ramen, and a Side of Hope" takes a unique approach to the topic of wrongful convictions by focusing on the human experience of surviving incarceration and the challenges of life after exoneration. The title refers to prison culture - "pruno" is prison-made alcohol, and ramen noodles are a staple in prison commissaries.

The book weaves together personal narratives from exonerees and their families with insights into prison life, including details about prison yard gardens, contraband cooking, and family visits. These stories reveal not just the injustice of wrongful conviction but also the resilience of the human spirit in the face of extreme adversity.

What distinguishes this book from others on wrongful convictions is its focus on hope and survival rather than just the legal processes that led to conviction and exoneration. It explores how individuals maintained their humanity and hope in an environment designed to strip both away.

The book also addresses the often-overlooked challenges that exonerees face after release. While exoneration might seem like a happy ending, many exonerees struggle with finding employment, maintaining health, rebuilding relationships, and adjusting to technological and social changes that occurred during their incarceration.

In addition to the personal stories, the book includes a defense attorney's perspective on the immense effort required to overturn a guilty verdict and free an innocent person. It exposes systemic problems in the justice system and suggests ways to address these issues.

The book received recognition from the Northern California Innocence Project, which awarded the authors its 2015 Media Award for their contribution to raising awareness about wrongful convictions.
