# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 40: Strengthening Forensic Science in the United States: A Path Forward

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Strengthening Forensic Science in the United States: A Path Forward
- **Author(s)**: Committee on Identifying the Needs of the Forensic Science Community, National Research Council
- **Publication Year**: 2009
- **Publisher**: National Academies Press
- **Format**: Paperback, PDF (freely available)
- **Focus/Theme**: Comprehensive assessment of the state of forensic science in the United States and recommendations for improvement to reduce wrongful convictions
- **Key Statistics**: The report notes that forensic science analyses may have contributed to wrongful convictions of innocent people

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
Unlike the previous books researched, this report does not focus on specific named cases of wrongfully convicted individuals. Instead, it takes a systemic approach, examining the forensic science disciplines as a whole and how their shortcomings can contribute to wrongful convictions. The report references studies and analyses of wrongful convictions in the United States but does not detail specific cases.

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "The benefits of improving and regulating the forensic science disciplines are clear: assisting law enforcement officials, enhancing homeland security, and reducing the risk of wrongful conviction."
  - "Further improvements in forensic science practices should reduce the occurrence of wrongful convictions, which reduces the risk that true offenders continue to commit crimes while innocent persons inappropriately serve time."
  - "Forensic science analyses may have contributed to wrongful convictions of innocent people. This fact has demonstrated the potential danger of giving undue weight to evidence and testimony derived from imperfect testing and analysis."

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
  - Fragmentation of the forensic science system across federal, state, and local jurisdictions
  - Lack of mandatory standardization, certification, and accreditation
  - Absence of rigorous, mandatory certification programs for forensic scientists
  - Insufficient research to validate many forensic science methods
  - Inadequate education and training for forensic scientists
  - Lack of peer-reviewed, published studies establishing the scientific bases and reliability of many forensic methods
  - Insufficient resources for public forensic science laboratories
  - Potential bias and conflicts of interest when forensic laboratories are administratively part of law enforcement agencies

- **Policy Recommendations**:
  - Creation of a new, independent federal entity—the National Institute of Forensic Science (NIFS)—to oversee and regulate the field
  - Establishment of standard terminology and reporting practices
  - Implementation of mandatory certification for forensic science professionals
  - Mandatory accreditation for forensic science laboratories
  - Removal of public forensic laboratories from administrative control of law enforcement agencies
  - Increased research to establish the scientific bases and validity of forensic methods
  - Improved education and training for forensic scientists
  - Development of tools for advancing measurement, validation, reliability, and proficiency testing in forensic science
  - Improved practices and standards for analyzing and reporting on evidence
  - Improved medical examiner and coroner systems

- **Additional Resources**:
  - The report was the result of a comprehensive study conducted over two years
  - The committee heard testimony from numerous experts in the field
  - The report includes detailed analyses of various forensic science disciplines
  - The report has been cited extensively in subsequent research and policy discussions

### Additional Notes
This report represents a landmark assessment of forensic science in the United States. Unlike the other books researched, which focus more on specific cases or aspects of wrongful convictions, this report takes a comprehensive, systemic approach to examining the entire field of forensic science and its role in the justice system.

The report was commissioned by Congress in 2005 and published in 2009. It has had a significant impact on discussions about forensic science reform and has been cited by organizations like The Innocence Project as a foundational document for understanding the relationship between forensic science and wrongful convictions.

While the report does not focus on specific wrongful conviction cases, it acknowledges that flawed forensic science has contributed to wrongful convictions and argues that improving forensic science practices is essential for reducing such miscarriages of justice. The report's recommendations aim to create a more reliable, scientifically sound forensic science system that can better serve the cause of justice.
