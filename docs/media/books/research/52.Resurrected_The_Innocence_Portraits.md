# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Resurrected: The Innocence Portraits

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Resurrected: The Innocence Portraits
- **Author(s)**: Dan Bolick (Artist)
- **Publication Year**: 2009 (Exhibition), 2011 (Exhibition at National Underground Railroad Freedom Center)
- **Publisher**: Not a traditional book but an art exhibition; some paintings appear in "Illustrated Truth: Expressions of Wrongful Conviction" (2011)
- **Format**: Art exhibition featuring large-scale portraits
- **Focus/Theme**: Visual representation of wrongfully convicted individuals, humanizing exonerees through portraiture
- **Key Statistics**: Featured 10 wrongfully convicted men who were freed after years on death row or facing life sentences

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
- **Drew Whitley**: Exonerated through DNA evidence after 16-18 years in prison for a murder he did not commit in Pennsylvania
- **Michael Graham**: Cleared after 14 years on death row
- **Ryan Matthews**: Spent 5 years on death row for a murder he did not commit before DNA testing proved him innocent
- **Dan Bright**: Spent 10 years on death row for a murder he did not commit; works with at-risk youth in New Orleans
- **Greg Bright**: Sentenced to life imprisonment for a murder he did not commit; exonerated after more than 27 years; taught himself to read while in prison
- **Curtis Kyles**: Sentenced to death for a murder he did not commit; spent 18 years in prison, including 14 on death row; subject of the book "Desire Street"; put on trial five different times for the same murder
- **Albert Burrell**: Exoneree featured in the portrait series
- **Calvin Willis**: Exoneree featured in the portrait series
- **Clyde Charles**: Exoneree featured in the portrait series; passed away in January 2009

### Research Notes
"Resurrected: The Innocence Portraits" is not a traditional book but an art exhibition by Dan Bolick, a retired art teacher from Pittsburgh. The exhibition features large-scale portraits of 10 wrongfully convicted men who were exonerated after spending years in prison, many of them on death row.

Bolick was inspired to create these portraits after his experience teaching in inner-city schools, where he saw students caught up in the criminal justice system. He wanted to raise awareness about wrongful convictions by humanizing the exonerees through his art.

The exhibition was first shown at the Westmoreland Museum of American Art in Greensburg, Pennsylvania in 2009, and later at the National Underground Railroad Freedom Center in Cincinnati, Ohio in 2011. Some of Bolick's paintings were also included in the book "Illustrated Truth: Expressions of Wrongful Conviction."

What makes this project significant is its approach to raising awareness about wrongful convictions through visual art. As Bolick noted, when the issue is presented through art and the exonerees are humanized, people become more engaged and willing to discuss the problems in the criminal justice system. The portraits include the words of each subject, adding their personal narratives to the visual representation.

While not a book in the traditional sense, this art series serves as an important visual resource on wrongful convictions, putting faces to the statistics and humanizing the individuals who have suffered through this injustice.
