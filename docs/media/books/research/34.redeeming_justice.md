# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 34: Redeeming Justice: From Defendant to Defender, My Fight for Equity on Both Sides of a Broken System

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Redeeming Justice: From Defendant to Defender, My Fight for Equity on Both Sides of a Broken System
- **Author(s)**: Jarrett Adams
- **Publication Year**: September 14, 2021
- **Publisher**: Convergent Books
- **Format**: Hardcover, 304 pages
- **Focus/Theme**: Wrongful conviction, personal memoir, criminal justice reform, racial bias in the justice system
- **Key Statistics**: The author was one of thousands of wrongfully convicted individuals in the United States; served nearly 10 years of a 28-year sentence

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

#### Jarrett Adams
- **Name**: Jarrett M. Adams
- **Case Location**: Whitewater, Wisconsin (Jefferson County)
- **Conviction Year**: 2000
- **Exoneration Year**: 2007
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Sexual Assault
- **Sentence**: 28 years
- **Time Served**: Nearly 10 years (released January 28, 2007)
- **Contributing Factors**: 
  - Perjury/False Accusation: The alleged victim's testimony was contradicted by other witnesses
  - Inadequate Legal Defense: Defense attorney failed to call a key witness who could have established consent
  - Racial Bias: Adams and his co-defendants (Black) were tried by an all-white jury for an alleged crime against a white woman
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated (charges dismissed February 9, 2007)
- **Organizations Involved**: Wisconsin Innocence Project
- **Cross-Reference**: Listed in the National Registry of Exonerations

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - The book recounts "the many ways the justice system not only failed him but acted as an adversary to Adams"
  - "It's a book that will make everyone think about how America's systems govern our citizens and how injustice all too often masquerades as justice"

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
  - Racial bias in the criminal justice system
  - Inadequate legal representation for defendants
  - Procedural barriers to proving innocence after conviction
  - All-white juries in cases involving Black defendants
  - Prosecutorial misconduct and false testimony
  - Difficulty of life after exoneration

- **Policy Recommendations**:
  - Reform of legal defense systems to ensure adequate representation
  - Addressing racial bias in jury selection and throughout the justice system
  - Creating better support systems for exonerees after release
  - Establishing more robust review processes for potential wrongful convictions

- **Additional Resources**:
  - Life After Justice (non-profit co-founded by Adams)
  - The Innocence Project
  - National Registry of Exonerations
  - Wisconsin Innocence Project

### Additional Notes
After his exoneration, Jarrett Adams earned a law degree from Loyola University Chicago School of Law in 2015. He went on to work with Judge Ann Claire Williams of the Seventh Circuit U.S. Court of Appeals (the same court that reversed his conviction). He later joined the Innocence Project as their first Post-Conviction Litigation Fellow in 2016. Adams co-founded a non-profit called Life After Justice to support exonerees and prevent wrongful convictions. By 2022, he had established law offices in New York, Chicago, and Milwaukee, focusing on helping others who have been wrongfully convicted.

The book not only tells the story of Adams' wrongful conviction but also chronicles his remarkable journey from prisoner to attorney, highlighting the systemic issues in the American criminal justice system that lead to wrongful convictions, particularly for people of color.
