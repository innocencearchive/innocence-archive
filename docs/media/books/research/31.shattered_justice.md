### Research Notes
"Shattered Justice: Crime Victims' Experiences with Wrongful Convictions and Exonerations" by Kimberly J. Cook offers a unique perspective in the literature on wrongful convictions by focusing on the experiences of the original crime victims and their family members. While most books on wrongful convictions center on the wrongfully convicted individuals themselves, Cook shifts the focus to those who were victimized twice—first by the crime itself and then by the justice system's failure to convict the actual perpetrator.

The book is based on in-depth interviews with 21 crime victims across the United States, including homicide victims' family members and rape survivors. Through these interviews, Cook reveals the complex and painful impact of what she describes as three layers of trauma:

1. Primary trauma: The original violent crime
2. Secondary trauma: The investigations and trials
3. Tertiary trauma: The wrongful conviction and subsequent exoneration

This framework helps to illuminate the unique challenges faced by crime victims when they learn that the person convicted of the crime against them or their loved one is actually innocent. Such revelations can reopen wounds, create new uncertainties, and force victims to relive their trauma in unexpected ways.

The book also explores important themes related to grief, loss, healing, and repair. Cook discusses how restorative justice practices can be developed and delivered through healing retreats for survivors, expanding the application of restorative justice principles to address the needs of all parties affected by wrongful convictions.

Finally, the book addresses policy reforms aimed at preventing, mitigating, and repairing the harms of wrongful convictions. By examining wrongful convictions through the lens of the original crime victims, Cook offers a meaningful perspective that complements existing literature on wrongful convictions and provides a more complete understanding of the far-reaching impacts of errors in the criminal justice system.
