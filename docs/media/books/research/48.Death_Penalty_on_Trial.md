# Book Research: The Death Penalty on Trial: Crisis in American Justice

## Basic Information
- **Title**: The Death Penalty on Trial: Crisis in American Justice
- **Author**: Bill Kurtis
- **Publication Year**: 2004
- **Publisher**: PublicAffairs
- **ISBN**: 9781586481698
- **Length**: 192 pages

## Author Background
Bill Kurtis is a television journalist, producer, and former attorney. He passed the Kansas Bar in 1966 but instead pursued a 30-year career as a correspondent and anchorman with CBS Television. He later founded Kurtis Productions, which produces award-winning programs including A&E's "Investigative Reports" and "Cold Case Files." Kurtis also anchors "American Justice" on A&E. His background as both a legal professional and investigative journalist gives him a unique perspective on the criminal justice system and capital punishment.

## Book Summary
"The Death Penalty on Trial" chronicles Bill Kurtis's journey from being a supporter of capital punishment to becoming an opponent. After observing the justice system for thirty years and examining cases where innocent people were sent to death row, Kurtis came to the realization that the criminal justice system is too error-prone to justify the death penalty. The book frames its argument around two detailed case studies of wrongful convictions that led to death sentences, reconstructing the crimes, the investigations, the trials, and the eventual exonerations. Kurtis identifies eight main reasons why innocent people are condemned to death, including overzealous prosecutors, dishonest prosecutors, corrupt policemen, unreliable witnesses, expert witnesses, incompetent defense attorneys, biased judges, and jailhouse informants.

## Key Themes and Arguments
1. **Systemic Flaws in the Justice System**: Kurtis argues that the criminal justice system has too many "working parts" and is therefore prone to error. He compares it to other fields where mistakes are expected (medicine, sports) but notes that we assume the criminal justice system is infallible.

2. **The Impact of DNA Evidence**: The book discusses how DNA evidence has revealed the fallibility of the justice system by exonerating innocent people who were sentenced to death.

3. **The Role of Emotion in Death Penalty Cases**: Kurtis acknowledges that heinous crimes can create a visceral sense that the death penalty is justified, but argues that this emotional response should not override concerns about the system's accuracy.

4. **The Concept of "Closure"**: The book challenges the notion that executions provide closure for victims' families, arguing that this is a flawed justification for capital punishment.

5. **Life Without Parole as an Alternative**: Kurtis advocates for life without parole as an alternative to the death penalty, addressing concerns about punishment and public safety.

## Case Studies of Wrongful Convictions
The book examines in detail two cases where innocent men were wrongfully convicted and sentenced to death before being exonerated:

1. **Case Study 1**: A brutal rape/murder case where the wrong man was convicted based on flawed evidence and later exonerated.

2. **Case Study 2**: A case involving the stabbing of a mother, her two children, and another child, where an innocent person was initially convicted.

These case studies illustrate the various systemic failures that can lead to wrongful convictions, including eyewitness misidentification, prosecutorial misconduct, inadequate defense, and unreliable forensic evidence.

## Impact and Reception
The book received positive reviews for its thorough examination of the flaws in the capital punishment system. Publishers Weekly noted that Kurtis's "refusal to shrink from [the] reality [of heinous crimes] makes his indictment all the more compelling." The book has been cited in discussions about criminal justice reform and has contributed to the ongoing debate about the death penalty in America.

## Relevance to Wrongful Convictions
This book is highly relevant to the study of wrongful convictions as it:

1. Provides detailed case studies of innocent people who were sentenced to death
2. Identifies specific systemic failures that contribute to wrongful convictions
3. Examines how DNA evidence has exposed flaws in the criminal justice system
4. Discusses the role of various actors (police, prosecutors, defense attorneys, witnesses) in contributing to wrongful convictions
5. Considers the moral and ethical implications of maintaining a system that risks executing innocent people

## Notable Quotes
- "Look, I was for the death penalty, but looking at these cases and the rapidly increasing number of exonerations, there are just too many possibilities for error."
- "You have a system with too many working parts. We have malpractice in medicine. We don't expect the Yankees to win all their games. And yet we assume the criminal justice system is without error."
- "The possibility for error in our justice system is simply too great to allow the death penalty to stand as our ultimate punishment."
