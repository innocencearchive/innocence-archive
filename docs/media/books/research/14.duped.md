# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 14: Duped: Why Innocent People Confess — and Why We Believe Their Confessions

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Duped: Why Innocent People Confess — and Why We Believe Their Confessions
- **Author(s)**: Saul M. Kassin, Ph.D.
- **Publication Year**: 2022 (June 15, 2022)
- **Publisher**: Prometheus Books
- **Format**: Hardcover, ebook, 368 pages
- **Focus/Theme**: Analysis of false confessions, why innocent people confess to crimes they didn't commit, and why others believe these false confessions
- **Key Statistics**: Research shows that false confessions are a leading cause of wrongful convictions

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
This book differs from others in our research as it focuses on the psychological mechanisms behind false confessions rather than specific individual cases, though it likely references several cases as examples. The book examines:

- How police interrogation techniques can lead innocent people to confess
- How psychological pressure and manipulation are used in interrogation rooms
- How false confessions occur across various settings including criminal justice, schools, and the military
- How juries and judges often believe false confessions despite contradictory evidence

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "We do not interrogate innocent people" - Joe Buckley, author of "Criminal Interrogation and Confessions," highlighting the problematic assumption that begins many interrogations
  - "This is easily a 10 star book, it is a horror story, a frightening book, to use a cliché, 'the stuff of nightmares'. It presents information that is hidden from us on how the police behave and how the prosecution is in cahoots with them is enlightening." - Goodreads review
  - "97% of all people charged in the US take a plea deal. At least 23% of those people take a no-contest or Alford plea." - Highlighting the prevalence of plea bargaining

- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - Police interrogation techniques designed to extract confessions rather than find truth
  - Legal permission for police to lie to suspects during interrogations
  - Psychological manipulation and pressure during interrogations
  - Lack of proper recording of interrogations in many jurisdictions
  - Distinction between "interviews" (not in custody) and "interrogations" (in custody) used to circumvent Miranda rights
  - Overreliance on lie detector tests despite their low accuracy (only 57% accurate)
  - Judges and juries unable to disregard confession evidence even when instructed to do so
  - Plea bargaining system that pressures innocent people to plead guilty

- **Policy Recommendations**: 
  - Mandatory recording of all police interactions with suspects
  - Reforms to interrogation techniques to reduce psychological pressure
  - Limitations on police ability to lie to suspects
  - Better training for judges and juries on the psychology of false confessions
  - Improved standards for evaluating confession evidence in court
  - Clearer definitions of custody to ensure proper Miranda warnings

- **Additional Resources**: 
  - Scientific research on false confessions pioneered by Saul Kassin
  - Experimental studies on the psychology of interrogation
  - Analysis of known cases of false confessions
  - Research on how juries perceive confession evidence

## Research Strategy Notes
This book is particularly valuable for our project as it:
1. Provides expert analysis from the world's leading researcher on false confessions
2. Examines the psychological mechanisms that lead to false confessions
3. Explains why false confessions are often believed despite contradictory evidence
4. Connects false confessions to wrongful convictions
5. Offers science-based recommendations for reducing false confessions

Unlike other books in our research that focus on specific cases of wrongful conviction, "Duped" examines the psychological and systemic factors that lead to false confessions, which are a significant cause of wrongful convictions. This broader perspective helps us understand how wrongful convictions can occur through psychological manipulation rather than just through evidence tampering or witness misidentification.
