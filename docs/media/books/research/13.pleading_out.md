# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 13: Pleading Out: How Plea Bargaining Creates a Permanent Criminal Class

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Pleading Out: How Plea Bargaining Creates a Permanent Criminal Class
- **Author(s)**: Dan Canon
- **Publication Year**: 2022 (March 8, 2022)
- **Publisher**: Basic Books
- **Format**: Hardcover, paperback, ebook, 336 pages
- **Focus/Theme**: Analysis of how plea bargaining contributes to wrongful convictions and creates a permanent criminal class
- **Key Statistics**: 97% of criminal cases in the United States are resolved through plea bargaining

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
This book differs from others in our research as it focuses on the systemic issue of plea bargaining rather than specific individual cases. While it likely references individual cases as examples, it primarily examines the broader pattern of how plea bargaining contributes to wrongful convictions across the system.

The book addresses:
- How innocent people are pressured to plead guilty to crimes they didn't commit
- How the plea bargaining system disproportionately affects marginalized communities
- How the system creates a permanent criminal class through mass incarceration
- How the legal system's focus on efficiency over justice leads to wrongful convictions

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "What we have inherited is an amoral system of criminal proceedings; it cannot be called criminal justice….. So why not look at the faults— and yes, the motives— of the system itself? Would a system devoted to high-minded principles of fairness and justice produce these results?"
  - "A blistering critique of America's assembly-line approach to criminal justice and the shameful practice at its core: the plea bargain."

- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - The overwhelming majority (97%) of criminal convictions result from plea bargains rather than trials
  - Plea bargaining creates pressure on innocent people to plead guilty to avoid harsher sentences
  - The system prioritizes efficiency and case processing over justice and truth-finding
  - Plea bargaining contributes to mass incarceration and the creation of a permanent criminal class
  - The legal system uses laws as a weapon to control the poor and marginalized
  - Lack of transparency in the plea bargaining process makes it difficult to track and reform
  - The system's unwillingness to admit error perpetuates injustice

- **Policy Recommendations**: 
  - Reform or potentially eliminate the plea bargaining system
  - Increase transparency in the negotiation process
  - Reduce the "trial penalty" that pressures defendants to plead guilty
  - Implement stronger protections for vulnerable defendants
  - Promote class and worker solidarity to challenge the system
  - Support grassroots advocacy for criminal justice reform
  - Create better mechanisms for reviewing potential wrongful convictions

- **Additional Resources**: 
  - Legal, historical, and sociopolitical analysis of the development of plea bargaining
  - Statistical data on the prevalence and impact of plea bargaining
  - Experiences of legal professionals from around the country
  - References to studies and cases illustrating the problems with plea bargaining

## Research Strategy Notes
This book is particularly valuable for our project as it:
1. Provides a systemic analysis of how plea bargaining contributes to wrongful convictions
2. Examines the structural issues in the criminal justice system that lead to innocent people pleading guilty
3. Offers a perspective from a civil rights lawyer who has worked on significant cases
4. Presents policy recommendations for addressing the issues identified
5. Connects wrongful convictions to broader issues of mass incarceration and class inequality

Unlike other books in our research that focus on specific cases of wrongful conviction, "Pleading Out" examines the systemic issue of plea bargaining that affects millions of cases. This broader perspective helps us understand how wrongful convictions can occur on a mass scale through institutional practices rather than just individual errors or misconduct.
