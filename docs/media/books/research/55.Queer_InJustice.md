## Queer (In)Justice: The Criminalization of LGBT People in the United States

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Queer (In)Justice: The Criminalization of LGBT People in the United States
- **Author(s)**: Joey L. Mogul, Andrea J. Ritchie, and Kay Whitlock
- **Publication Year**: 2012 (January 24, 2012)
- **Publisher**: Beacon Press
- **Format**: Paperback, hardcover, eTextbook
- **Focus/Theme**: Examination of how the criminal justice system criminalizes, polices, and punishes LGBTQ+ people; analysis of queer criminal archetypes and their impact
- **Key Statistics**: 240 pages; Book 5 of 10 in the Queer Ideas/Queer Action series

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
While the book does not focus exclusively on wrongful convictions, it examines how bias against LGBTQ+ people contributes to unjust treatment throughout the criminal justice system. The book discusses various cases that illustrate the criminalization of LGBTQ+ identities, including:

- Cases involving the "gleeful gay killer" archetype, where homosexuality is portrayed as inherently criminal or deviant
- Cases involving the "lethal lesbian" archetype, where lesbian identity is portrayed as violent and dangerous
- Cases involving "disease spreaders" archetype, targeting LGBTQ+ people, particularly during the AIDS crisis
- Cases involving "deceptive gender benders" archetype, criminalizing transgender and gender non-conforming people

The book traces these archetypes through historical and contemporary cases, showing how they have been used to justify the punishment of queer expression regardless of whether a crime was actually committed.

### Research Notes
"Queer (In)Justice" provides a critical analysis of how the criminal justice system in the United States has historically and continues to criminalize LGBTQ+ people. Rather than focusing solely on individual cases of wrongful conviction, the book takes a broader approach, examining how the entire system functions to police and punish queer identities and expressions.

The authors draw on their extensive experience as attorneys, activists, and organizers working on issues of LGBTQ+ rights, police misconduct, and criminal justice reform. Joey L. Mogul is a partner at the People's Law Office in Chicago and director of the Civil Rights Clinic at DePaul University's College of Law. Andrea J. Ritchie is a police misconduct attorney and organizer in New York City. Kay Whitlock is a Montana-based organizer and writer whose work focuses on dismantling structural injustice in law enforcement and other public institutions.

The book challenges mainstream LGBTQ+ rights movements that focus primarily on marriage equality and military service, arguing for a more radical approach that addresses the criminalization of LGBTQ+ people, particularly those who are also marginalized due to race, class, and immigration status. It calls for LGBTQ+ activists to develop a more comprehensive analysis that understands ending state violence as central to a transformative queer politics.

By examining the policing of gender and sexuality through an intersectional lens, "Queer (In)Justice" reveals how criminal legal responses to LGBTQ+ people are deeply intertwined with systems of racial and economic oppression. The book has been praised for its accessible prose, thorough research, and powerful critique of the criminal justice system from a queer perspective.
