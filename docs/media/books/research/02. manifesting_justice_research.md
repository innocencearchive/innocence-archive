# Book Research: Manifesting Justice

## Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Manifesting Justice: Wrongly Convicted Women Reclaim Their Rights
- **Author**: Valena Beety
- **Publication Year**: 2022
- **Publisher**: Kensington
- **Format**: Hardcover, paperback, e-book
- **Focus/Theme**: Wrongful convictions of women and LGBTQ+ individuals, gender and sexuality bias in the justice system
- **Key Statistics**: Highlights the unique challenges faced by women and LGBTQ+ people in the criminal justice system

## About the Author
- **Valena Beety**: Attorney and founding director of the West Virginia Innocence Project
- **Background**: Law professor and advocate for criminal justice reform
- **Organization**: Has worked with multiple innocence organizations

## Book Summary
"Manifesting Justice" examines the unique challenges and biases that women and LGBTQ+ individuals face in the criminal justice system, particularly in wrongful conviction cases. The book centers on the case of Leigh Stubbs and Tami Vance, two queer women who were wrongfully convicted in Mississippi based on junk science and homophobia. Through their story and others, Beety explores how gender, sexuality, and other intersecting identities can influence criminal investigations, prosecutions, and convictions.

The book reveals how biases against women and LGBTQ+ people can lead to wrongful convictions, and how these individuals face additional barriers in seeking exoneration. Beety also discusses the role of flawed forensic science, particularly bite mark analysis, in these cases, and advocates for reforms to address these systemic issues.

## Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

### 1. Leigh Stubbs
- **Case Location**: Mississippi
- **Conviction Year**: 2001
- **Exoneration Year**: Not fully exonerated (as of book publication)
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Assault and sexual battery of their friend
- **Sentence**: 44 years in prison
- **Contributing Factors**: Flawed bite mark evidence, homophobia, junk science
- **Legal Status**: Released after serving 17 years
- **Organizations Involved**: Mississippi Innocence Project
- **Additional Notes**: 
  - Queer woman recovering from substance use disorder
  - Case was heavily influenced by anti-LGBTQ bias
  - Convicted based on discredited bite mark analysis
  - No actual evidence a crime had occurred

### 2. Tami Vance

### 3. Kimberly Williams Haddix
- **Case Location**: Mississippi
- **Conviction Year**: 2001
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Drug possession
- **Contributing Factors**: Guilt by association with Stubbs and Vance
- **Additional Notes**: 
  - Friend of Stubbs and Vance who was with them during the alleged incident
  - Was the alleged victim in the assault case against Stubbs and Vance
  - Later testified that no assault occurred

### 4. Tammy Vance (different from Tami Vance above)
- **Case Location**: West Virginia
- **Conviction Year**: Not specified
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of her child
- **Contributing Factors**: Flawed forensic evidence, gender bias
- **Additional Notes**: 
  - Case involved allegations of Shaken Baby Syndrome, a controversial diagnosis
  - Beety represented her through the West Virginia Innocence Project

### 5. Sabrina Butler
- **Case Location**: Mississippi
- **Conviction Year**: 1990
- **Exoneration Year**: 1995
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of her nine-month-old son
- **Sentence**: Death sentence
- **Time Served**: 5 years
- **Contributing Factors**: Flawed forensic evidence, gender bias
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated
- **Additional Notes**: 
  - Only woman exonerated from death row in the United States
  - Child actually died from a kidney condition
  - Her attempts to resuscitate her child were misinterpreted as abuse

## Systemic Issues Identified
1. Gender bias in the criminal justice system
2. Anti-LGBTQ+ discrimination and prejudice
3. Flawed forensic science, particularly bite mark analysis
4. Prosecutorial misconduct
5. Inadequate defense representation
6. Societal expectations about "appropriate" female behavior
7. Intersection of race, gender, and sexuality in wrongful convictions
8. Barriers to post-conviction relief for women and LGBTQ+ individuals

## Policy Recommendations
1. Reform of forensic science standards and practices
2. Training for legal professionals on gender and LGBTQ+ bias
3. Increased funding for innocence organizations focused on women and LGBTQ+ cases
4. Improved post-conviction remedies for wrongfully convicted individuals
5. Recognition of the unique challenges faced by women and LGBTQ+ defendants
6. Addressing the role of homophobia and gender bias in criminal investigations
7. Reform of laws that disproportionately impact women and LGBTQ+ individuals

## Research Notes
- The book takes an intersectional approach to wrongful convictions, examining how multiple forms of discrimination can compound
- Bite mark evidence, which has been discredited in many cases including those featured in "Junk Science and the American Criminal Justice System," plays a significant role in the Stubbs and Vance case
- The book highlights how women's cases often receive less attention from innocence organizations and the media
- Many of the cases involve women being convicted of crimes that never actually occurred
