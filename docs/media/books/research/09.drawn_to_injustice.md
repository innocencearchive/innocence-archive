# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 9: Drawn to Injustice: The Wrongful Conviction of Timothy Masters

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Drawn to Injustice: The Wrongful Conviction of Timothy Masters
- **Author(s)**: Timothy Masters and Steve Lehto
- **Publication Year**: 2012 (June 5, 2012)
- **Publisher**: Berkley Books (Penguin Publishing Group)
- **Format**: Mass Market Paperback, 448 pages
- **Focus/Theme**: Case study of Timothy Masters, wrongfully convicted based on his violent artwork and writings as a teenager
- **Key Statistics**: Timothy Masters spent nearly 10 years in prison for a crime he did not commit

### Wrongfully Convicted Individual Featured
- **Name**: Timothy Masters
- **Case Location**: Fort Collins, Colorado
- **Conviction Year**: 1999
- **Exoneration Year**: 2008 (officially exonerated by Colorado Attorney General in 2011)
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of Peggy Hettrick in 1987
- **Sentence**: Life imprisonment without parole
- **Time Served**: Nearly 10 years
- **Contributing Factors**: 
  - Flawed forensic psychology (Dr. J. Reid Meloy analyzed Masters' teenage drawings and concluded they represented him reliving the crime)
  - Prosecutorial misconduct (withholding evidence)
  - Police tunnel vision (Detective Jim Broderick focused on Masters early in the investigation)
  - Misinterpretation of evidence (Masters' violent artwork and writings as a teenager)
  - Failure to consider alternative suspects (including Dr. Richard Hammond)
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated, received settlements from Fort Collins ($5.9 million) and Larimer County
- **Organizations Involved**: Special prosecutors assigned to the appeal, defense attorneys who arranged for touch DNA testing
- **Cross-Reference**: Not specified in research

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "It's hard for me to review this one without judging what happened to Timothy Masters. He was accused of a murder he clearly didn't commit and was convicted because a detective didn't want to admit he'd made a mistake?!?!?!" (Goodreads review)
  - "This is a well-written book about a horrible tragedy and how it ruined one man's life." (Goodreads review)

- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - Reliance on questionable forensic psychology
  - Withholding of exculpatory evidence
  - Police tunnel vision and confirmation bias
  - Inadequate legal representation
  - Failure to properly investigate alternative suspects
  - Misuse of circumstantial evidence (artwork and writings)
  - Lack of physical evidence connecting Masters to the crime

- **Policy Recommendations**: 
  - Need for more rigorous standards for forensic psychology testimony
  - Stronger enforcement of Brady disclosure requirements
  - Better oversight of police investigations
  - More thorough consideration of alternative suspects

- **Additional Resources**: 
  - DNA testing that eventually exonerated Masters
  - Colorado Attorney General's official exoneration in 2011
  - Civil lawsuits against Fort Collins and Larimer County
  - Documentary about the case (mentioned in research)

## Research Strategy Notes
This book is particularly valuable for our project as it:
1. Provides a first-person account from the wrongfully convicted individual himself
2. Highlights how non-scientific evidence (artwork and writings) can be misinterpreted and lead to wrongful convictions
3. Demonstrates the impact of tunnel vision and confirmation bias in investigations
4. Shows how withholding evidence can lead to wrongful convictions
5. Illustrates the power of DNA evidence in exonerating the innocent

The book offers a unique perspective as it is co-authored by the exoneree himself, providing direct insight into the experience of being wrongfully convicted and the psychological impact of such an experience.
