  - Suicides misclassified as homicides
  - Accidental fires misclassified as arson
  - Natural deaths misclassified as murders
  - Drugs planted on innocent suspects
  - False allegations of assault invented to resolve custody disputes

### Additional Notes
Jessica S. Henry was a public defender for nearly ten years in New York City before joining the Department of Justice Studies at Montclair State University, where she is a Professor and frequent commentator on national television, radio, and in print media. Her book won the 2020 Montaigne Medal award for most thought-provoking book and the INDIE forward Book of the Year Award (Silver, Political and Social Science).

The book is structured to examine the various factors that contribute to no-crime wrongful convictions, from forensic errors to systemic issues within the criminal justice system. It highlights how innocent people can be convicted of crimes that never actually occurred through a combination of official misconduct, aggressive policing, forensic errors, false accusations, inadequate defense, and judicial bias.

According to Henry's research and the National Registry of Exonerations, approximately one-third of all exonerations involve no-crime wrongful convictions, making this a significant but previously underdocumented type of miscarriage of justice.
