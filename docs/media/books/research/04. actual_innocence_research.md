# Book Research: Actual Innocence

## Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Actual Innocence: Five Days to Execution, and Other Dispatches from the Wrongly Convicted
- **Author(s)**: Jim Dwyer, Peter Neufeld, Barry Scheck
- **Publication Year**: 2000 (Original), 2003 (Updated edition: "When Justice Goes Wrong and How to Make it Right")
- **Publisher**: Doubleday (Original), NAL Trade (Updated edition)
- **Format**: Hardcover, paperback, e-book
- **Focus/Theme**: DNA exonerations, systemic causes of wrongful convictions, Innocence Project cases
- **Key Statistics**: Documents some of the first 67 DNA exonerations in the United States

## About the Authors
- **Jim Dwyer**: Two-time Pulitzer Prize-winning columnist for the New York Daily News
- **Peter Neufeld**: Co-founder of the Innocence Project
- **Barry Scheck**: Co-founder of the Innocence Project, professor at Cardozo School of Law

## Book Summary
"Actual Innocence" examines the cases of innocent people who were wrongfully convicted and later exonerated through DNA evidence. The book explores the systemic flaws in the American criminal justice system that lead to wrongful convictions, including eyewitness misidentification, false confessions, forensic science misconduct, prosecutorial misconduct, inadequate defense counsel, and the use of jailhouse informants.

Each chapter focuses on a specific cause of wrongful conviction, illustrated through the stories of exonerees. The book not only documents these miscarriages of justice but also proposes reforms to prevent future wrongful convictions.

## Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

### 1. Marion Coakley
- **Case Location**: New York
- **Conviction Year**: 1985
- **Exoneration Year**: 1987
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Rape
- **Contributing Factors**: Eyewitness misidentification, forensic errors
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated
- **Notes**: Described as "mentally retarded and a very angry individual" in the book

### 2. Kirk Bloodsworth
- **Case Location**: Maryland
- **Conviction Year**: 1985
- **Exoneration Year**: 1993
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Rape and murder of a 9-year-old girl
- **Sentence**: Death sentence, later commuted to life imprisonment
- **Time Served**: 8 years, including 2 years on death row
- **Contributing Factors**: Eyewitness misidentification
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated through DNA evidence
- **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project
- **Cross-Reference**: Listed in National Registry of Exonerations
- **Additional Notes**: First death row inmate in the United States to be exonerated by DNA evidence

### 3. Walter McMillian
- **Case Location**: Monroeville, Alabama
- **Conviction Year**: 1988
- **Exoneration Year**: 1993
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of a white woman
- **Sentence**: Death sentence
- **Time Served**: 6 years on death row
- **Contributing Factors**: Perjured testimony, prosecutorial misconduct, racial bias
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated
- **Organizations Involved**: Equal Justice Initiative (Bryan Stevenson)
- **Cross-Reference**: Listed in National Registry of Exonerations
- **Additional Notes**: Also featured prominently in Bryan Stevenson's book "Just Mercy"

### 4. Dennis Fritz and Ron Williamson
- **Case Location**: Ada, Oklahoma
- **Conviction Year**: 1988
- **Exoneration Year**: 1999
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Rape and murder of Debbie Carter
- **Sentence**: Fritz: Life imprisonment; Williamson: Death sentence
- **Time Served**: 11 years
- **Contributing Factors**: Jailhouse informant testimony, prosecutorial misconduct, forensic errors
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated through DNA evidence
- **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project
- **Cross-Reference**: Listed in National Registry of Exonerations
- **Additional Notes**: Their case was later featured in John Grisham's non-fiction book "The Innocent Man"

### 5. Calvin Johnson
- **Case Location**: Georgia
- **Conviction Year**: 1983
- **Exoneration Year**: 1999
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Rape
- **Sentence**: Life imprisonment
- **Time Served**: 16 years
- **Contributing Factors**: Eyewitness misidentification
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated through DNA evidence
- **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project
- **Cross-Reference**: Listed in National Registry of Exonerations

## Systemic Issues Identified
1. Eyewitness misidentification
2. False confessions
3. Forensic science misconduct
4. Prosecutorial misconduct
5. Inadequate defense counsel
6. Use of jailhouse informants
7. Racial bias in the criminal justice system
8. Resistance to post-conviction DNA testing

## Policy Recommendations
1. Improved eyewitness identification procedures
2. Mandatory recording of interrogations
3. Independent forensic laboratories
4. Increased funding for public defenders
5. Open file discovery policies for prosecutors
6. Preservation of biological evidence
7. Access to post-conviction DNA testing
8. Compensation for the wrongfully convicted

## Research Notes
- This book is considered a foundational text in the wrongful conviction literature
- Written by Innocence Project co-founders, providing authoritative perspective
- Documents early cases of DNA exonerations, showing the beginning of this movement
- Provides detailed analysis of systemic issues that remain relevant today
- Many of the cases featured have been further documented in subsequent books and documentaries
