# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 19: Convicting the Innocent

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Convicting the Innocent: Where Criminal Prosecutions Go Wrong
- **Author(s)**: Brandon L. Garrett
- **Publication Year**: 2011 (Hardcover), 2012 (Paperback)
- **Publisher**: Harvard University Press
- **Format**: Academic non-fiction
- **Focus/Theme**: Systematic analysis of DNA exonerations, patterns in wrongful convictions
- **Key Statistics**: Examines the first 250 DNA exonerations in the United States

### Wrongfully Convicted Individual Featured
- **Name**: Multiple individuals (250 DNA exonerees)
- **Case Location**: Various locations across the United States
- **Conviction Years**: Various
- **Exoneration Years**: Various (all prior to 2011)
- **Crimes Convicted Of**: Primarily serious violent crimes (murder, rape)
- **Sentences**: Various, including death sentences
- **Time Served**: Various
- **Contributing Factors**: 
  - False confessions
  - Eyewitness misidentifications
  - Flawed forensic evidence
  - Jailhouse informant testimony
  - Inadequate legal representation
  - Prosecutorial misconduct
- **Legal Status**: All exonerated through DNA evidence
- **Organizations Involved**: The Innocence Project (founded by Barry Scheck and Peter Neufeld)
- **Cross-Reference**: Book 4: "Actual Innocence" by Jim Dwyer, Peter Neufeld & Barry Scheck

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "In this unsettling in-depth analysis, Brandon Garrett examines what went wrong in the cases of the first 250 wrongfully convicted people to be exonerated by DNA testing."
  - "DNA exonerations have shattered confidence in the criminal justice system by exposing how often we have convicted the innocent and let the guilty walk free."

- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - Flawed eyewitness identification procedures
  - Coercive interrogation techniques leading to false confessions
  - Unreliable forensic science practices
  - Inadequate defense representation
  - Prosecutorial misconduct and Brady violations
  - Difficulties in post-conviction remedies
  - Systemic racial and socioeconomic biases

- **Policy Recommendations**: 
  - Reforms to eyewitness identification procedures
  - Mandatory recording of interrogations
  - Improved forensic science standards and oversight
  - Better funding for public defenders
  - Enhanced disclosure requirements for prosecutors
  - Expanded access to post-conviction DNA testing
  - Creation of conviction integrity units

- **Additional Resources**: 
  - Convicting the Innocent website (convictingtheinnocent.com)
  - DNA Exonerations Database maintained by the author
  - Follow-up academic papers by Brandon Garrett, including "Convicting the Innocent Redux"

## Research Strategy Notes
This book is particularly valuable for our project as it:
1. Provides a comprehensive academic analysis of a large sample of DNA exonerations
2. Identifies patterns and systemic issues across multiple cases rather than focusing on individual stories
3. Is written by a leading legal scholar in the field of wrongful convictions (Brandon L. Garrett, L. Neil Williams, Jr. Professor of Law at Duke University School of Law)
4. Uses empirical data and trial records to document exactly what went wrong in each case
5. Offers evidence-based policy recommendations for preventing wrongful convictions
6. Has been influential in shaping legal scholarship and policy discussions around wrongful convictions

The book stands out from others in our research as it takes a more academic, data-driven approach to analyzing wrongful convictions rather than focusing primarily on personal narratives. It examines the systemic patterns across hundreds of cases, providing a broader perspective on the factors that contribute to wrongful convictions in the American criminal justice system.
