# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## A False Report: A True Story of Rape in America

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: A False Report: A True Story of Rape in America
- **Author(s)**: T. Christian Miller and Ken Armstrong
- **Publication Year**: 2018 (February 6, 2018)
- **Publisher**: Crown
- **Format**: Hardcover, 304 pages
- **Focus/Theme**: Investigation of false rape accusations, victim credibility, police investigation methods
- **Key Statistics**: The book discusses statistics on rape victims and the prevalence of skepticism toward rape accusations

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
While this book doesn't feature traditional wrongful convictions (where someone is convicted of a crime they didn't commit), it does feature a case where a victim was wrongfully accused of filing a false report:

- **Name**: Marie Adler (18-year-old)
- **Case Location**: Lynnwood, Washington
- **Conviction Year**: 2008 (charged with filing a false report after police pressured her to recant her rape allegation)
- **Exoneration Year**: 2011 (when her rapist was caught in Colorado)
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Filing a false police report (misdemeanor)
- **Sentence**: Probation, court fees, and mandatory counseling
- **Time Served**: N/A (received probation)
- **Contributing Factors**: Police skepticism, inconsistencies in her account due to trauma, lack of support system (foster care background)
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated when her rapist was caught and confessed
- **Organizations Involved**: Lynnwood Police Department (initial investigation), Colorado detectives who solved the case
- **Cross-Reference**: This case was later adapted into the Netflix series "Unbelievable"

The actual perpetrator:
- **Name**: Marc O'Leary
- **Details**: Army veteran who committed multiple rapes in Washington and Colorado
- **Outcome**: Arrested in 2011, sentenced to 327.5 years in prison for multiple rapes

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: The book highlights the dismaying skepticism and credibility issues surrounding rape accusers
- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - Police skepticism toward rape victims
  - Inadequate training in trauma-informed interviewing techniques
  - Lack of coordination between police departments in different jurisdictions
  - Tendency to disbelieve victims with complicated backgrounds or inconsistent accounts
- **Policy Recommendations**: 
  - Better training for police in handling sexual assault cases
  - Improved coordination between law enforcement agencies
  - More trauma-informed approaches to interviewing victims
- **Additional Resources**: 
  - The original ProPublica/Marshall Project article "An Unbelievable Story of Rape" that won the 2016 Pulitzer Prize
  - Netflix adaptation "Unbelievable" based on the same case

### Research Notes
This book is particularly relevant to wrongful conviction studies because it examines a case where the victim herself was wrongfully accused of a crime (filing a false report) when she was actually telling the truth. While not a traditional wrongful conviction case, it highlights similar systemic issues in the criminal justice system: skepticism toward certain types of witnesses, pressure to close cases quickly, and the devastating impact of being disbelieved by the justice system.

The book follows two parallel narratives: Marie's experience being disbelieved and charged with filing a false report, and the later investigation by two female detectives in Colorado who connected multiple rape cases and eventually identified Marc O'Leary as the serial rapist responsible for Marie's rape and others.

The book was expanded from a ProPublica/Marshall Project article by the same authors that won the 2016 Pulitzer Prize for Explanatory Reporting.
