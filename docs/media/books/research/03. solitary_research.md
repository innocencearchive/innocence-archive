# Book Research: Solitary

## Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Solitary: A Biography
- **Author**: Albert Woodfox
- **Publication Year**: 2019
- **Publisher**: Grove Press
- **Format**: Hardcover, paperback, e-book, audiobook
- **Focus/Theme**: Wrongful conviction, solitary confinement, prison reform, racial justice
- **Key Statistics**: Documents the author's experience of nearly 44 years in solitary confinement, the longest period in U.S. history
- **Recognition**: National Book Award finalist, Pulitzer Prize finalist

## About the Author
- **Albert Woodfox**: Member of the "Angola Three," wrongfully convicted of murder
- **Background**: Former Black Panther who became a prison activist and advocate
- **Personal History**: Spent 43 years and 10 months in solitary confinement at Louisiana's Angola Prison
- **Death**: Passed away in August 2022 at age 75, six years after his release

## Book Summary
"Solitary" is Albert Woodfox's powerful memoir detailing his experience of being wrongfully convicted of murder and subsequently spending nearly 44 years in solitary confinement at Louisiana's notorious Angola Prison. The book chronicles Woodfox's early life, his political awakening as a member of the Black Panther Party, and his tireless fight for justice while enduring the psychological torture of extreme isolation.

Woodfox, along with Herman Wallace and Robert King (collectively known as the "Angola Three"), was convicted of the 1972 murder of a prison guard based on flimsy evidence and false testimony. The book details how their political activism as Black Panthers made them targets within the prison system, leading to their wrongful convictions and extreme punishment.

Throughout the memoir, Woodfox describes how he maintained his sanity and dignity during decades of solitary confinement, educating himself, organizing fellow prisoners, and never giving up hope for exoneration. His story is not only a personal account of survival but also a damning indictment of the American criminal justice system, particularly its treatment of Black men and its use of solitary confinement as punishment.

## Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

### 1. Albert Woodfox
- **Case Location**: Louisiana
- **Conviction Year**: 1972
- **Exoneration Year**: 2016
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of prison guard Brent Miller
- **Sentence**: Life imprisonment
- **Time Served**: 43 years and 10 months, mostly in solitary confinement
- **Contributing Factors**: Racial bias, false testimony, political targeting as a Black Panther
- **Legal Status**: Released in 2016 after pleading no contest while maintaining innocence
- **Organizations Involved**: Amnesty International, International Coalition to Free the Angola Three
- **Additional Notes**: 
  - Conviction was overturned multiple times but he remained imprisoned
  - Maintained his innocence throughout his incarceration
  - Became an activist for prison reform after his release
  - Died in 2022, six years after gaining freedom

### 2. Herman Wallace
- **Case Location**: Louisiana
- **Conviction Year**: 1972
- **Exoneration Year**: 2013
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of prison guard Brent Miller
- **Sentence**: Life imprisonment
- **Time Served**: 41 years in solitary confinement
- **Contributing Factors**: Racial bias, false testimony, political targeting as a Black Panther
- **Legal Status**: Conviction overturned in 2013
- **Organizations Involved**: Amnesty International, International Coalition to Free the Angola Three
- **Additional Notes**: 
  - Released just days before his death from liver cancer in 2013
  - Fellow member of the "Angola Three"
  - Maintained his innocence throughout his incarceration

### 3. Robert King
- **Case Location**: Louisiana
- **Conviction Year**: 1973
- **Exoneration Year**: 2001
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of another inmate
- **Sentence**: Life imprisonment
- **Time Served**: 29 years in solitary confinement
- **Contributing Factors**: Racial bias, false testimony, political targeting as a Black Panther
- **Legal Status**: Released in 2001 after his conviction was overturned
- **Organizations Involved**: Amnesty International, International Coalition to Free the Angola Three
- **Additional Notes**: 
  - First of the "Angola Three" to be released
  - Became an activist for prison reform and against solitary confinement
  - Has spoken internationally about his experience and prison reform

## Systemic Issues Identified
1. Racial discrimination in the criminal justice system
2. Use of solitary confinement as torture and political repression
3. Targeting of political activists, particularly Black Panthers
4. Prosecutorial misconduct and use of false testimony
5. Inadequate legal representation for poor defendants
6. Prison conditions and prisoner abuse
7. Barriers to post-conviction relief and exoneration
8. Psychological impact of long-term solitary confinement

## Policy Recommendations
1. Abolition or severe restriction of solitary confinement
2. Reform of prison conditions and practices
3. Addressing racial bias in the criminal justice system
4. Improved oversight of prosecutorial conduct
5. Enhanced post-conviction remedies for wrongfully convicted individuals
6. Recognition of political factors in wrongful convictions
7. Mental health support for formerly incarcerated individuals
8. Prison education and rehabilitation programs

## Research Notes
- The book provides a rare first-person account of extended solitary confinement
- Woodfox's case gained international attention and support from human rights organizations
- The "Angola Three" case highlights the intersection of racial justice, political activism, and wrongful conviction
- Angola Prison (Louisiana State Penitentiary) has a notorious history as a former plantation converted to a prison
- The book connects to broader movements for prison reform and abolition
