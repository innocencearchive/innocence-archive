# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 15: Chasing Me to My Grave: An Artist's Memoir of the Jim Crow South

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Chasing Me to My Grave: An Artist's Memoir of the Jim Crow South
- **Author(s)**: Winfred Rembert with Erin I. Kelly
- **Publication Year**: 2021 (September 7, 2021)
- **Publisher**: Bloomsbury Publishing
- **Format**: Hardcover, paperback, ebook, 304 pages with 4-color images throughout
- **Focus/Theme**: Memoir of survival and artistry during the Jim Crow era, including wrongful arrest and incarceration
- **Key Statistics**: Won the Pulitzer Prize in 2022

### Wrongfully Convicted Individual Featured
- **Name**: Winfred Rembert (the author himself)
- **Case Location**: Georgia
- **Conviction Year**: 1960s
- **Exoneration Year**: Not formally exonerated
- **Crime Convicted Of**: No formal charges filed despite incarceration
- **Sentence**: Illegally detained for more than a year without charges
- **Time Served**: More than a year in jail without formal charges
- **Contributing Factors**: 
  - Racial discrimination in the Jim Crow South
  - Civil rights activism
  - Lack of due process
  - Police misconduct
  - Systemic racism in the criminal justice system
- **Legal Status**: Was eventually released but not formally exonerated
- **Organizations Involved**: Not specified in available research
- **Cross-Reference**: Not specified in available research

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "Vivid, confrontational, revelatory, and complex, Chasing Me to My Grave is a searing memoir in prose and painted leather that celebrates Black life and summons readers to confront painful and urgent realities at the heart of American society."
  - "He was arrested and incarcerated in the local jail for more than a year, without charges being filed against him."

- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - Arbitrary arrest and detention without charges
  - Racial discrimination in the criminal justice system
  - Police brutality and misconduct
  - Lack of due process for Black Americans in the Jim Crow South
  - Systemic racism embedded in legal institutions
  - Extrajudicial punishment including attempted lynching
  - Dehumanization of incarcerated individuals

- **Policy Recommendations**: 
  - Need for stronger protections against arbitrary detention
  - Reforms to ensure due process for all defendants
  - Addressing systemic racism in the criminal justice system
  - Accountability for law enforcement misconduct
  - Recognition and redress for historical injustices

- **Additional Resources**: 
  - Rembert's artwork depicting his experiences in the Jim Crow South
  - Rembert's survival of a near-fatal lynching attempt
  - His experiences in Georgia's prison system
  - His development as an artist who documented his experiences through leather tooling

## Research Strategy Notes
This book is particularly valuable for our project as it:
1. Provides a first-person account of wrongful incarceration during the Jim Crow era
2. Illustrates how racial discrimination led to arbitrary detention without formal charges
3. Documents survival through an unjust criminal justice system
4. Shows the intersection of art and memoir as a means of processing trauma from wrongful incarceration
5. Highlights historical patterns of injustice that continue to influence the modern criminal justice system

The book stands out from others in our research as it combines memoir with visual art (Rembert's leather tooling artwork) to tell the story of wrongful incarceration. It also provides historical context for understanding contemporary issues of wrongful conviction by examining the Jim Crow era's legal system. The Pulitzer Prize recognition underscores the significance and quality of this work in documenting this important aspect of American history.
