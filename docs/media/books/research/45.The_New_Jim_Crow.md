# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## The New Jim Crow: Mass Incarceration in the Age of Colorblindness

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: The New Jim Crow: Mass Incarceration in the Age of Colorblindness
- **Author(s)**: Michelle Alexander (with foreword by Cornel West in anniversary edition)
- **Publication Year**: 2010 (original), 2012 (paperback), 2020 (anniversary edition)
- **Publisher**: The New Press
- **Format**: Hardcover, paperback, e-book, audiobook
- **Focus/Theme**: Mass incarceration as a system of racial control, War on Drugs, racial disparities in criminal justice system, systemic racism
- **Key Statistics**: 
  - One in 9 black men between the ages of twenty and thirty-five was behind bars in 2006
  - In 2010, white people were incarcerated at a rate of 450 per 100,000 while black people were incarcerated at a rate of 2,306 per 100,000
  - The U.S. prison population increased by more than 600 percent from the mid-1960s until 2000
  - The United States has a crime rate lower than the international norm, yet an incarceration rate 6-10 times higher than other countries

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
*Note: This book focuses primarily on systemic issues in the criminal justice system rather than detailed accounts of specific wrongfully convicted individuals. It examines mass incarceration as a form of social control rather than presenting individual case studies of wrongful convictions.*

The book mentions Jarvious Cotton in the introduction as an example of disenfranchisement across generations, but does not focus on specific wrongful conviction cases. Instead, it analyzes the broader patterns and policies that have led to mass incarceration disproportionately affecting communities of color.

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "We have not ended racial caste in America; we have merely redesigned it."
  - "The criminal justice system functions as a contemporary system of racial control—relegating millions to a permanent second-class status—even as it formally adheres to the principle of colorblindness."
  - "By targeting black men through the War on Drugs and decimating communities of color, the U.S. criminal justice system functions as a contemporary system of racial control."

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
  - The War on Drugs as a primary driver of mass incarceration
  - Racial bias in policing, prosecution, and sentencing
  - Collateral consequences of conviction (loss of voting rights, employment discrimination, housing discrimination, exclusion from public benefits)
  - Procedural hurdles that make legal challenges difficult after conviction
  - Colorblind rhetoric that masks racial disparities
  - Political incentives for "tough on crime" policies

- **Policy Recommendations**:
  - End the War on Drugs and shift to a public health approach to drug addiction
  - Eliminate mandatory minimum sentences
  - Reform policies that create barriers to reentry (employment, housing, education)
  - Restore voting rights to formerly incarcerated individuals
  - Increase public awareness about racial disparities in the criminal justice system
  - Shift resources from incarceration to education and community development

- **Additional Resources**:

  - "Prison State" (FRONTLINE documentary)
  - "13th" (documentary that builds on themes from The New Jim Crow)

### Research Notes
This book, while not focused specifically on individual wrongful conviction cases, provides essential context for understanding the systemic factors that contribute to wrongful convictions. Alexander argues that the criminal justice system operates more like a caste system than a system of crime control, which creates conditions where wrongful convictions are more likely to occur and less likely to be addressed, particularly for people of color.

The book has been highly influential in criminal justice reform movements and has been cited in judicial decisions. It provides a framework for understanding how racial bias operates within seemingly race-neutral policies and practices in the criminal justice system.
