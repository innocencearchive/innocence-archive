# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 8: Tested: How Twelve Wrongly Imprisoned Men Held Onto Hope

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Tested: How Twelve Wrongly Imprisoned Men Held Onto Hope
- **Author(s)**: Peyton Budd and Dorothy Budd
- **Publication Year**: 2010 (October 12, 2010)
- **Publisher**: Brown Books Publishing Group (Dallas, TX)
- **Format**: Hardcover, Paperback (130 pages)
- **Focus/Theme**: Psychological resilience of wrongfully convicted individuals, personal journeys of hope, faith, and redemption
- **Key Statistics**: 12 innocent men, 189 lost years, 68,985 lost days, 1,655,640 lost hours

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "These innocent men illuminate both the failures of the justice system and the resilience of the human spirit. Their surprising stories teach us that no matter how we are tested in our lives, it is never wrong to have hope."
  - "In Reverend Dorothy Budd's words, the book is 'an inquiry into how twelve human beings, trapped in impossible and unfair situations, somehow made a way where there was no way.'"
  - "12 innocent men, 189 lost years, 68,985 lost days, 1,655,640 lost hours" (statistics from the book's back cover)

- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - Eyewitness misidentification
  - Flaws in the criminal justice system
  - Racial bias (implied in the research)
  - Lack of adequate legal representation
  - Procedural issues in trials

- **Policy Recommendations**: 
  - The book advocates for an "honest conversation about our justice system, the new tools available, and the hurdles the poor face once they are drawn into that system" (Craig Watkins, Dallas County District Attorney)
  - Supports the use of DNA testing and post-conviction review

- **Additional Resources**: 
  - Actual Innocence Clinic at The University of Texas at Austin School of Law
  - University of Texas at Arlington's Innocence Network
  - Dallas County District Attorney's Office (under Craig Watkins)
  - Innocence Project of Texas

## Research Strategy Notes
This book is valuable for our project as it:
1. Focuses on multiple cases (twelve wrongfully convicted men) to maximize data collection
2. Provides insight into the psychological aspects of wrongful conviction and how individuals maintain hope during incarceration
3. Highlights systemic issues in the criminal justice system that contribute to wrongful convictions
4. Features cases from Dallas County, Texas, which has had a significant number of exonerations
5. Includes personal narratives based on one-on-one interviews with the exonerees

The book appears to be unique in its focus on the psychological and emotional aspects of wrongful conviction, making it a valuable complement to more legally-focused resources in our research.
