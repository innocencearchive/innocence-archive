# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Policing the Black Man: Arrest, Prosecution, and Imprisonment in America

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Policing the Black Man: Arrest, Prosecution, and Imprisonment in America
- **Author(s)**: Edited by Angela J. Davis with contributions from Bryan Stevenson, Sherrilyn Ifill, Jeremy Travis, and others
- **Publication Year**: 2017
- **Publisher**: Pantheon Books, a division of Penguin Random House LLC
- **Format**: Hardcover, Paperback, Ebook
- **Focus/Theme**: Racial bias in criminal justice, police brutality, mass incarceration, wrongful convictions of Black men
- **Key Statistics**: The book examines disproportionate rates of arrest, prosecution, and imprisonment of African American men in the United States

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
The book discusses systemic issues leading to wrongful convictions rather than focusing exclusively on individual cases. It examines:

- How racial profiling leads to disproportionate arrests of Black men
- How prosecutorial discretion can contribute to wrongful convictions
- How implicit bias affects every stage of the criminal justice process
- How these systemic issues have resulted in numerous wrongful convictions of Black men

While specific named individuals are not prominently mentioned in the available research materials, the book addresses the structural factors that have led to many wrongful convictions of Black men throughout the American criminal justice system.

### Book Research Notes

- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "Somewhere among the anger, mourning and malice that Policing the Black Man documents lies the pursuit of justice. This powerful book demands our fierce attention." —Toni Morrison
  - "Like Michelle Alexander's The New Jim Crow: Mass Incarceration in the Age of Colorblindness or Ta-Nehisi Coates' Between the World and Me, Policing the Black Man insightfully shows us why the encounter between black men and even black boys with the criminal justice system is, and long has been historically, fraught, reflecting larger social and economic relations between white and black Americans." —Henry Louis Gates, Jr.
  - "Angela J. Davis powerfully shows the American police and justice system are heavily biased against non-white Americans. Policing the Black Man is an indictment of American justice system and police." —The Washington Book Review

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
  1. Racial profiling by police leading to disproportionate arrests
  2. Prosecutorial misconduct and abuse of discretion
  3. Implicit bias throughout the criminal justice system
  4. Presumption of guilt for Black men (as noted in Bryan Stevenson's chapter "A Presumption of Guilt: The Legacy of America's History of Racial Injustice")
  5. Mass incarceration and its impact on Black communities
  6. Historical legacy of racial injustice in America's legal system
  7. Structural racism embedded in policing practices

- **Policy Recommendations**:
  The book suggests reforms to address racial disparities in the criminal justice system, including:
  - Reform of police practices and training to reduce racial bias
  - Greater accountability for prosecutorial misconduct
  - Addressing implicit bias in the criminal justice system
  - Reforms to reduce mass incarceration
  - Recognition of historical injustices and their ongoing impact

- **Additional Resources**:
  - The book includes contributions from multiple experts including:
    - Bryan Stevenson (author of "Just Mercy")
    - Sherrilyn Ifill
    - Jeremy Travis
    - Kristin Henning
    - Renée McDonald Hutchins
    - Roger A. Fairfax, Jr.
  - Related works mentioned include "The New Jim Crow" by Michelle Alexander and "Between the World and Me" by Ta-Nehisi Coates
  - The book is part of a broader conversation about racial justice in America's criminal justice system

### Integration with Wrongful Conviction Database
This book provides valuable context for understanding the systemic factors that contribute to wrongful convictions of Black men in America. While it may not focus on individual cases in detail, it offers:

1. Analysis of how racial bias operates at every stage of the criminal justice process
2. Examination of how these biases lead to wrongful arrests, prosecutions, and convictions
3. Expert perspectives from legal scholars and practitioners
4. Historical context for understanding contemporary racial disparities

The book would be most valuable for understanding the structural and systemic issues that contribute to wrongful convictions of Black men, providing a framework for analyzing patterns across multiple cases in a wrongful conviction database.
