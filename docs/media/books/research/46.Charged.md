# Book Research: Charged: The New Movement to Transform American Prosecution and End Mass Incarceration

## Basic Information
- **Title**: Charged: The New Movement to Transform American Prosecution and End Mass Incarceration
- **Author**: Emily Bazelon
- **Publication Year**: 2019
- **Publisher**: Random House
- **ISBN**: 9780399590016

## Book Summary
"Charged" examines the enormous power of American prosecutors in the criminal justice system and how this power has contributed to mass incarceration. Emily Bazelon, a staff writer for The New York Times Magazine and lecturer at Yale Law School, argues that over the past 40 years, prosecutors have amassed more power than the system was designed for, largely using it to put more people in prison. The book follows two main case studies: Kevin, a young man from Brooklyn who was arrested for gun possession but given a second chance through a diversion program, and Noura, who was wrongfully convicted of murdering her mother and spent 11 years incarcerated before eventually being released. Through these stories, Bazelon illustrates both the destructive potential of prosecutorial power and the promise of reform-minded approaches.

The book also documents a new movement of reform-minded prosecutors who are working to transform the system by prioritizing diversion instead of jail or prison, minimizing the use of cash bail, and addressing racial disparities. Bazelon argues that electing reform-minded district attorneys represents one of the most promising paths to reducing mass incarceration, though she acknowledges the challenges and limitations of this approach.

## Themes Related to Wrongful Convictions
1. **Prosecutorial Power**: The book demonstrates how prosecutors control the outcome of cases through charging decisions and plea bargaining, often with little oversight or accountability.

2. **Plea Bargaining System**: Bazelon explains how the plea bargaining system can be inherently coercive, pressuring innocent people to plead guilty to avoid the risk of harsher sentences if they go to trial.

3. **Racial Disparities**: The book examines how prosecutorial discretion often leads to racial disparities in charging and sentencing.

4. **Mandatory Minimum Sentences**: Bazelon discusses how mandatory minimum sentences give prosecutors leverage in plea negotiations, contributing to mass incarceration.

5. **Reform Efforts**: The book highlights efforts by reform-minded prosecutors to reduce incarceration rates and address systemic injustices.

## Notable Cases of Wrongful Conviction
The primary wrongful conviction case featured in the book is that of Noura Jackson, who was convicted of murdering her mother in Tennessee. Key aspects of her case include:

1. **Prosecutorial Misconduct**: Days after Noura's conviction, prosecutors filed a motion to submit previously omitted evidence that called into question the testimony of the only witness who had placed her at the scene of the crime.

2. **Improper Closing Arguments**: The prosecutor's behavior during closing arguments was later deemed improper by the Tennessee Supreme Court.

3. **Supreme Court Reversal**: After more than five years following her conviction, the Tennessee Supreme Court unanimously decided to overturn the conviction and grant Noura a new trial.

4. **Alford Plea**: Rather than face another trial, Noura ultimately took an Alford plea, which allowed her to maintain her innocence while acknowledging that the state had enough evidence to convict her.

5. **Release After 11 Years**: After spending a total of 11 years incarcerated, Noura was eventually released.

## Causes of Wrongful Convictions Identified
1. **Unchecked Prosecutorial Power**: The book identifies the vast, often unchecked power of prosecutors as a primary driver of wrongful convictions.

2. **Coercive Plea Bargaining**: The pressure to accept plea deals, even for innocent defendants, to avoid the risk of much harsher sentences at trial.

3. **Withholding of Evidence**: As in Noura's case, prosecutors sometimes fail to disclose exculpatory evidence that could help defendants.

4. **Systemic Pressures**: Prosecutors face political and institutional pressures to secure convictions, which can lead to misconduct.

5. **Inadequate Defense Resources**: The book highlights the imbalance of power between prosecutors and defense attorneys, particularly for defendants who cannot afford private counsel.

## Reform Proposals
1. **Electing Reform-Minded Prosecutors**: Bazelon argues that electing district attorneys committed to reform is one of the most promising paths to reducing mass incarceration.

2. **Diversion Programs**: The book advocates for more diversion programs like the one that helped Kevin avoid prison time.

3. **Reducing Cash Bail**: Reform-minded prosecutors are working to minimize the use of cash bail, which disproportionately affects poor defendants.

4. **Addressing Racial Disparities**: The book calls for prosecutors to actively work to reduce racial disparities in charging and sentencing.

5. **Transparency and Accountability**: Bazelon suggests that greater transparency and accountability in prosecutorial decision-making could help prevent wrongful convictions.

## Connections to Other Research
Bazelon's work connects to other research on prosecutorial power and criminal justice reform, including:

1. Rachel Barkow's work on reducing the influence of elections and politics in criminal justice policy.

2. E.J. Patterson's research showing that reforms targeting only nonviolent offenses will not significantly reduce overall incarceration rates.

3. Studies by the Vera Institute of Justice on pretrial detention rates in rural versus urban counties.

## Additional Notes
While Bazelon is optimistic about the potential of reform-minded prosecutors to transform the system, she acknowledges several challenges:

1. The reform movement is relatively recent and localized, raising questions about its ability to transform places with entrenched tough-on-crime approaches.

2. Reform efforts often focus on lower-level offenses, but substantial reductions in incarceration would require addressing violent crimes as well.

3. The vulnerability of elected prosecutors to public opinion means reforms could be reversed if crime rates rise or high-profile cases generate backlash.

4. The book was published in 2019, making it a relatively recent contribution to the literature on prosecutorial power and criminal justice reform.
