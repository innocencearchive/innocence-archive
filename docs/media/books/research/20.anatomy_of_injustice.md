# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 20: Anatomy of Injustice

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Anatomy of Injustice: A Murder Case Gone Wrong
- **Author(s)**: Raymond Bonner
- **Publication Year**: 2012 (Paperback edition: January 8, 2013)
- **Publisher**: Knopf/Vintage (Penguin Random House)
- **Format**: Non-fiction book
- **Focus/Theme**: Detailed examination of a single wrongful conviction case, racial bias in the justice system, death penalty issues
- **Key Statistics**: The book that helped free an innocent man who had spent twenty-seven years on death row

### Wrongfully Convicted Individual Featured
- **Name**: Edward Lee Elmore
- **Case Location**: Greenwood, South Carolina
- **Conviction Year**: 1982
- **Exoneration Year**: 2012
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of Dorothy Edwards, an elderly white widow
- **Sentence**: Death penalty
- **Time Served**: Nearly 30 years (approximately 11,000 days), much of it on death row
- **Contributing Factors**: 
  - Racial bias (Black man accused of killing white woman in the South)
  - Police misconduct
  - Prosecutorial misconduct and evidence tampering
  - Inadequate defense representation
  - Mental disability (Elmore was described as "semi-literate" and "mentally retarded")
  - Flawed forensic evidence
- **Legal Status**: Released in 2012 after reaching an agreement with the state of South Carolina
- **Organizations Involved**: None specifically mentioned in the search results
- **Cross-Reference**: Similar themes to "Just Mercy" by Bryan Stevenson and "The Innocent Man" by John Grisham

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "Masterful... Eloquent, important, and accessible... The book of the century about the death penalty." —Andrew Cohen, The Atlantic
  - "A genuine whodunit, a page-turner, and a tale of redemption. And it's all true. For all that, however, Anatomy of Injustice is also a blistering indictment of the death penalty." —Laura Miller, Salon.com
  - "Gripping and enraging... Bonner's book is not a treatise against the death penalty. Rather, it is a look at what happens in America's justice system when justice is absent." —The Economist

- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - Racial discrimination in the criminal justice system
  - Prosecutorial misconduct and evidence tampering
  - Police misconduct in investigations
  - Inadequate defense representation for poor defendants
  - Flaws in the appeals process for death penalty cases
  - Difficulties in proving innocence after conviction
  - Systemic resistance to acknowledging and correcting wrongful convictions
  - Treatment of mentally disabled defendants

- **Policy Recommendations**: 
  - Reform of death penalty procedures
  - Better oversight of prosecutorial conduct
  - Improved standards for defense counsel in capital cases
  - Enhanced procedures for handling and preserving evidence
  - Greater scrutiny of cases involving racial disparities
  - Compensation for the wrongfully convicted
  - Reforms to make it easier to challenge wrongful convictions

- **Additional Resources**: 
  - After his release, Edward Lee Elmore filed a federal lawsuit against Greenwood County for his wrongful conviction
  - The case has been cited in discussions about compensation for the wrongfully convicted in South Carolina

## Research Strategy Notes
This book is particularly valuable for our project as it:
1. Provides an in-depth examination of a single case that exemplifies multiple systemic issues in the criminal justice system
2. Highlights the intersection of race, mental disability, and economic disadvantage in wrongful convictions
3. Documents the extraordinary length of time (nearly 30 years) that an innocent person spent incarcerated
4. Demonstrates the difficulty of overturning wrongful convictions even with substantial evidence of innocence
5. Shows how a book can contribute to public awareness and ultimately help secure an exoneration
6. Examines the death penalty system through the lens of a specific case

The book received significant critical acclaim and was described by reviewers as one of the most important books on the death penalty and wrongful convictions. Written by Pulitzer Prize-winning journalist Raymond Bonner, it combines investigative reporting with legal analysis to create what reviewers called a "seductive" and "compelling" narrative that educates readers about systemic injustices while telling a powerful human story.
