# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## The Psychology of Interrogations and Confessions: A Handbook

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: The Psychology of Interrogations and Confessions: A Handbook
- **Author(s)**: Gisli H. Gudjonsson
- **Publication Year**: 2003
- **Publisher**: John Wiley & Sons, Ltd
- **Format**: Hardcover, Paperback
- **Focus/Theme**: False confessions, police interrogation techniques, psychological vulnerability, miscarriages of justice
- **Key Statistics**: The book examines numerous cases of wrongful convictions resulting from false confessions across multiple jurisdictions

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured
The book discusses numerous case studies of wrongful convictions resulting from false confessions. While specific details for all individuals are not provided in the extracted text, the book mentions:

- Various individuals from Borchard's study who falsely confessed due to police pressure or psychological factors
- Cases where defendants' low intelligence was a factor in false confessions
- A 19-year-old woman whose voluntary false confessions were linked to Munchausen syndrome
- Cases where people confessed to protect the real criminal
- British cases of wrongful conviction resulting from false confessions

### Book Research Notes

- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "The influence of a stronger mind upon the weaker often produces, by persuasion or suggestion, the desired result" (Borchard, cited in the book)
  - "All judicial systems, whether adversarial or inquisitorial, are inherently fallible"
  - "Too often people have a misguided faith in the infallibility of the criminal justice system"

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
  1. Police pressure and coercive interrogation techniques
  2. Prosecutorial misconduct and abuse of power
  3. Psychological vulnerability of suspects
  4. Memory distrust syndrome - where individuals lack confidence in their memory or have their confidence undermined during questioning
  5. Imagination inflation - where false beliefs can be created through suggestion
  6. Public pressure for conviction in high-profile cases
  7. Overreliance on confession evidence in court proceedings

- **Policy Recommendations**:
  The book is part of the Wiley Series in The Psychology of Crime, Policing and Law, which aims to "bring out implications for both practice and policy." The text suggests:
  - Recognition of the inherent fallibility of judicial systems
  - Better understanding of psychological factors that contribute to false confessions
  - Improved interrogation practices that reduce the risk of false confessions
  - Greater awareness of psychological vulnerability in suspects

- **Additional Resources**:
  - Borchard's studies on wrongful convictions
  - Radin's (1964) examination of reasons for wrongful convictions in 25 American States
  - Research on memory distrust syndrome by Gudjonsson and MacKeith (1982)
  - Studies on imagination inflation and false beliefs (Loftus, 2001)
  - Research on confidence in memory (Saucier & Gaudette, 2000)

### Research Notes on Book Discrepancy
There appears to be a discrepancy in the book list provided. Book #44 was listed as "The Psychology of Interrogations and Confessions: A Handbook of Issues, Research, and Controversies" by David C. Raskin, Charles E. Honts, and John C. Yuille. However, the actual book with this title is authored by Gisli H. Gudjonsson. The book by Gudjonsson is a comprehensive handbook on interrogations and confessions that examines psychological factors, case studies, and legal implications of false confessions.

### Integration with Wrongful Conviction Database
This book provides valuable theoretical frameworks for understanding false confessions, which is a significant contributing factor to wrongful convictions. While it doesn't focus exclusively on individual cases with complete details that could be directly added to an exoneree database, it offers:

1. Psychological explanations for why false confessions occur
2. Categorization of different types of false confessions
3. Analysis of systemic issues that contribute to wrongful convictions
4. Case illustrations that demonstrate how psychological vulnerability interacts with interrogation techniques

The book would be most valuable for understanding the psychological mechanisms behind false confessions and for developing preventative measures to reduce wrongful convictions resulting from this factor.
