# Book Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Book 35: Anatomy of Innocence: Testimonies of the Wrongfully Convicted

### Basic Book Information
- **Title**: Anatomy of Innocence: Testimonies of the Wrongfully Convicted
- **Author(s)**: Edited by Laura Caldwell and Leslie S. Klinger
- **Publication Year**: March 28, 2017
- **Publisher**: Liveright Publishing Corporation
- **Format**: Hardcover, 304 pages
- **Focus/Theme**: Collection of exoneree stories told by prominent mystery/thriller writers
- **Key Statistics**: Features 14 exonerees; eyewitness misidentifications played a role in 70% of DNA-confirmed exonerations

### Book Research Notes
- **Key Quotes**: 
  - "The stories are variations on Kafkaesque themes: Innocents inexplicably implicated in gruesome crimes, stripped of their humanity in sterile interrogation rooms and jail cells, and denied any actual presumption of innocence in their eventual trials."
  - "Remarkably, most of the exonerees are more upbeat than bitter when freed."

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
  - Unreliable eyewitness testimony (played a role in 70% of DNA-confirmed exonerations)
  - Incentivized informants (witnesses promised rewards for testimony)
  - Police misconduct (coaching witnesses, withholding evidence)
  - Prosecutorial misconduct (suborning perjurious testimony, withholding information on deals)
  - Racial bias in the criminal justice system
  - Flawed forensic science

- **Policy Recommendations**:
  - Criminal justice reforms to prevent future miscarriages of justice
  - Better support systems for exonerees
  - Improved eyewitness identification procedures
  - Reforms to prevent use of incentivized informants

- **Additional Resources**:
  - Life After Innocence project at Loyola University in Chicago (founded by Laura Caldwell)
  - Innocence Project
  - National Registry of Exonerations

### Additional Notes
The book pairs each exoneree with a prominent mystery or thriller writer to tell their story, including Lee Child, Sara Paretsky, Laurie King, S.J. Rozan, and others. The editors note that apart from "nominal" fees paid to authors, editors, and exonerees, any royalties from the book go to the Life After Innocence project at Loyola University in Chicago.

The book is structured to cover every aspect of the wrongful conviction experience, from arrest through imprisonment to exoneration and life afterward. It aims to give readers "a deeper understanding of the price of our flawed criminal justice system and an urge to help."
