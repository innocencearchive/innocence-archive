# Book Research: Prosecutorial Misconduct

## Basic Information
- **Title**: Prosecutorial Misconduct
- **Author**: Bennett L. Gershman
- **Publication Year**: First published in 1985-1987, with 2nd edition in 2001-2002 (continuously updated)
- **Publisher**: West Group/Thomson Reuters
- **ISBN**: 0836613864 (ISBN-10), 978-0836613865 (ISBN-13)

## Book Summary
"Prosecutorial Misconduct" is a comprehensive legal treatise that establishes systematic guidelines on prosecutorial behavior, citing thousands of precedential cases. The book has become a preeminent resource for scholars and practitioners seeking guidance on wrongful convictions. It contains an extensive discussion of various types of prosecutorial misconduct and their relationship to wrongful convictions.

The book examines how prosecutors, who are constitutionally and ethically mandated to promote justice as "Ministers of Justice," sometimes deviate from these rules and engage in conduct that distorts the fact-finding process and produces erroneous convictions. Gershman argues that when prosecutors are motivated to zealously win convictions by any means and engage in conduct that either intentionally or carelessly undermines the integrity of the fact-finding process, they inevitably contribute to the conviction of innocent defendants.

The treatise covers various forms of prosecutorial misconduct, including investigative misconduct, misconduct in the grand jury, abuse of the charging function, selective and vindictive prosecution, suppression of evidence, misconduct in the plea bargaining process, misconduct in the presentation of evidence, prosecutorial-provoked mistrials, double jeopardy, misconduct at sentencing, and sanctions for misconduct.

## Themes Related to Wrongful Convictions
1. **Prosecutorial Ethics**: The book explores the ethical obligations of prosecutors as "Ministers of Justice" and how violations of these obligations can lead to wrongful convictions.

2. **Abuse of Power**: Gershman examines how the unchecked power of prosecutors in the American justice system can contribute to miscarriages of justice.

3. **Evidentiary Misconduct**: The book details how prosecutors' suppression of exculpatory evidence, presentation of false evidence, and other evidentiary misconduct directly contributes to wrongful convictions.

4. **Systemic Failures**: The treatise addresses systemic issues in the criminal justice system that enable prosecutorial misconduct to occur without adequate checks and balances.

5. **Accountability Mechanisms**: Gershman discusses the limited accountability mechanisms for prosecutorial misconduct and how this lack of oversight contributes to wrongful convictions.

## Notable Cases of Wrongful Conviction
While the book cites thousands of precedential cases, some notable wrongful conviction cases discussed include:

1. **The DeShaney Case**: The book contains an extensive discussion of this case, which illustrates how prosecutorial misconduct can lead to wrongful outcomes.

2. **Brady Violation Cases**: The book examines numerous cases where prosecutors failed to disclose exculpatory evidence as required by Brady v. Maryland, leading to wrongful convictions.

3. **Cases of False Testimony**: Gershman discusses instances where prosecutors knowingly presented false testimony, resulting in wrongful convictions.

4. **Selective Prosecution Cases**: The book addresses cases where discriminatory charging decisions contributed to unjust outcomes.

5. **Grand Jury Misconduct Cases**: Gershman examines cases where prosecutorial misconduct during grand jury proceedings led to wrongful indictments and subsequent convictions.

## Causes of Wrongful Convictions Identified
1. **Prosecutorial Tunnel Vision**: The book identifies how prosecutors' focus on obtaining convictions rather than seeking justice can lead to wrongful convictions.

2. **Suppression of Exculpatory Evidence**: Gershman details how prosecutors' failure to disclose evidence favorable to the defense is a significant cause of wrongful convictions.

3. **Presentation of False Evidence**: The book examines how prosecutors sometimes present false or misleading evidence, contributing to wrongful convictions.

4. **Improper Argument and Rhetoric**: Gershman discusses how prosecutors' inflammatory or improper arguments to juries can prejudice defendants and lead to wrongful convictions.

5. **Abuse of Discretionary Power**: The book identifies how prosecutors' broad discretionary powers, when abused, can result in charging and plea bargaining practices that contribute to wrongful convictions.

## Reform Proposals
1. **Enhanced Ethical Standards**: Gershman advocates for stronger ethical guidelines for prosecutors and better enforcement of existing standards.

2. **Increased Transparency**: The book suggests greater transparency in prosecutorial decision-making to prevent misconduct.

3. **Improved Accountability Mechanisms**: Gershman proposes more effective sanctions and disciplinary measures for prosecutors who engage in misconduct.

4. **Judicial Oversight**: The book recommends enhanced judicial supervision of prosecutorial conduct throughout criminal proceedings.

5. **Systemic Reforms**: Gershman suggests broader systemic reforms to address the structural issues that enable prosecutorial misconduct.

## Connections to Other Research
Gershman's work connects to other research on wrongful convictions and prosecutorial ethics, including:

1. **Innocence Project Research**: The book's findings align with the Innocence Project's documentation of prosecutorial misconduct as a leading cause of wrongful convictions.

2. **Studies on Brady Violations**: Gershman's work complements research on the frequency and impact of Brady violations in wrongful conviction cases.

3. **Research on Prosecutorial Immunity**: The book relates to studies examining how prosecutorial immunity doctrines limit accountability for misconduct.

4. **Wrongful Conviction Pattern Studies**: Gershman's analysis connects to research identifying patterns in cases of exoneration and wrongful conviction.

5. **Legal Ethics Scholarship**: The book contributes to the broader field of legal ethics scholarship, particularly regarding the special ethical obligations of prosecutors.

## Additional Notes
Bennett L. Gershman is a distinguished professor at the Elisabeth Haub School of Law at Pace University and a former prosecutor in the New York State Anti-Corruption Office. He is considered one of the foremost authorities on prosecutorial misconduct in the United States and has written extensively on the subject. His treatise "Prosecutorial Misconduct" has been continuously updated since its initial publication and remains a standard reference in the field.

In 2014, Gershman contributed a chapter titled "The Prosecutor's Contribution to Wrongful Convictions" to the book "Examining Wrongful Convictions: Stepping Back, Moving Forward," further emphasizing the connection between prosecutorial misconduct and wrongful convictions.

The book is particularly significant because it not only documents instances of misconduct but also establishes guidelines for proper prosecutorial behavior, making it valuable for both identifying causes of wrongful convictions and proposing solutions.
