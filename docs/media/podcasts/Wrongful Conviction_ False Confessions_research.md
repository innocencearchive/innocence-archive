# Podcast Research: Wrongful Conviction: False Confessions

## Basic Podcast Information

- **Title**: Wrongful Conviction: False Confessions
- **Host(s)**: Laura Nirider and Steve Drizin
- **Release Year(s)**: 2020-present
- **Production Company(ies)/Network**: Lava for Good Podcasts in association with Signal Co. No1
- **Series Type**:
  - Sub-series of the main "Wrongful Conviction" podcast
  - Episodic format focusing on different cases of false confessions
- **Format**: Investigative podcast examining cases of false confessions leading to wrongful convictions
- **Focus/Theme**: Explores how innocent people can be manipulated into giving false confessions
- **Key Statistics/Information**: 
  - Hosts are co-directors at Northwestern University's Center on Wrongful Convictions
  - Both hosts were central figures in the Netflix docuseries "Making a Murderer"
  - The podcast uses real interrogation audio to demonstrate techniques used to extract false confessions

## Episode List

### Norfolk Four
- **Description**: Four U.S. Navy sailors who falsely confessed to murdering another sailor's wife
- **Key Information**: This case contributed to Virginia becoming the first southern state to abolish the death penalty in 2021

### Tommy Ward (Parts 1 & 2)
- **Description**: Two-part series on Tommy Ward and Karl Fontenot from Ada, Oklahoma
- **Key Information**: Ward thought police would recognize his statement was just a dream, not reality

### Matt Livers
- **Description**: Case from small-town Nebraska where two murders shattered a peaceful Easter Sunday
- **Key Information**: Explores what happens when a confession starts falling apart and how police may "double down" or "fix it up"

### Robert Davis
- **Description**: Uses real interrogation audio to demonstrate how false confessions can occur

### Midnight Crew
- **Description**: Case from Chicago highlighting persistent issues in interrogation techniques

### David McCallum
- **Description**: Story of one of two New York teens wrongfully convicted of murder in 1986

## Podcast Research Notes

- **Key Quotes/Moments**: "Hosts Laura Nirider and Steve Drizin, co-directors at Northwestern University's Center on Wrongful Convictions and central figures in the smash hit Netflix docuseries Making a Murderer, tell us about not one, but four U.S. Navy sailors who falsely confessed to murdering another sailor's wife."

- **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
  - Interrogation techniques that can lead to false confessions
  - Psychological manipulation during police questioning
  - Reliance on confession evidence despite contradictions or lack of corroborating evidence
  - Tunnel vision by investigators once a confession is obtained

- **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**: 
  - The podcast implicitly advocates for reform in interrogation techniques
  - Promotes awareness of how false confessions occur
  - Directs listeners to the Center on Wrongful Convictions website for more information and involvement

- **Additional Resources**: 
  - Center on Wrongful Convictions website: https://www.centeronwrongfulconvictions.org/
  - Available on multiple platforms including Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and iHeart Radio
