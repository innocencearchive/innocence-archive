#   Podcast Research: In the Dark (Season 2)

##   Basic Podcast Information

-   **Title**: In the Dark (Season 2)
-   **Host(s)**: Madeleine Baran
-   **Release Year(s)**: 2018-2020
-   **Production Company(ies)/Network**: APM Reports (American Public Media)
-   **Series Type**:
    -   Investigative series (11 main episodes plus multiple updates)
-   **Format**: Investigative podcast
-   **Focus/Theme**: Investigation of the case of Curtis Flowers, a Black man from Winona, Mississippi, who was tried six times for the same crime
-   **Key Statistics/Information**: 
    - Curtis Flowers spent more than 20 years fighting for his life while a white prosecutor spent that same time trying to execute him
    - The podcast's investigation helped lead to Flowers' eventual exoneration
    - The case went to the U.S. Supreme Court

##   Episode List

### Episode 1: July 16, 1996
- **Description**: Introduces the case where someone walked into a furniture store in downtown Winona, Mississippi, and murdered four employees, each shot in the head. Curtis Flowers was charged with the murders.

### Episode 2: The Route
- **Description**: Examines the prosecution's case against Flowers, focusing on the route he allegedly walked the morning of the murders, the gun investigators believe he used, and his alleged confessions.

### Episode 5: The Confessions
- **Description**: Investigates Odell Hallmon, the prosecution's star witness who testified that Flowers confessed to him in prison.

### Episode 6: Reversal of Fortune
- **Description**: Follows up with Odell Hallmon, who later recanted his testimony against Flowers.

### Episode 7: The Trials of Curtis Flowers
- **Description**: Examines the makeup of the juries in Flowers' trials, which were all white or nearly all white.

### Episode 8: The D.A.
- **Description**: Investigates District Attorney Doug Evans, who prosecuted Flowers through six trials.

### Episode 9: Why Curtis?
- **Description**: Explores why investigators focused on Curtis Flowers as the main suspect despite limited evidence.

### Episode 13: Supreme Court
- **Description**: Covers the U.S. Supreme Court hearing of Flowers' case, focusing on whether DA Doug Evans tried to keep African-Americans off the jury.

### Episode 14: The Decision
- **Description**: Reports on the Supreme Court's 7-2 ruling throwing out Flowers' conviction from his sixth trial.

### Episode 16: Bail
- **Description**: Follows Flowers' chance to get out on bail after nearly 23 years locked up.

### Episode 18: The Prosecutor
- **Description**: Reports that after 23 years and six trials, District Attorney Doug Evans says he's done prosecuting the case.

### Episode 19: Freedom
- **Description**: Covers the Mississippi Attorney General asking the judge to dismiss the charges against Flowers for lack of evidence, leading to his release.

### Episode 20: Curtis Flowers
- **Description**: Features Curtis Flowers reflecting on his experience of being wrongfully convicted and his journey to freedom.

##   Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

### Curtis Flowers
-   **Name**: Curtis Flowers
-   **Age at Time of Conviction**: 26
-   **Conviction Year**: 1997 (first trial)
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Quadruple murder at Tardy Furniture store in Winona, Mississippi
-   **Sentence**: Death penalty
-   **Years Served Before Exoneration**: 23 years
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2020
-   **Contributing Factors**: 
    - Racial bias in jury selection
    - Unreliable witness testimony
    - Prosecutorial misconduct
    - Questionable evidence regarding his alleged route on the day of the murders
    - Witness recantations
-   **Legal Status**: Fully exonerated, charges dismissed
-   **Additional Information**: 
    - Tried six times for the same crime
    - Case went to the U.S. Supreme Court, which ruled in his favor
    - Mississippi Attorney General ultimately dismissed the charges for lack of evidence

##   Podcast Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**: "In this season, we investigate the case of Curtis Flowers, a Black man from Winona, Mississippi, who was tried six times for the same crime. Flowers spent more than 20 years fighting for his life while a white prosecutor spent that same time trying just as hard to execute him."

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
    - Racial discrimination in jury selection
    - Prosecutorial misconduct and persistence despite weak evidence
    - Eyewitness misidentification (leading cause of wrongful convictions according to the Innocence Project)
    - Unreliable jailhouse informant testimony
    - Systemic racism in the criminal justice system

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**: 
    - Reform of jury selection processes to prevent racial discrimination
    - Greater accountability for prosecutors
    - More scrutiny of jailhouse informant testimony
    - Better standards for eyewitness identification procedures
