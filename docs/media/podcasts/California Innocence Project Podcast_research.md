#   Podcast Research: California Innocence Project Podcast

##   Basic Podcast Information

-   **Title**: California Innocence Project Podcast
-   **Host(s)**: California Innocence Project team
-   **Release Year(s)**: 2018
-   **Production Company(ies)/Network**: California Innocence Project
-   **Series Type**:
    -   Limited Series (13 episodes)
-   **Format**: Podcast
-   **Focus/Theme**: Examines cases of wrongful conviction that the California Innocence Project has worked on, highlighting issues in the criminal justice system
-   **Key Statistics/Information**: 
    - Produced by the California Innocence Project, a legal organization that works to free the wrongfully convicted in California
    - Categorized as a Government podcast
    - Features detailed case studies of wrongful convictions and their causes

##   Episode List

### Episode 13 - Rodney Gupton: A Bad ID And A Lie Lead To A Wrongful Conviction
- **Description**: Examines how Rodney Gupton was convicted of shooting at a car based on the eye witness testimony of a 17-year-old

### Episode 12 - JoAnn Parks: How False Arson Evidence Put A Mother Behind Bars
- **Description**: Covers a tragic house fire where three children died, coupled with bad arson science that led to a mother's wrongful conviction

### Episode 11 - Horace Roberts: How An Affair Turned Into A False Murder Conviction
- **Description**: Details how Horace Roberts was wrongfully convicted of murder in 1999, and how new DNA testing suggested his innocence

### Episode 10 - Compensation Laws: The Fight To Compensate Those Wrongfully Convicted
- **Description**: Discusses California's compensation law for exonerees and the practical challenges in obtaining compensation

##   Podcast Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**: The podcast focuses on specific cases the California Innocence Project has worked on, highlighting different causes of wrongful conviction in each episode

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**: 
    - Eyewitness misidentification

    - Issues with DNA evidence handling and interpretation
    - Inadequate compensation for exonerees

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
