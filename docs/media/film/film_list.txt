# Organized Wrongful Conviction Reports by Series

## 1. 60 Minutes Reports

### 1.1. Eyewitness Testimony
- **Format:** TV news magazine segment
- **Focus:** Reliability of eyewitness testimony in wrongful convictions
- **Featured Individual(s):** Ronald Cotton, Jennifer Thompson, Other wrongfully identified individuals
- **Availability:** CBS website, Paramount+, 60 Minutes archives
- **Notes:** Multiple segments over the years examining how eyewitness misidentification contributes to wrongful convictions. The Cotton/Thompson case became a landmark example of the fallibility of eyewitness testimony.

### 1.2. The Pot Plot
- **Format:** TV news magazine segment
- **Focus:** Extreme sentencing in non-violent drug cases
- **Featured Individual(s):** Clarence Aaron (sentenced to life without parole for a first-time, non-violent drug offense)
- **Availability:** CBS website, Paramount+, 60 Minutes archives
- **Notes:** While not a wrongful conviction in terms of factual innocence, this case highlights unjust sentencing and prosecutorial overreach. Aaron eventually received clemency after serving 19 years.

### 1.3. 28 Years
- **Format:** TV news magazine segment
- **Focus:** DNA exoneration after decades of imprisonment
- **Featured Individual(s):** Cornelius Dupree (exonerated after 28 years in prison)
- **Availability:** CBS website, Paramount+, 60 Minutes archives
- **Notes:** Examination of one of the longest-serving exonerees in US history. Dupree was cleared by DNA evidence decades after a wrongful rape conviction.

### 1.4. Prosecutorial Misconduct
- **Format:** TV news magazine segment
- **Focus:** Consequences of prosecutors withholding evidence
- **Featured Individual(s):** Michael Morton (wrongfully convicted of murdering his wife), Ken Anderson (prosecutor who withheld evidence)
- **Availability:** CBS website, Paramount+, 60 Minutes archives
- **Notes:** This segment explores how Morton spent 25 years in prison before exoneration and the rare case of a prosecutor facing legal consequences for misconduct.

### 1.5. False Confessions
- **Format:** TV news magazine segment
- **Focus:** Psychological techniques leading to false confessions
- **Featured Individual(s):** Multiple cases including juveniles and vulnerable individuals, Often features work of the Innocence Project
- **Availability:** CBS website, Paramount+, 60 Minutes archives
- **Notes:** Series examining how interrogation techniques can produce false confessions, particularly from vulnerable populations such as youth and those with cognitive impairments.

### 1.6. Forced Confession
- **Format:** TV news magazine segment
- **Focus:** Police techniques leading to false confessions
- **Featured Individual(s):** Kevin Fox (falsely confessed to killing his daughter), Riley Fox (murder victim whose case was solved years later)
- **Availability:** CBS website, Paramount+, 60 Minutes archives
- **Notes:** Analysis of interrogation techniques that led to Fox's false confession after 14 hours of questioning without an attorney.

### 1.7. Shon Hopwood: From Bank Robber To Law Professor
- **Format:** TV news magazine segment
- **Focus:** Criminal justice reform through personal experience
- **Featured Individual(s):** Shon Hopwood (former inmate who became a law professor), Multiple wrongfully convicted individuals he helped
- **Availability:** CBS website, Paramount+, 60 Minutes archives
- **Notes:** While not focused exclusively on wrongful convictions, this segment explores how Hopwood transformed from bank robber to Georgetown law professor working on behalf of the wrongfully convicted.

## 2. Frontline (PBS) Reports

### 2.1. The Plea
- **Format:** Investigative documentary
- **Focus:** Plea bargaining system and innocent defendants pleading guilty
- **Featured Individual(s):** Erma Faye Stewart, Regina Kelly, Others who pled guilty despite innocence
- **Availability:** PBS website, PBS app, Educational distribution
- **Notes:** Explores the systemic pressures that lead innocent defendants to plead guilty rather than risk trial, particularly focusing on those with limited resources.

### 2.2. The Confessions
- **Format:** Investigative documentary
- **Focus:** False confessions in rape and murder case
- **Featured Individual(s):** The "Norfolk Four": Danial Williams, Joseph Dick, Derek Tice, Eric Wilson
- **Availability:** PBS website, PBS app, Educational distribution
- **Notes:** In-depth analysis of how four Navy sailors were convicted based on false confessions for a crime later linked to another perpetrator through DNA. Features interviews with the exonerees.

### 2.3. Death by Fire
- **Format:** Investigative documentary
- **Focus:** Flawed arson science in capital case
- **Featured Individual(s):** Cameron Todd Willingham (executed in 2004)
- **Availability:** PBS website, PBS app, Educational distribution
- **Notes:** Investigates the case of Willingham, executed for allegedly setting a fire that killed his children. Modern fire science experts now believe the fire was likely accidental, raising questions about his guilt.

### 2.4. Burden of Innocence
- **Format:** Investigative documentary
- **Focus:** Challenges faced by exonerees after release
- **Featured Individual(s):** Scott Hornoff, Calvin Willis, Neil Miller, Other exonerated individuals
- **Availability:** PBS website, PBS app, Educational distribution
- **Notes:** Examines the difficulties exonerees face reintegrating into society, including lack of compensation, PTSD, and limited support systems.

### 2.5. What Jennifer Saw
- **Format:** Investigative documentary
- **Focus:** Eyewitness testimony reliability
- **Featured Individual(s):** Ronald Cotton, Jennifer Thompson
- **Availability:** PBS website, PBS app, Educational distribution
- **Notes:** Detailed exploration of the Cotton/Thompson case that became a landmark example of eyewitness misidentification. Thompson later became an advocate for criminal justice reform after realizing her testimony sent an innocent man to prison.

### 2.6. The Case for Innocence
- **Format:** Investigative documentary
- **Focus:** DNA exonerations and resistance to overturning convictions
- **Featured Individual(s):** Clyde Charles, Roy Criner, Various exonerees whose cases faced institutional resistance
- **Availability:** PBS website, PBS app, Educational distribution
- **Notes:** Investigation into why the system often resists reopening cases even when new DNA evidence points to innocence. Explores institutional reluctance to acknowledge mistakes.

### 2.7. Reckoning with the Prisons
- **Format:** Documentary (PBS Frontline)
- **Focus:** Prison reform and wrongful convictions
- **Availability:** PBS website, YouTube
- **Notes:** A Frontline special examining the impact of wrongful convictions on prison reform movements and how exonerations have changed perspectives on criminal justice.

## 3. 20/20 Reports

### 3.1. The Wrong Man
- **Format:** TV news magazine episode
- **Focus:** DNA exoneration in murder case
- **Featured Individual(s):** Ray Krone (exonerated after 10 years for murder he didn't commit)
- **Availability:** ABC website, Hulu, ABC News archives
- **Notes:** Known as the "Snaggletooth Killer" due to dental evidence used against him, Krone was exonerated through DNA evidence. He became the 100th person exonerated from death row in the US.

### 3.2. Innocence Lost
- **Format:** TV news magazine episode
- **Focus:** West Memphis Three case
- **Featured Individual(s):** Damien Echols, Jason Baldwin, Jessie Misskelley Jr.
- **Availability:** ABC website, Hulu, ABC News archives
- **Notes:** Coverage of the controversial West Memphis Three case, featuring "satanic panic" elements and questionable evidence. Subjects were eventually released through an Alford plea after significant public attention.

### 3.3. Confession at Gunpoint
- **Format:** TV news magazine episode
- **Focus:** Coerced confession leading to wrongful conviction
- **Featured Individual(s):** Jeffrey Deskovic (wrongfully convicted at 16 of rape and murder)
- **Availability:** ABC website, Hulu, ABC News archives
- **Notes:** Explores how Deskovic falsely confessed after lengthy interrogation as a teenager. He was exonerated by DNA evidence after serving 16 years in prison and later established the Jeffrey Deskovic Foundation.

### 3.4. My Father's Killer
- **Format:** TV news magazine episode
- **Focus:** Arson conviction based on flawed science
- **Featured Individual(s):** Madison Hobley (wrongfully convicted of setting fire that killed his family)
- **Availability:** ABC website, Hulu, ABC News archives
- **Notes:** Investigation into the conviction of Madison Hobley for arson based on now-discredited fire investigation techniques. Hobley was sentenced to death before being pardoned.

### 3.5. Last Chance
- **Format:** TV news magazine episode
- **Focus:** Post-conviction DNA testing
- **Featured Individual(s):** Multiple cases of individuals seeking DNA testing to prove innocence
- **Availability:** ABC website, Hulu, ABC News archives
- **Notes:** Examines the challenges of obtaining post-conviction DNA testing and profiles several cases where it ultimately led to exonerations.

### 3.6. Stolen Years
- **Format:** TV news magazine episode
- **Focus:** Wrongful conviction for murder
- **Featured Individual(s):** Juan Rivera (exonerated after 20 years)
- **Availability:** ABC website, Hulu, ABC archives
- **Notes:** Investigates how Rivera was convicted three times despite DNA evidence pointing to another perpetrator. Explores systemic resistance to acknowledging wrongful convictions.

### 3.7. An Execution in Doubt
- **Format:** TV news magazine episode
- **Focus:** Possible wrongful execution
- **Featured Individual(s):** Claude Jones (executed in 2000, DNA later questioned key evidence)
- **Availability:** ABC website, Hulu, ABC archives
- **Notes:** Post-execution investigation that found the single piece of physical evidence linking Jones to a murder was actually not his, raising questions about his guilt.

### 3.8. The Perfect Lie
- **Format:** TV news magazine episode
- **Focus:** False confession in murder case
- **Featured Individual(s):** Martin Tankleff (wrongfully convicted of murdering his parents)
- **Availability:** ABC website, Hulu, ABC archives
- **Notes:** Examines how police convinced Tankleff that his father had awakened from a coma and identified him as the attacker - a complete fabrication that led to his false confession.

- **Featured Individual(s):** Gary Duncan (wrongfully prosecuted in Jim Crow-era Louisiana)
- **Availability:** Digital rental platforms, PBS
