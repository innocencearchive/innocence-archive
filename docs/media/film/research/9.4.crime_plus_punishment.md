## Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Focus is on whistleblower officers (the "NYPD 12") and PI Manuel Gomez, not specific exonerees. Features young minorities targeted by quotas.
-   **Note on Wrongful Conviction Focus**: The film highlights how illegal quotas pressure officers into making stops, issuing summonses, and making arrests without proper cause, particularly targeting minority youth. This leads to wrongful *interactions* with the justice system (stops, summonses, arrests) that can escalate, but the documentary focuses on this systemic cause rather than following specific individuals through a full wrongful *conviction* and exoneration arc. Searches did not identify named individuals presented as wrongfully convicted and later exonerated.
-   **Case Location**: New York City
-   **Contributing Factors**: Illegal policing quotas, pressure on officers, racial profiling/targeting.

## Film/Documentary Research Notes

-   **Notes from `film_list.txt`**: Explores how NYPD quotas directly lead to wrongful arrests and prosecutions, particularly in minority communities. Directed by Stephen Maing. Available via Hulu.
-   **Research Findings**: This documentary provides significant insight into the systemic issue of policing quotas and their consequences. It follows active-duty NYPD officers who risked their careers to expose the practice through a class-action lawsuit. While it powerfully illustrates the *cause* of potential wrongful encounters and arrests, the focus is on the whistleblowers and the system, rather than tracking specific individuals wrongly convicted as a result.
