## Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: The Exonerated Five (formerly known as The Central Park Five): Antron McCray, Kevin Richardson, Yusef Salaam, Raymond Santana, Korey Wise
-   **Case Location**: New York City, New York
-   **Conviction Year**: 1990 (for 1989 crime)
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2002 (Convictions vacated)
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Assault, Rape, Robbery (related to attack on Trisha Meili)
-   **Sentence**: Varied, from 5-10 to 5-15 years (Korey Wise, tried as adult, received 5-15)
-   **Time Served**: Between ~6 and ~13 years each
-   **Contributing Factors**: Coerced false confessions (obtained after lengthy interrogations without adequate counsel/parental presence), police misconduct, prosecutorial misconduct, racial bias, intense media pressure creating public frenzy.
-   **Legal Status**: Exonerated (Convictions vacated after Matias Reyes confessed and DNA confirmed his sole involvement); Received settlement from NYC.
-   **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project (involved in later stages/advocacy); Legal teams representing the five.

## Film/Documentary Research Notes

-   **Notes from `film_list.txt`**: Directed by Ken Burns, Sarah Burns, David McMahon; Examines the 1989 case of five Black and Latino teenagers wrongfully convicted. Available via PBS, Amazon, DVD.
-   **Research Findings**: This documentary provides a detailed account of the wrongful conviction of five teenagers in the high-profile "Central Park Jogger" case. It highlights the intense pressure, coercive interrogation tactics, and racial undertones that led to false confessions and convictions despite a lack of physical evidence connecting them. Their exoneration came years later when the actual perpetrator confessed and DNA confirmed his guilt. The case became a symbol of systemic flaws in the justice system, particularly regarding treatment of minority youth and reliance on confessions.
