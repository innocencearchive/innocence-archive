## Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: The San Antonio Four: Elizabeth Ramirez, Cassandra Rivera, Kristie Mayhugh, Anna Vasquez
-   **Case Location**: San Antonio, Texas
-   **Conviction Year**: 1997 / 1998
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2016 (Declared "actually innocent" by Texas Court of Criminal Appeals)
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Aggravated sexual assault of two young girls (Ramirez's nieces)
-   **Sentence**: Ramirez & Rivera: 37.5 years; Mayhugh & Vasquez: 15 years
-   **Time Served**: Varied, Ramirez served longest (~16 years before release on bond)
-   **Contributing Factors**: Allegations arising during "Satanic Panic" era, homophobic bias against the four lesbian women, coerced/unreliable accusations from children, flawed and later recanted medical evidence [cite: 79, 1.1]
-   **Legal Status**: Fully exonerated ("actually innocent")
-   **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project of Texas; Pro-bono legal teams.

## Film/Documentary Research Notes

-   **Notes from `film_list.txt`**: Examines case of four Latina lesbians wrongfully convicted; Themes include homophobia, "Satanic Panic," flawed forensics. Available via Amazon, Apple TV, DVD.
-   **Research Findings**: This documentary chronicles the decades-long fight of four women convicted based on unfounded accusations during the "Satanic Panic". It exposes how homophobia and flawed medical/child forensic interview practices contributed to their wrongful convictions. The film itself played a role in raising awareness and contributing to their eventual exoneration.
