### Basic Film/Documentary Information

-   **Title**: Deadline
-   **Episode # & Title (if applicable)**: N/A
-   **Director(s)**: Katy Chevigny, Kirsten Johnson
-   **Release Year**: 2004
-   **Production Company(ies)**: Big Mouth Productions *[Confirmation needed]*
-   **Format**: Documentary
-   **Focus/Theme**: Follows Illinois Governor George Ryan and his staff as they grapple with the state's flawed death penalty system, prompted by numerous exonerations (many stemming from investigations by Northwestern University journalism students). Culminates in Ryan's decision to commute all death sentences in Illinois.
-   **Key Statistics/Information**: Highlights the series of wrongful convictions uncovered on Illinois' death row. Documents Governor Ryan's blanket commutation of over 160 death sentences to life imprisonment in 2003.

### Film/Documentary Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**: Interviews with Governor Ryan, his staff, journalists, lawyers, potentially exonerees or their families. Footage of the clemency hearings and Ryan's final announcement commuting the sentences.
-   **Systemic Issues Identified**: High rate of error and wrongful convictions in capital punishment cases, systemic flaws in the criminal justice system (police/prosecutorial misconduct, inadequate defense), the role of journalism and academic investigation in uncovering injustice, political and moral dilemmas surrounding the death penalty.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**: The film documents a major policy action (blanket commutation) and implicitly supports moratoriums or abolition of the death penalty due to the risk of executing the innocent.
-   **Additional Resources**: Reports from Northwestern's Center on Wrongful Convictions, news archives covering the Illinois death penalty moratorium.
