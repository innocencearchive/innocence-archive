# Film & Documentary Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Research for: Murder to Mercy: The Cyntoia Brown Story (Film List Item 9.38)

### Basic Film/Documentary Information

-   **Title**: Murder to Mercy: The Cyntoia Brown Story
-   **Episode # & Title (if applicable)**: N/A
-   **Director(s)**: Daniel H. Birman
-   **Release Year**: 2020
-   **Production Company(ies)**: Daniel H. Birman Productions (Distributed by Netflix)
-   **Format**: Documentary Film
-   **Focus/Theme**: Chronicles the case of Cyntoia Brown, who was sentenced to life in prison at age 16 for killing a man in 2004. Explores her claims of self-defense and history as a victim of sex trafficking, the controversy around trying juveniles as adults, and her fight for clemency.
-   **Key Statistics/Information**: Brown was sentenced to life with parole eligibility only after 51 years. She served 15 years before being granted clemency by Tennessee Governor Bill Haslam in 2019.

-   **Legal Status**: Granted clemency; sentence commuted. Released to parole supervision in August 2019.
-   **Organizations Involved**: Legal teams advocating for juvenile justice reform, numerous public figures and advocates who supported her clemency campaign.
-   **Cross-Reference**: N/A (Not an exoneration case based on factual innocence).

### Film/Documentary Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**: Interviews with Cyntoia Brown over many years (building on earlier documentary work by the same director), legal experts discussing juvenile sentencing, details of her troubled upbringing and trafficking experience, the public campaign for clemency, Governor Haslam's clemency decision.
-   **Systemic Issues Identified**: Juvenile justice issues (trying minors as adults, life sentences for juveniles), intersection of sex trafficking and criminal culpability, sentencing reform needs, role of clemency, potential racial disparities in sentencing (raised by advocates).
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**: Implicitly supports reforms in juvenile sentencing laws, better recognition and support for victims of sex trafficking within the justice system, and consideration of mitigating factors like age and trauma in sentencing.
-   **Additional Resources**: Cyntoia Brown Long's memoir ("Free Cyntoia: My Search for Redemption in the American Prison System"), information on juvenile justice reform from organizations like The Sentencing Project.
