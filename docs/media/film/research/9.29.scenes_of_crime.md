## Research for: Scenes of a Crime (Film List Item 9.29)

### Basic Film/Documentary Information

-   **Title**: Scenes of a Crime
-   **Episode # & Title (if applicable)**: N/A
-   **Director(s)**: Grover Babcock, Blue Hadaegh
-   **Release Year**: 2011
-   **Production Company(ies)**: *Not found in search results.*
-   **Format**: Documentary
-   **Focus/Theme**: Examines the lengthy (nearly 10 hours) police interrogation of Adrian Thomas in Troy, New York, which resulted in a disputed confession regarding the death of his infant son. Critiques interrogation techniques and explores the phenomenon of false confessions.
-   **Key Statistics/Information**: Thomas's confession was the sole basis for his conviction, despite autopsy evidence indicating the baby died from sepsis (infection), not trauma. His conviction was later overturned because the confession was deemed coerced.

-   **Name**: Adrian Thomas

-   **Conviction Year**: 2009 (Crime/Death occurred 2008)
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2014 (Confession suppressed, conviction overturned; later acquitted at retrial in 2016).

-   **Contributing Factors**: False confession obtained through coercive interrogation techniques (prolonged interrogation, minimization, false suggestion of leniency/help, factual misrepresentations by police, exploiting defendant's emotional distress).
-   **Legal Status**: Exonerated. Confession suppressed and conviction overturned by NY Court of Appeals (2014). Acquitted at retrial (2016).
-   **Organizations Involved**: New York State Defenders Association, lawyers, potentially false confession experts (like Richard Ofshe or Saul Kassin, though not explicitly named in provided search snippets). APA filed amicus brief supporting admissibility of expert testimony.
-   **Cross-Reference**: Yes, Adrian Thomas is listed in the National Registry of Exonerations.

### Film/Documentary Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**: Extensive footage from Adrian Thomas's actual videotaped interrogation, interviews with Thomas, his family, lawyers, jurors, and potentially interrogation experts. Analysis of the specific techniques used by police during the 10-hour questioning.
-   **Systemic Issues Identified**: Coercive police interrogation methods (implicitly critiques Reid technique elements), psychological factors leading to false confessions (especially under duress/stress), unreliability of confessions as sole evidence, importance of allowing expert testimony on false confession research in court, difficulty for jurors to understand why someone would falsely confess without expert context.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**: Implicitly calls for reform of police interrogation practices, mandatory recording of entire interrogations, and ensuring courts allow relevant expert testimony on psychological factors influencing confessions.
-   **Additional Resources**: NY Court of Appeals decision in People v. Thomas (2014), APA amicus brief, resources on false confessions (e.g., from the Innocence Project).
