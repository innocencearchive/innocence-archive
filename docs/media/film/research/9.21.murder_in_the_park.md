#   Film & Documentary Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

##   Research for: A Murder in the Park (Film List Item 9.21)

###   Basic Film/Documentary Information

-   **Title**: A Murder in the Park
-   **Episode # & Title (if applicable)**: N/A
-   **Director(s)**: Christopher S. Rech, Brandon Kimber
-   **Release Year**: 2015
-   **Production Company(ies)**: Transition Studios, Whole Truth Films
-   **Format**: Documentary
-   **Focus/Theme**: Argues that Anthony Porter, famously exonerated from death row in Illinois, was actually guilty, and that Alstory Simon was wrongfully coerced into confessing to the crime by journalism professor David Protess, PI Paul Ciolino, and Northwestern University students, leading to Simon's wrongful conviction. Investigates potential "wrongful exoneration."
-   **Key Statistics/Information**: Anthony Porter was released in 1999, 50 hours before execution. Alstory Simon served 15 years before his conviction was vacated in 2014. Porter's exoneration contributed to Illinois Governor George Ryan's moratorium on the death penalty.

###   Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Alstory Simon (Argued as wrongfully convicted)
-   **Case Location**: Chicago, Illinois, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1999 (pleaded guilty)
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2014 (Conviction vacated)
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Double murder (of Jerry Hillard and Marilyn Green in 1982)
-   **Sentence**: 37 years
-   **Time Served**: Approximately 15 years
-   **Contributing Factors (Alleged by Film)**: Coerced confession (manipulated by PI Paul Ciolino), unethical investigative tactics by journalism professor/students (including allegedly fabricating evidence and manipulating witnesses), potential ineffective assistance of counsel (Simon's own lawyer allegedly conspired with Protess team).
-   **Legal Status**: Exonerated (Conviction vacated 2014). Filed a $40 million lawsuit afterwards.
-   **Organizations Involved**: Northwestern University's Medill Innocence Project (implicated in wrongdoing), Cook County State's Attorney's Office (involved in both Porter's release and Simon's exoneration).
-   **Cross-Reference**: Yes, Alstory Simon appears in the National Registry of Exonerations.

*(Note: The film also heavily features Anthony Porter, arguing he was rightfully convicted initially and wrongfully exonerated.)*

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**: *Specific quotes not found in search results.* Key moments include the depiction of the original 1982 crime, the methods used by Protess's team to investigate Porter's case, the videotaped confession obtained from Simon, Simon's eventual exoneration, and interviews detailing the alleged ethical breaches.
