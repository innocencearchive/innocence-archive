# Film & Documentary Research: Wrongful Conviction Media Sources

## Research for: A Crime on the Bayou (Film List Item 9.39)

### Basic Film/Documentary Information

-   **Title**: A Crime on the Bayou
-   **Episode # & Title (if applicable)**: N/A
-   **Director(s)**: Nancy Buirski
-   **Release Year**: 2020 (Premiere), 2021 (US Release)
-   **Production Company(ies)**: Augusta Films, Get Lifted Film Company, Artemis Rising Foundation (Distributed by Shout! Studios)
-   **Format**: Documentary Film
-   **Focus/Theme**: Tells the story of Gary Duncan, a Black teenager arrested in 1966 in Plaquemines Parish, Louisiana, for touching a white youth's arm while trying to de-escalate a confrontation. Chronicles his challenge against the segregated justice system, aided by young Jewish attorney Richard Sobol, leading to the landmark Supreme Court case *Duncan v. Louisiana*.
-   **Key Statistics/Information**: The case established the right to a jury trial for serious offenses in state courts, incorporating the Sixth Amendment right via the Fourteenth Amendment.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Gary Duncan (Subject of wrongful prosecution, not necessarily conviction in the traditional sense, as the core issue was denial of rights).
-   **Case Location**: Plaquemines Parish, Louisiana, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: Initially convicted 1966/67 (at bench trial).
-   **Exoneration Year**: N/A (Focus is on the legal challenge to the process/denial of rights). Supreme Court decision (1968) effectively invalidated the process under which he was denied a jury trial.
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Charged with and initially convicted of simple battery.
-   **Sentence**: 60 days in jail and $150 fine.
-   **Time Served**: *Unclear if sentence was served before/during appeals.*
-   **Contributing Factors**: Pervasive systemic racism in the Jim Crow South, abuse of power by local white supremacist officials (Leander Perez), denial of the constitutional right to a jury trial for an offense deemed "serious" based on potential punishment (up to 2 years).
-   **Legal Status**: His fight led to the landmark Supreme Court ruling in *Duncan v. Louisiana* (1968) guaranteeing the right to a jury trial in state courts for serious crimes.
-   **Organizations Involved**: Lawyers' Constitutional Defense Committee (Richard Sobol volunteered).
-   **Cross-Reference**: N/A (Not a typical exoneration case, but a landmark civil rights / criminal procedure case stemming from unjust prosecution).

### Film/Documentary Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**: Interviews with Gary Duncan and potentially Richard Sobol (or archival), detailing the events of the arrest, the atmosphere of hostility and racism in Plaquemines Parish under Leander Perez, the legal strategy challenging the denial of a jury trial, and the significance of the Supreme Court victory.
-   **Systemic Issues Identified**: Deep-seated institutional racism in the Jim Crow South, weaponization of the legal system against Black citizens, denial of fundamental constitutional rights (right to jury trial), courage required to challenge systemic injustice, role of civil rights lawyers, importance of Supreme Court precedents in advancing civil rights.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**: Highlights the importance of enforcing constitutional rights, particularly the right to a fair trial by jury, and serves as a historical reminder of the fight against systemic racism in the legal system.
-   **Additional Resources**: Supreme Court opinion for *Duncan v. Louisiana*, 391 U.S. 145 (1968); historical accounts of the Civil Rights Movement in Louisiana.
