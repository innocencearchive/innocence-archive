### Basic Film/Documentary Information

-   **Title**: Murder on a Sunday Morning (Un coupable idéal)
-   **Episode # & Title (if applicable)**: N/A
-   **Director(s)**: Jean-Xavier de Lestrade
-   **Release Year**: 2001
-   **Production Company(ies)**: Maha Productions (and others, potentially including HBO/Canal+) *Specific production list not fully confirmed in search.*
-   **Format**: Documentary
-   **Focus/Theme**: The wrongful arrest and trial of 15-year-old Brenton Butler for the murder of a tourist in Jacksonville, Florida, in 2000. Focuses on his defense by public defenders and the exposure of a coerced false confession.
-   **Key Statistics/Information**: Won the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature in 2002. Butler was acquitted after his defense demonstrated the confession was false and coerced.

### Film/Documentary Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**: Courtroom footage showing the defense dismantling the prosecution's case, Butler's testimony describing the interrogation and coercion, interviews with the defense attorneys Patrick McGuinness and Ann Finnell.
-   **Systemic Issues Identified**: Coercive police interrogation tactics leading to false confessions, particularly with juveniles; reliability of eyewitness identification; potential for racial bias in policing and initial suspect identification; importance of vigorous public defense.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**: Implicitly calls for reforms in police interrogation methods (especially video recording and protections for juveniles), scrutiny of eyewitness ID procedures, and adequate funding/support for public defenders.
-   **Additional Resources**: News articles covering the Brenton Butler case and its aftermath in Jacksonville.
