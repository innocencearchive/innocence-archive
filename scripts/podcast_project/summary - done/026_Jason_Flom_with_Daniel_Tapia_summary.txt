### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features Daniel Tapia, a man wrongfully convicted in New Orleans, Louisiana, a city with a notoriously high incarceration rate. Daniel recounts his harrowing experience of being arrested for a murder he did not commit, despite being a bystander to a shootout. He details the corrupt police tactics, including fabricated witness statements and racial bias, that led to his conviction for second-degree murder and a life sentence.  Daniel also vividly describes the inhumane conditions he and other inmates endured during Hurricane Katrina at Orleans Parish Prison, where they were abandoned without food, water, or assistance. Despite facing seemingly insurmountable odds, including overturned appeals and pressure to accept a plea deal, Daniel's relentless pursuit of justice and a crucial legal article he recalled during prayer ultimately led to a new trial.  Facing the risk of another wrongful conviction, he was coerced into taking a plea deal for manslaughter to secure his release after serving years in prison for a crime he did not commit.  Daniel's story highlights the deep flaws within the American criminal justice system, particularly the coercive nature of plea bargains and the dire consequences of wrongful convictions.
