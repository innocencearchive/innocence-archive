### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features Valentino Dixon, who was exonerated after serving 27 years for a murder he did not commit in Buffalo, New York.  Despite the actual perpetrator confessing on video shortly after the incident and numerous witnesses attesting to Valentino's innocence, he was wrongfully convicted.  The episode highlights the egregious failures of the justice system in his case, including an incompetent public defender who failed to present critical evidence like the confession and exculpatory witness statements.  Valentino recounts the devastating trial, his time in Attica prison, and how he found solace and purpose through art, specifically drawing golf courses.  Ultimately, it was the reinvestigation by Georgetown University students that uncovered a crucial Brady violation - withheld negative gun residue test results - which paved the way for his exoneration in 2018.  Valentino's story is a stark example of systemic failures and the long fight for justice, emphasizing the urgent need for prison and sentencing reform.

- **Cross-Reference**: Not specified if in exoneree database in transcript.
- **Narrative of Original Crime**: A shooting incident occurred in East Buffalo, NY, in the early morning hours. Toriano Jackson was killed, and two others were injured. Valentino Dixon was present nearby but not involved in the shooting.
- **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**: Eyewitness testimony identifying Valentino as the shooter.  These witnesses later proved to be unreliable and contradicted prior statements.
- **Trial Defender Notes**:  Failed to challenge eyewitness accounts effectively, did not present alibi or witness testimonies supporting Valentino's innocence, and crucially, did not introduce the video confession of the actual perpetrator or negative gun residue evidence.  Judge disregarded Valentino's concerns about his lawyer's incompetence.
- **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**: Georgetown University students uncovered that gun residue tests on Valentino's clothing were negative, and this evidence was withheld by the prosecution (Brady violation). This, combined with the existing confession video and witness accounts of his innocence, formed the basis for overturning the conviction.
- **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Lamora confessed multiple times, including on video shortly after the crime. He stated he acted in self-defense/defense of a friend because the deceased, Toriano Jackson, had shot another individual first.  Police initially dismissed his confession and did not arrest him. Lamora later committed another murder, reinforcing his violent tendencies and confession in the Dixon case.
