### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom tells the harrowing story of Eric Riddick's wrongful conviction for the murder of his friend, William Catlett, in Philadelphia in 1991. Despite a strong alibi and eyewitness accounts pointing to another individual, Edward Pina Johnson, Riddick was convicted based on a coerced statement and ineffective defense.  Key evidence, including ballistic reports and alibi witnesses, were ignored or suppressed.  Even after the coerced witness recanted and new evidence emerged, Riddick spent decades fighting for his freedom due to restrictive post-conviction laws.  Ultimately, through persistent legal efforts, support from various organizations, and the intervention of the Conviction Integrity Unit, Eric Riddick was exonerated after serving 30 years.  The episode highlights systemic issues of police misconduct, prosecutorial overreach, and the urgent need for criminal justice reform.  Riddick’s resilience and dedication to helping others after exoneration are also emphasized.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Police misconduct: Coercion of witnesses, ignoring evidence pointing to other suspects, withholding exculpatory evidence.
    -   Prosecutorial misconduct: Pressuring witnesses to implicate innocent individuals, prioritizing convictions over finding the truth.
    -   Ineffective defense counsel: Incompetence in presenting evidence and alibi witnesses.
    -   Flaws in post-conviction relief system: Restrictive time limits (PCRA - 60 days in Pennsylvania) that obstruct justice and prevent review of new evidence.
    -   Culture of corruption within the criminal justice system.
