### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction: Junk Science" examines the case of Dale Johnston, who was wrongfully convicted of murdering his stepdaughter and her boyfriend based on unreliable footwear comparison evidence presented by anthropologist Louise Robbins. The podcast details how Robbins, despite lacking scientific validation for her methods, convinced a three-judge panel that a boot print found at the crime scene matched Johnston's boot based on wear patterns inside the boot.  The episode highlights the devastating impact of junk science in the justice system, leading to Johnston's death sentence and four years on death row before his eventual exoneration due to inadmissible hypnotized witness testimony, not the debunking of the footwear evidence itself.  Years later, the actual perpetrator, Chester McKnight, confessed to the crime, revealing a drastically different narrative than what was presented at Johnston's trial.  The episode underscores the dangers of accepting unverified scientific claims in court and the profound consequences for the wrongfully convicted.
