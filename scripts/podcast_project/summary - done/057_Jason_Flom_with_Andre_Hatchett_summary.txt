### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom" tells the harrowing story of Andre Hatchet, who was wrongfully convicted of murder in 1992 and spent 25 years in prison.  The podcast features Andre, his brother Jerry, and Sima Safie from the Innocence Project, detailing the gruesome crime and the egregious failures of the justice system that led to Andre's conviction.  Despite being physically disabled at the time of the murder and having a solid alibi, Andre was convicted based solely on the unreliable testimony of a crack-addicted informant. The episode highlights the ineffective assistance of his defense attorneys, the withholding of crucial evidence by the prosecution, and the systemic biases that contribute to wrongful convictions. After years of fighting, the Innocence Project and the Brooklyn District Attorney's Conviction Review Unit uncovered evidence of undisclosed information proving the informant's unreliability, leading to Andre's exoneration in 2016. Andre's story exemplifies the devastating impact of wrongful convictions and the arduous journey to freedom and justice.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Andre Hatchet
-   **Case Location**: Brooklyn, New York, USA (Bedford-Stuyvesant park)
-   **Conviction Year**: 1992
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2016
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder (beating death of Netta May Carter)
-   **Sentence**: 25 years to life
-   **Time Served**: 26 years (25 years in prison, 1 year in jail awaiting trial)
-   **Contributing Factors**: Eyewitness misidentification (unreliable informant testimony), ineffective defense counsel, prosecutorial misconduct (Brady violation - failure to disclose exculpatory evidence).
-   **Legal Status**: Exonerated
-   **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project, Brooklyn District Attorney's Conviction Integrity Unit, Paul Weiss (law firm - pro bono assistance)
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Sima Safie (Innocence Project attorney featured in the podcast), Jason Craig (predecessor at Innocence Project who initially worked on the case), Barry Shek (mentioned in relation to witness questioning legislation, associated with Innocence Project generally), Paul Weiss law firm (provided legal resources).
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Netta May Carter, a 38-year-old woman, was brutally murdered in a Brooklyn park. Her body was found bludgeoned, skull exposed, teeth knocked out, dragged, and sexually violated. Metal pipes were found near the body, believed to be the murder weapon.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**: The sole key evidence was the eyewitness testimony of Jerry Williams, a crack-addicted informant with a lengthy criminal record (30 prior arrests, 20 convictions). Williams claimed to have witnessed Andre committing the murder from 40 feet away on a rainy night, recognizing him by voice despite never having spoken to him before.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Andre had two defense attorneys. The first was deemed ineffective by the judge for failing to present an alibi or cross-examine witnesses. The second, though ordered to present medical records proving Andre's physical inability to commit the crime due to recent gunshot wounds and mobility limitations, also failed to present this crucial evidence to the jury. Both were described as providing "unconscionable" defense.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:  The Brooklyn Conviction Review Unit uncovered two critical pieces of undisclosed evidence: 1) A memo showing that Jerry Williams initially identified a different man, James Pringle, as the perpetrator (who had an alibi), undermining Williams' reliability. 2) Evidence that Jerry Williams admitted to being on crack cocaine on the night of the murder, contradicting his trial testimony and further damaging his credibility. These undisclosed facts constituted Brady violations, leading to the overturning of Andre's conviction.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: While not definitively named, the podcast strongly implies Jerry Williams as a potential actual perpetrator. He knew the victim, was a crack addict (and the victim exchanged sex for drugs), and had a history of violence (including throwing a woman in a dumpster). The podcast suggests the extreme violence of the crime indicated a personal motive, raising further suspicion about Williams's possible involvement. James Pringle, initially identified by Williams, was ruled out due to an alibi.

    -   **Prosecutorial Misconduct/Brady Violations**: Failure by the prosecution to disclose exculpatory evidence that would have impeached the credibility of the key witness, Jerry Williams.
    -   **Police Misconduct**: Possible coercion or suggestive tactics in obtaining witness testimony.
