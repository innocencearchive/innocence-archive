### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction details the harrowing story of Jennifer Delprey, who was wrongfully convicted of first-degree murder in the death of a four-month-old infant under her care.  The prosecution relied on the now-discredited "shaken baby syndrome" (SBS) diagnosis, ignoring crucial medical evidence of pre-existing conditions and misinterpreting Jennifer's actions as abusive shaking.  Despite maintaining her innocence and presenting expert testimony challenging the SBS theory, Jennifer was convicted and spent over nine years in prison.  The case highlights the dangers of junk science in the courtroom, flawed interrogation tactics leading to misconstrued statements, and the arduous fight for exoneration.  Ultimately, through persistent legal efforts and the discovery of critical withheld evidence, Jennifer's conviction was overturned, and charges were dismissed, revealing a profound failure of the justice system and the devastating impact of wrongful convictions on individuals and families.

- **Innocence Lawyer Details**:
    - **Pat Blagan (Appellate Counsel, Blagan and Garvey)**:  Appellate lawyer who took on Jennifer's case in federal court, focused on Daubert standard and challenging SBS as junk science.
    - **Jody Garvey (Law Partner and Wife of Pat Blagan, Blagan and Garvey)**:  Specializes in appeals and post-conviction work, devised the strategy to argue "actual innocence" to get federal evidentiary hearing.

- **Cross-Reference**: Not explicitly stated whether Jennifer Delprey is in an exoneree database within the transcript.
- **Narrative of Original Crime**:  Infant IZ was in Jennifer Delprey's care at a daycare and became unresponsive.  Medical professionals initially diagnosed injuries as resulting from violent shaking by Jennifer, leading to charges of child battery and later murder after the child's death.
- **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - Testimony of four state medical experts, primarily Dr. Emily Flaherty, who asserted the injuries were caused by violent shaking (SBS/AHT).
    - Detective Kenneth Kroll's testimony regarding Jennifer's interrogation and the interpretation of her statement about a "slight shake" as a confession.
    - Medical findings of acute and chronic brain bleeds and retinal hemorrhages, interpreted solely as evidence of abuse.
- **Trial Defender Notes**:
    - Chose a bench trial instead of a jury trial, believing a judge would better understand the medical complexities.
    - Presented expert witness Dr. Wayne Tucker, a pathologist and pediatrician, who challenged the SBS diagnosis, pointed to alternative causes like seizures possibly triggered by medication, and highlighted the lack of bruising expected in SBS cases.
    - Character witnesses from Jennifer's coworkers testified about IZ's pre-existing discomfort and crying.
- **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - **The "Crow Letter"**: A memo by Detective Crow revealing that Dr. Harky, the autopsy pathologist, initially doubted SBS and questioned the homicide determination, which was withheld from the defense (Brady violation).
    - **Medill Innocence Project Investigation**: Students uncovered the Crow letter and other crucial documents through a Freedom of Information Act request.
    - **Evidentiary Hearing in Federal Court (2012-2013)**: Defense experts (Dr. Patrick Barnes, Dr. Michael Prang, and others) testified against SBS, highlighting pre-existing conditions (chronic subdural hematoma, CVT), biomechanical impossibility of shaking causing the injuries without other trauma, and lack of bruising.
    - **New Expert Report (2022)**:  State's new pathologist, Dr. Bennett, produced a report that was not confident in concluding homicide, weakening the prosecution's case.
- **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Not applicable. The exoneration was based on the conclusion that the infant's death was likely due to natural medical causes (undiagnosed conditions, potential seizures) rather than abuse by Jennifer Delprey.
