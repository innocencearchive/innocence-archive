### Executive Summary

This episode of Wrongful Conviction details the case of Richard Glossip, sentenced to death in Oklahoma for the 1997 murder-for-hire of his boss, Barry Van Tries, despite undisputed evidence he wasn't present during the crime. Glossip's conviction rested solely on the testimony of the actual killer, Justin Sneed, a meth addict who received life without parole in exchange for implicating Glossip. The episode explores Glossip's two trials, marred by ineffective counsel and prosecutorial focus, his harrowing experiences facing multiple execution dates, and the lethal injection drug controversies that led to stays. It highlights systemic issues like reliance on incentivized testimony, inadequate defense, and the influence of a "tough on crime" political culture. An updated introduction notes the Oklahoma AG's support for a new trial due to state errors and the US Supreme Court's recent decision to vacate Glossip's conviction based on a Brady violation concerning Sneed's credibility, though Glossip remains in custody awaiting a new trial.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    *   Over-reliance on testimony from co-defendants/informants who receive significant benefits.
    *   Ineffective assistance of counsel, particularly for indigent defendants in capital cases.
    *   Prosecutorial misconduct (alleged witness steering, Brady violations).
    *   Tunnel vision/confirmation bias in police investigations.
    *   The political nature of the death penalty and its prosecution ("tough on crime" culture, legacy of prosecutors like Bob Macy).
    *   Flawed clemency processes with potential conflicts of interest (prosecutor, prosecutor's son on board).
    *   Procedural bars (finality of judgment) preventing courts from addressing strong claims of actual innocence.
    *   The psychological torture inherent in death row and repeated execution dates/stays.
    *   Problems with lethal injection protocols, drug sourcing, and botched executions.
