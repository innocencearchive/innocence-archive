### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction, False Confessions, hosted by Lauren Nyreider and Steve Trisen, details the wrongful conviction of Henry McCollum and Leon Brown for the rape and murder of 11-year-old Sabrina Bowie in Robison County, North Carolina in 1983.  Both brothers, who had intellectual disabilities, were coerced into false confessions and sentenced to death based largely on these confessions by notorious prosecutor Joe Freeman Britt. Despite evidence pointing to another suspect, Roscoe Artists, and inconsistencies in the confessions, the brothers spent 31 years in prison. DNA evidence in 2014 finally exonerated them, highlighting systemic issues within the justice system, including the dangers of false confessions, prosecutorial misconduct, and the fallibility of the death penalty. The episode underscores the importance of innocence projects and commissions in rectifying wrongful convictions and questions the fairness and accuracy of the death penalty.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   False confessions, particularly from individuals with intellectual disabilities.
    -   Rush to judgment and tunnel vision in law enforcement investigations under pressure to solve horrific crimes.
    -   Prosecutorial misconduct, including withholding evidence (fingerprint evidence, cigarette butt evidence).
    -   Ineffective defense representation, especially for marginalized individuals against powerful prosecutors.
    -   Flaws and potential for error within the death penalty system.
    -   Racial and socioeconomic disparities within the justice system in Robison County.

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Abolish the death penalty.
    -   Support and expand Innocence Inquiry Commissions like the one in North Carolina in other states.
    -   Reform interrogation practices to prevent false confessions, especially for vulnerable individuals.
    -   Increase scrutiny of prosecutorial conduct and accountability for misconduct.
    -   Improve access to effective legal representation for all defendants, especially those with intellectual disabilities and from marginalized communities.
