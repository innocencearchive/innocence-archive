### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom features Joanne Parks, who was wrongfully convicted of arson and the murder of her three children in 1989.  The episode details the tragic fire, the initial accidental ruling, and the subsequent shift in investigation that led to Joanne's arrest and conviction four years later.  Raquel Cohen, attorney with the California Innocence Project, explains how outdated and unscientific arson investigation methods contributed to the wrongful conviction.  The podcast highlights the crucial role of evolving fire science in Joanne's case and the fight for her exoneration.  Despite facing life without parole, Joanne received clemency after 29 years and is now free, although her legal battle for full exoneration continues. The episode emphasizes systemic issues within the justice system and the urgent need for scientifically sound forensic practices.

-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:

-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Defense expert attempted to explain flashover and the possibility of accidental causes, including a Zenith TV known for starting fires. The defense expert's credibility was undermined by the prosecution. The defense also mentioned the Zenith television as a possible cause, but the original TV was discarded by investigators.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    -   Advancements in fire science proving flashover occurred, invalidating the two points of origin theory as definitive proof of arson.
    -   Re-evaluation of burn patterns showing they were consistent with flashover, not necessarily arson.
    -   Recognition that initial arson investigation methods were flawed and not scientifically sound.
    -   Clemency granted by Governor Newsom in 2021, commuting sentence to parole eligibility due to doubts about the conviction.
