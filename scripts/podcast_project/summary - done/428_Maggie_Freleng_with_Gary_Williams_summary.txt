### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Maggie Freeling tells the story of Gary Williams, who has been incarcerated in New York since 2001 for allegedly raping his 86-year-old grandmother, Rosemary Williams.  The podcast reveals significant inconsistencies and lack of evidence in the case, including no physical evidence of assault, no proper forensic exam, and conflicting testimonies.  The prosecution heavily relied on the testimony of the alleged victim and a doctor whose credibility is questioned. Gary maintained his innocence and refused plea deals.  Despite a hung jury in the first trial, he was convicted in a second trial primarily based on the victim's testimony and circumstantial factors like him leaving the state after the accusation.  The episode highlights potential motives for false accusation within the family and ineffective legal representation during the second trial.  It concludes with the efforts of the Legal Aid Society's Wrongful Conviction Unit to review Gary's case and seek his release, emphasizing systemic failures and the devastating impact of wrongful convictions.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Flawed police investigation (lack of crime scene investigation, no evidence collection, failure to follow protocols).
    -   Inadequate medical examination and questionable testimony from Dr. Ware.
    -   Ineffective assistance of counsel, particularly in the second trial.
    -   Potential for bias in the justice system based on prior record and sympathy for the alleged victim.
    -   Pressure on defendants to plead guilty even when innocent due to potential for harsher sentences.
    -   Challenges faced by wrongfully convicted individuals in parole hearings when required to admit guilt for sex crimes.
