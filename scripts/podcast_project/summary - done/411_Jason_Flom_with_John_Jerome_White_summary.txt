### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features the story of John Jerome White, wrongfully convicted in 1979 for rape, robbery, and aggravated assault in Manchester, Georgia.  Despite no physical evidence linking him to the crime and significant issues with eyewitness identification, John was convicted based on the victim's identification in a photo array and live lineup. He served over 27 years in prison before being exonerated in 2004 thanks to DNA testing and the Georgia Innocence Project. The podcast highlights the devastating impact of wrongful convictions, the flaws in eyewitness testimony, especially cross-racial identification, and the challenges exonerees face upon release, including the lack of compensation and societal reintegration. John and Amy Maxwell from the Georgia Innocence Project discuss the systemic issues and the ongoing fight for compensation laws in Georgia and support for exonerees.

    -   Discovery that James Parham was a suspect at the time of the original investigation.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: James Parham. He was in the live lineup but misidentified. DNA evidence confirmed him as the perpetrator. He later pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 20 years for the rape. He was even roommates with John White at one point during their incarceration, unknowingly.
