### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom details the story of Ruel Saylor, wrongfully convicted of murder, kidnapping, and assault in Cuyahoga County, Ohio.  Despite being innocent and having a strong alibi, Ruel was convicted based on a corrupt vice detective's false informant claim and coerced witness testimonies.  The real shooter, Cordell Hubbard, confessed multiple times, including in court, but Ruel's appeals were repeatedly denied by the same judge.  After 15 years of incarceration, the Ohio Innocence Project and private attorney Kim Carole took on his case.  Key evidence, including recantations and new affidavits, eventually led to the vacating of his conviction, although he had to plead guilty to perjury to secure his release.  Ruel’s case highlights systemic issues of police misconduct, prosecutorial overreach, and the immense personal toll of wrongful convictions. He was finally freed but not fully exonerated and continues to fight for full legal recognition of his innocence and compensation.

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    - Police corruption: Vice detective fabricating evidence and threatening witnesses.
    - Witness coercion and intimidation by police.
    - Ineffective assistance of counsel due to rushed trial preparation and external pressures.
    - Judicial bias: Judge repeatedly denied new trials despite compelling evidence of innocence and confession from the actual perpetrator.
    - Prosecutorial tactics: Offering a "deal" requiring a perjury plea even when evidence of innocence was overwhelming.
    - Racial bias in jury selection and perception.
    - Difficulties in challenging convictions even with strong evidence of innocence.
