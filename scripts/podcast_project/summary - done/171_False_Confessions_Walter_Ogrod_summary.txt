### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction, False Confessions" tells the harrowing story of Walter O'Grod, wrongfully convicted in Philadelphia for the 1988 murder of four-year-old Barbara Jean Horn.  Despite profound learning disabilities and being interrogated for 14 hours while sleep-deprived, Walter confessed to the crime, a confession later proven false.  The episode details the corrupt Philadelphia justice system under Frank Rizzo, police coercion tactics, and the use of jailhouse snitches.  After nearly three decades on death row, Walter was exonerated in 2020 thanks to the efforts of a new District Attorney's Conviction Integrity Unit and newly discovered DNA evidence and autopsy findings.  The story highlights systemic issues of police misconduct, prosecutorial overreach, and the importance of reform-minded prosecutors in rectifying past injustices. The episode ends with a hopeful message about change and the pursuit of truth in the justice system.

    -   Use of unreliable jailhouse snitches and prosecutorial reliance on such testimony.
    -   Prosecutorial misconduct in withholding exculpatory evidence.
    -   Judicial resistance to overturning wrongful convictions, even with strong evidence of innocence.
    -   Impact of cognitive disabilities on vulnerability to false confessions.
    -   Lack of recording of interrogations.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:

    -   Need for increased scrutiny of jailhouse snitch testimony.
    -   Importance of conviction integrity units within prosecutor's offices to review and rectify wrongful convictions.
    -   Call for prosecutors to prioritize truth and justice over conviction rates.
