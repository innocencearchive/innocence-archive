### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This special edition of the Wrongful Conviction podcast is recorded live from the Innocence Network conference in San Diego. Host Jason Flom interviews Maddie DeLone, Executive Director of the Innocence Project, who discusses the history and purpose of the conference as a vital support network for exonerees. The episode then features interviews with two exonerees, Floyd Bledsoe and Cornelius Dupree Jr., who share their harrowing stories of wrongful conviction and subsequent exoneration. Bledsoe recounts being convicted of a murder his brother confessed to, serving 16 years before DNA evidence cleared him. Dupree details his 31-year imprisonment for a robbery and sexual assault he did not commit, highlighting the role of eyewitness misidentification and racial bias. Both exonerees emphasize the importance of the Innocence Network community in their healing and advocacy for criminal justice reform.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Eyewitness misidentification, especially cross-racial misidentification.
    -   Flawed forensic science and admission of bad evidence.
    -   Tunnel vision and poor investigation by law enforcement.
    -   Lack of compensation for exonerees in many states.
    -   Pressure to protect jury verdicts over pursuing justice.
    -   Ineffective defense representation.
    -   Racial bias in the criminal justice system.

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Strategic litigation to challenge bad evidence in court.
    -   Improving eyewitness identification procedures.
    -   Mandatory recording of interrogations (mentioned by Floyd Bledsoe as a reform needed in Kansas).
    -   Need for compensation statutes for exonerees in all states.
    -   Encouraging public support and involvement with the Innocence Project and similar organizations.

-   **Personal Narrative/Impact**:
    -   **Floyd Bledsoe**:  Emphasis on the emotional impact of being separated from his children for 16 years and the healing and support found at the Innocence Network conference.  Expresses amazement at the joy and grace of exonerees. Now advocates for legislative change in Kansas.
    -   **Cornelius Dupree**:  Highlights the psychological toll of wrongful imprisonment and the importance of community and family for healing.  His marriage to a former prison guard is presented as a remarkable and positive outcome. Now serves as an ambassador to new exonerees, offering support and guidance.
