### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction: Junk Science" hosted by Josh Dubin, focuses on the unreliability of gunshot residue evidence, using the case of Raymond Carl Allen Warren as a central example.  Warren was wrongfully convicted of murder at 17 based on a flawed atomic absorption gunshot residue test and coerced testimony from his friends. The podcast details the history of gunshot residue testing, highlighting the now-discredited paraffin test and the limitations of the atomic absorption test used in Warren's case, emphasizing its susceptibility to false positives from environmental contamination and common substances like brake linings.  Joanna Sanchez from the Ohio Public Defender's Office, Warren's current lawyer, discusses the specifics of his case and the systemic issues with gunshot residue evidence.  The episode stresses the importance of critical evaluation of forensic evidence in the justice system and encourages listeners to advocate for reform and share information to prevent future wrongful convictions based on junk science.  It concludes with a call to action for listeners to engage with public officials and support organizations fighting wrongful convictions.
