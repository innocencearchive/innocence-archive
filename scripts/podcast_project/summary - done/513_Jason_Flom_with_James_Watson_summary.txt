### Executive Summary

This episode of Wrongful Conviction details the case of James Watson, wrongfully convicted alongside 16-year-old Fred Clay for the 1979 robbery and murder of Boston cab driver Jeffrey Boyajian. The conviction rested heavily on questionable eyewitness identifications: one from cab driver Richard Dwyer allegedly aided by hypnosis (with evidence later showing the police and prosecution misrepresented the timeline of hypnosis vs. photo identification), and others from the Sweatt family, who had intellectual disabilities and were potentially incentivized by being moved from their housing project. Watson's girlfriend, Diane Moses, also testified against him after being threatened by police. Despite maintaining his innocence and initially facing the death penalty, Watson was convicted twice (1981, 1984) and served over 41 years. Following Fred Clay's exoneration, attorney Barb Munro and the New England Innocence Project uncovered withheld evidence and proof of prosecutorial misconduct, leading to Watson's release in 2020 and the granting of a new trial, effectively exonerating him. The victim's family supported his release, and Watson now runs the nonprofit "Confronting Injustice."

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

**Individual 1:**

-   **Name**: James Watson
-   **Case Location**: Boston, Massachusetts, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1981 (First trial), 1984 (Second trial)
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2020 (Motion for New Trial allowed, state did not pursue further)
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: First-degree murder, Robbery
-   **Sentence**: Life without parole (Initially faced the death penalty, which was ruled unconstitutional in MA before his trial concluded based partly on his case)
-   **Time Served**: Approx. 41 years (Released April 16, 2020)
-   **Contributing Factors**:
    *   Eyewitness Misidentification (Richard Dwyer, Neil Sweatt, Philippa Sweatt)
    *   Cross-racial identification issues
    *   Use of Hypnosis / Junk Science (Used on Dwyer, attempted on Neil Sweatt)
    *   Police Coercion / Witness Tampering (Threats made to Diane Moses)
    *   Witness Incentivization (Sweatt family moved to preferred housing project)
    *   Police/Prosecutorial Misconduct (Withholding exculpatory evidence - hypnosis letter, ignoring third-party culprits, misrepresenting hypnosis timeline at trial)
    *   Ineffective Assistance of Counsel (Failure to adequately challenge identifications, failure to object timely to prosecutor leading witness, failure to raise third-party culprit defense)
    *   Tunnel Vision (Focusing exclusively on Watson and Clay)
-   **Legal Status**: Exonerated (Motion for New Trial allowed, case not retried)
-   **Organizations Involved**: Committee for Public Counsel Services Innocence Program (via Fred Clay's case), New England Innocence Project
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**:
    *   Barb Munro: Appellate attorney who took over the case in 2018 and led the successful effort for release and exoneration.
    *   Lisa Kavanaugh & Jeff Harris: Handled Fred Clay's case; their work provided a foundation for Watson's appeal.
    *   Madeline Blanchett & Radha Naderajan (New England Innocence Project): Served as co-counsel, assisted with investigation and motion writing (specifically motion to stay).
    *   John Nerdese: Investigator credited with finding key evidence, including confirmation of Diane Moses's coerced testimony and the hypnosis letter.
-   **Cross-Reference**: Not specified in transcript (but Fred Clay was featured previously)
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: On November 16, 1979, around 4 AM, cab driver Jeffrey Boyajian was robbed and fatally shot by three men outside the Archdale housing projects in Roslindale, Boston, after dropping off a fare.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    *   Eyewitness identification by Neil Sweatt (neighbor with intellectual disabilities, viewed from ~80 ft at night).
    *   Eyewitness identification by Richard Dwyer (cab driver who claimed to see men hail the victim's cab downtown, identification made/strengthened post-hypnosis, timeline misrepresented at trial).
    *   Testimony of Diane Moses (James's girlfriend) stating James admitted being present but not shooting (testimony coerced by police threats).
    *   (Second Trial Only) Eyewitness identification by Philippa Sweatt (Neil's mother, also with intellectual disabilities, made hesitant ID after prosecutor repeatedly directed her attention to James).
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Original defense counsel was ineffective; failed to effectively counter the hypnosis-influenced ID, did not object timely when the prosecutor physically guided Philippa Sweatt's ID in the second trial, and failed to present known evidence pointing to alternative suspects (the Cooper brothers).
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    *   Discovery of a letter from the prosecutor (Tim O'Neill) to a hypnosis expert explicitly stating Dwyer was shown photos *after* being hypnotized, contradicting trial testimony and proving evidence was withheld (Brady violation).
    *   Confirmation from Diane Moses's daughter, Cheryl, that Diane admitted she lied about James's confession due to police threats of taking her children.
    *   Evidence of viable third-party culprits (Tyrone and Junior Cooper) known to police at the time but disregarded.
    *   Evidence highlighting the unreliability of the Sweatt identifications (distance, lighting, intellectual disabilities, motivation to move).
    *   Establishment of Ineffective Assistance of Counsel based on the failures of the original trial lawyers.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Tyrone and Junior Cooper were implicated early on by Diane Moses and another man, Jimmy Poole. They were known to wear leather jackets (matching witness descriptions), Tyrone was short (matching description of shooter), lived in the projects, and were known for similar crimes. Police did not pursue this lead.

### Podcast Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**:

    *   James Watson's final thoughts: "Man, don't be afraid to speak up. Somebody will believe you... I'm living proof. Somebody will listen to you."
-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    *   Unreliability and suggestibility of eyewitness identification, especially cross-racial.
    *   Use and misuse of junk science (hypnosis) in investigations and court.
    *   Police coercion and threats to secure witness testimony.
    *   Prosecutorial misconduct, including withholding exculpatory evidence (Brady violations).
    *   Ineffective assistance of counsel.
    *   Police tunnel vision ignoring alternative suspects.
    *   The profound psychological impact of long-term incarceration (Post Incarceration Syndrome).
    *   Lack of adequate support and compensation for exonerees upon release.
    *   The injustice of juvenile life without parole sentences.
    *   Influence of external pressure (cab driver union, company owner) on police investigations.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    *   Need for immediate and substantial compensation for wrongfully convicted individuals upon release.
    *   Reform regarding prosecutorial immunity.
    *   Increased awareness and resources to address Post Incarceration Syndrome.
    *   Encouragement for listeners to get involved with innocence organizations (like New England Innocence Project) and support exonerees.
    *   A call for individuals facing injustice to speak up and seek help.
-   **Personal Narrative/Impact**: James describes the terror of entering Walpole prison, the decades of feeling like waiting to die, the loss of raising his son, the overwhelming joy of freedom, reconnecting with his son and meeting grandchildren, the profound experience of meeting and being supported by the victim's family, and his dedication to helping others avoid similar fates through his nonprofit "Confronting Injustice." He emphasizes the deep appreciation for freedom after losing it and the psychological reprogramming that occurs during long-term incarceration.
