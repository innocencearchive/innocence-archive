### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction: Junk Science" examines the case of Dale Johnson, who was wrongfully convicted of murdering his stepdaughter and her boyfriend in 1984. The episode highlights the flawed forensic science of footwear analysis, specifically the testimony of anthropologist Louise Robbins, whose unvalidated methods led to Johnson's conviction and death sentence. Despite an FBI analyst finding the footprint evidence inconclusive, Robbins claimed with "scientific certainty" that a footprint at the crime scene matched Johnson's boot based on wear patterns inside the boot.  The podcast reveals how public opinion, prosecutorial misconduct, and the acceptance of "fantasy forensics" contributed to this miscarriage of justice. Johnson was eventually exonerated in 1990 due to inadmissible hypnotized witness testimony, and later, the actual perpetrator confessed. The episode underscores the dangers of subjective forensic analysis and the devastating impact of wrongful convictions.
