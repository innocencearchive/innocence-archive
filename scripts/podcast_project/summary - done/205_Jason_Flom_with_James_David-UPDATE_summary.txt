### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom details the harrowing story of James J. Davis, who was wrongfully convicted of murder and spent nearly 20 years in prison.  The episode highlights the egregious errors and misconduct that led to his conviction, including a suggestive lineup, false identification by a vindictive ex-girlfriend, and key testimony from a dangerous drug dealer who was later murdered and under FBI investigation at the time of the trial.  Davis's original defense was ineffective, failing to present alibi witnesses and crucial evidence. Despite these injustices and an initial hung jury leaning towards acquittal, Davis was convicted in a second trial.  The Legal Aid Society, particularly attorneys Susan Epstein and Elizabeth Felber, took on his case, eventually proving ineffective counsel and securing his release in May 2021.  The podcast emphasizes the systemic failures within the justice system and celebrates Davis's long-awaited freedom while urging listeners to support innocence projects and advocate for justice reform. The episode serves as a stark example of how easily wrongful convictions can occur and the immense dedication required to overturn them.

    -   Over-reliance on unreliable eyewitness testimony and suggestive identification procedures.
    -   Failure of conviction review units to act decisively and promptly on credible innocence claims.
    -   Potential prosecutorial misconduct in using a known unreliable witness (Jose Machicote).
