### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode details the wrongful conviction of Eric Glissen and five others for two separate 1995 Bronx murders they did not commit. Relying on shaky eyewitness testimony from Miriam Tavares and her young translator Kathy Gomez, police linked the unrelated murders of FedEx executive Denise Raymond and cab driver Bath Diop. Glissen was convicted for the Diop murder and sentenced to 25-to-life, ultimately serving over 17 years. While incarcerated, Glissen's persistent investigation uncovered phone records from Diop's stolen phone implicating members of the Sex Money Murder gang. This information reached US Attorney investigator John O'Malley, who realized two gang members had confessed to the Diop murder years prior in 2003, a fact missed due to an NYPD error stating the crime didn't exist in their records. This revelation led to Glissen's release in 2012 and the eventual exoneration of all six individuals.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Reliance on unreliable eyewitness testimony (especially single witness).
    -   Police tunnel vision and confirmation bias.
    -   Coercion and manipulation of witnesses (especially vulnerable ones like minors).
    -   Failure to conduct thorough investigations (e.g., checking witness vantage points, verifying phone logs).
    -   Mishandling or loss of physical evidence by prosecution.
    -   Lack of communication and information sharing between different law enforcement agencies (NYPD and US Attorney's Office).
    -   Prosecutorial resistance to admitting error and delaying exonerations.
    -   Absolute prosecutorial immunity shielding misconduct.
    -   Impact of poverty on obtaining adequate legal defense.
    -   Profit motive within the prison-industrial complex.
    -   Decline of vocational and educational programs in prisons.

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Implement reforms for eyewitness identification procedures.
    -   Increase accountability for police and prosecutorial misconduct (revisiting immunity).
    -   Ensure better evidence handling protocols.
    -   Mandate improved information sharing between agencies.
    -   Establish/strengthen conviction integrity units within DA offices.
    -   Promote public education about wrongful conviction causes (jury awareness).
    -   Reinstate and fund robust vocational and educational programs in prisons to aid rehabilitation and reduce recidivism.
    -   Address the financial incentives within the justice and prison systems.

-   **Personal Narrative/Impact**: Eric Glissen described the profound trauma of being arrested just after his daughter's birth, feeling his life crumble. He emphasized his unwavering belief in his innocence and relentless fight from prison, utilizing the law library and writing countless letters. He found strength in support networks like RTA and fellow innocent inmates. Post-exoneration, he dedicates himself to advocacy, stressing the importance of inner strength, positivity, and holding onto hope. His final thoughts focus critically on the prison system's profit motives and the detrimental removal of rehabilitative programs, arguing for systemic change and accountability.
