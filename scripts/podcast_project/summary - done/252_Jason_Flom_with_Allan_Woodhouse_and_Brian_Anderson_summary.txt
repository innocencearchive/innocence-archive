-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Racial bias in policing: Targeting Indigenous individuals as suspects based on flimsy eyewitness descriptions and assumptions.
    -   Coercive interrogation techniques: Use of physical violence and psychological pressure to extract false confessions from vulnerable individuals with limited English proficiency.
    -   Ineffective legal representation: Failure of trial defense to adequately challenge false confessions, present alibi evidence, and address police misconduct.
    -   Judicial bias: Trial judge's prejudicial comments and actions, including interrupting and dismissing alibi testimony, and racist sentencing remarks.
    -   Flawed forensic science: Reliance on now-debunked "junk science" even though it excluded the accused, yet still proceeding with prosecution based on confessions.
    -   Parole system challenges: Racist parole officers and overly restrictive conditions hindering successful reintegration after wrongful imprisonment, even decades later.
    -   Slow and arduous process for exoneration: Lengthy delays and bureaucratic hurdles in the Canadian criminal conviction review process, causing prolonged suffering for wrongfully convicted individuals.

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:

    -   Improve access to effective legal representation for marginalized communities.
    -   Streamline and expedite the process for reviewing potential wrongful convictions.
    -   Educate the public about the causes and consequences of wrongful convictions.
