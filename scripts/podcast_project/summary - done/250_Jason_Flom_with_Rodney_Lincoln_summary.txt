### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features the story of Rodney Lincoln, who was wrongfully convicted of murder and sexual assault in 1983 and served over 36 years in prison.  The episode details the flawed investigation, including a suggestive lineup with a traumatized child witness, Melissa DeBore, and the use of dubious hair microscopy evidence.  Despite inconsistencies in Melissa's initial statements, her powerful testimony, combined with junk science, led to Lincoln's conviction.  Decades later, DNA evidence excluded Lincoln, and Melissa recanted her testimony after recognizing serial killer Tommy Lynn Sells as the likely perpetrator.  Despite these developments and daughter K. Lincoln's tireless advocacy, legal hurdles persisted until Governor Greitens commuted Lincoln's sentence in 2018.  The episode highlights systemic issues of eyewitness misidentification, junk science in court, and the immense personal impact of wrongful convictions, while celebrating the resilience of Rodney Lincoln and his daughter's fight for justice.
