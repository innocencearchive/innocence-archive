### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom" features the case of Robert Jean Will, who has been on death row in Texas for the murder of Deputy Barrett Hill in 2000. Despite physical evidence suggesting his innocence and pointing towards Michael Allen Rosario, the son of a Houston cop, as the actual perpetrator, Will was convicted. The episode highlights critical pieces of evidence suppressed at trial, including the lack of gunshot residue on Will's hand, the absence of Deputy Hill's blood on his clothing, dispatch records proving Rosario had ample time to commit the murder, and bloodhound tracking evidence.  It also details Rosario's confessions to multiple individuals and attempts to hire a hitman to silence Will. The podcast underscores systemic issues such as ineffective defense counsel, prosecutorial misconduct in concealing evidence, and the prejudicial atmosphere created by the "wall of blue" at Will's trial.  Despite these overwhelming issues, Will remains on death row, fighting for a new trial and maintaining his innocence while advocating for broader criminal justice reform.

-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    -   Implied Rob was the only possible culprit at the scene after Deputy Hill was murdered, despite timeline and physical evidence inconsistencies.
    -   Narrative suggesting Rosario did not have enough time to return to the scene and commit the murder based on a flawed interpretation of dispatch records.
    -   "Wall of blue" – presence of numerous uniformed deputies in court to influence the jury.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**:
    -   Ineffective assistance of counsel. Failed to adequately investigate and present crucial exculpatory evidence.
    -   Failed to properly analyze dispatch records and timeline.
    -   Did not present bloodhound tracking evidence or witness testimonies about Rosario's confessions and suspicious behavior.
    -   State habeas appeal lawyer was grossly incompetent, submitting a cut-and-paste brief from another case.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    -   **No gunshot residue on Rob's right hand or glove**: Proves he did not fire a gun.
    -   **No Deputy Hill's blood on Rob or his clothing**: Contradicts the close-range shooting scenario if Rob was the shooter.
    -   **Dispatch records analysis**: Shows Rosario had ample time (44 seconds to 1 minute 26 seconds) to return to the crime scene.
    -   **Bloodhound tracking evidence**: Traced Rosario's scent directly towards the crime scene, not east as the state initially suggested.
    -   **Confessions of Rosario**: Multiple witnesses signed affidavits stating Rosario confessed to the murder.
    -   **Natasha Haleyan's testimony**: Rosario admitted to washing blood off his clothes after the murder.
    -   **Rosario's lies to police**: Inconsistencies and denials about his presence, knowledge of Rob, and handling of the gun.
    -   **Concealed police records**: Evidence that Rosario attempted to hire a gang member (David Cruz) to kill Rob, the eyewitness. Deputy Strickland falsely denied having information about Rob's case.
    -   **Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals ruling (2020)**: Sent the case back to Judge Ellison, acknowledging "disturbing uncertainties" and "considerable evidence supporting Will's innocence," and stating it is "reasonably likely" a jury would have reasonable doubt with the new evidence.
