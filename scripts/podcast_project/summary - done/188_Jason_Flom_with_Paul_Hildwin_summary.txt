### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom" features the harrowing story of Paul Hildwin, who spent 35 years on death row in Florida for a murder he did not commit.  Wrongfully convicted in 1986 based on circumstantial evidence, junk science, and ineffective counsel, Hildwin's case highlights systemic failures in the justice system. Despite DNA evidence in 2003 proving his innocence and pointing to the victim's boyfriend, William Haverty, as the actual perpetrator, Hildwin's legal battle for freedom continued for 17 more years.  Defense attorney Leanne Gaudy, a guest on the podcast, details the legal fight and the egregious errors that led to Hildwin's wrongful conviction.  Hildwin shares his personal experiences of enduring death row, battling cancer, and finally tasting freedom after accepting a plea deal in 2020. The episode emphasizes the importance of organizations like the Innocence Project and the Sunny Center in supporting exonerees.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Paul Hildwin
-   **Case Location**: Hernando County, Florida, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1986
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2020 (released, not fully exonerated in legal terms, but functionally exonerated by DNA evidence)
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: First-degree murder
-   **Sentence**: Death penalty
-   **Time Served**: 35 years
-   **Contributing Factors**: Ineffective trial counsel, junk science (false serology evidence), misleading testimony by experts, prosecutorial misconduct (withholding evidence), tunnel vision in police investigation, reliance on circumstantial evidence.
-   **Legal Status**: Released after pleading no contest to second-degree murder with time served and lifetime probation. Functionally exonerated due to DNA evidence identifying the actual perpetrator.
-   **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project, Innocence Project of Florida, Capital Collateral Representative (CCRC), Sunny Center.
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**:
    -   **Leanne Gaudy**: Renowned defense attorney, took on Paul's case pro bono in 2014, ultimately secured his release. Elected as Judge to the 13th Circuit Court during the timeframe of the podcast.
    -   **Marty McLean**: Previous lawyer who investigated Paul's case and initially uncovered undisclosed evidence and challenged the conviction through a 3850 motion. Worked for CCRC.
    -   **Nina Morrison**: From the Innocence Project, initially involved in Paul's case and persuaded Leanne Gaudy to take it on.
-   **Cross-Reference**: Not specified if he appears in a specific exoneree database within the transcript.
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Franz Eddie Cox was found murdered, nude, and stuffed in the trunk of her car.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    -   Paul Hildwin cashed a stolen check from the victim's checkbook.
    -   Stolen items from the victim's car were found at Paul's residence.
    -   False serology evidence from an FBI expert claimed bodily fluids found on the victim's underwear and a washcloth matched Paul Hildwin and could not have come from the victim's boyfriend.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**:
    -   **Dan Lewin**: Inexperienced lawyer, recently graduated from law school, had never handled a murder case before. Appointed counsel, conducted no independent investigation, and was unprepared to challenge the prosecution's serology evidence effectively.  Objected to the serology evidence but was overruled and given insufficient time to prepare a defense against it.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    -   **DNA Testing (2003)**: Independent DNA testing revealed that the bodily fluids on the victim's underwear and washcloth did not belong to Paul Hildwin. The DNA profile matched William Haverty, the victim's live-in boyfriend.
    -   **Undisclosed Evidence**: Marty McLean discovered withheld police reports and witness statements, including a nephew who saw the victim alive after the time the prosecution alleged she was murdered.
    -   **Florida Supreme Court Ruling (2014)**: Based on the newly discovered DNA evidence, the Florida Supreme Court granted Paul Hildwin a new trial.
    -   **Plea Deal (2020)**: To ensure his release, Paul accepted a plea deal to second-degree murder with time served and lifetime probation.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**:
    -   **William Haverty**: Victim's live-in boyfriend, initially considered a suspect. DNA evidence from the crime scene matched his profile.  At the time of the podcast, he was incarcerated for 16 counts of sexual battery against his stepdaughter, highlighting a pattern of violent behavior.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   **Ineffective Assistance of Counsel**: Paul's original trial lawyer was inexperienced and unprepared, failing to investigate or challenge key prosecution evidence.
    -   **Junk Science**:  False serology evidence from an FBI expert played a crucial role in Paul's conviction.
    -   **Prosecutorial Misconduct/Brady Violations**: Withholding of exculpatory evidence, including witness statements and a threatening note from the boyfriend, undermined the fairness of the trial.
    -   **Tunnel Vision**: Law enforcement fixated on Paul Hildwin as the suspect after discovering he cashed a stolen check, ignoring other potential suspects and exculpatory evidence.
    -   **Lengthy Appeals Process**: Despite DNA evidence of innocence in 2003, it took 17 years for Paul to be released, illustrating the slow pace of justice in wrongful conviction cases.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Support local Innocence Projects and organizations like the Sunny Center through donations to aid exonerees and fight wrongful convictions.
    -   Implicit call for broader criminal justice reform to address issues like ineffective counsel, junk science in court, and prosecutorial accountability to prevent wrongful convictions.
