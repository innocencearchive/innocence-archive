### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction details the harrowing story of Carlton Roman, wrongfully convicted in 1990 for a murder he did not commit and subsequently exonerated in 2021 after serving 32 years. The case unfolded in Queens, New York, amidst the backdrop of the late 1980s drug epidemic and alleged police corruption. Roman's conviction hinged on the unreliable and changing testimonies of Paul Anderson and Jomo Kenyatta, and the questionable conduct of Detective Peppy, despite no physical evidence linking him to the crime.  His original defense was ineffective, failing to adequately challenge the prosecution's narrative.  Years of appeals were denied until a new District Attorney's office and conviction integrity unit reinvestigated, leading to Anderson's recantation and the eventual overturning of Roman's conviction.  The episode highlights systemic failures within the justice system, the importance of accountability, and the immense personal toll of wrongful convictions, as well as the challenges exonerees face upon release.
