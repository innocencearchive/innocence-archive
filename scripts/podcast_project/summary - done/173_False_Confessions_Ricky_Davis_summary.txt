### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction, False Confessions, hosted by Lauren Nyreiter and Steve Drizen, details the case of Ricky Davis, wrongfully convicted of the 1985 murder of Jane Hilton.  Despite initial evidence pointing away from Davis and his girlfriend Connie Dahl, the case went cold for 14 years.  Reopened in 1999, detectives interrogated Connie using deceptive tactics, leading to a coerced false confession implicating both herself and Davis.  Connie testified against Davis, resulting in his conviction and 12-year imprisonment.  Decades later, DNA evidence from the crime scene excluded Davis and Dahl, and pointed to Michael Green, one of the teenagers present in the area on the night of the murder. Davis was exonerated in 2020, while Connie, who died in 2014, remains wrongly implicated. The episode highlights the devastating consequences of false confessions and flawed interrogation techniques, impacting multiple lives including Davis, his mother Maureen, and Connie Dahl.

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    - Use of deceptive and coercive interrogation tactics by police.
    - The phenomenon of persuaded false confessions, where individuals come to doubt their own memories and accept police narratives.
    - Tunnel vision and confirmation bias in police investigations, leading to neglect of exculpatory evidence and focus on pre-conceived theories.
    - Potential disparities in the justice system based on socioeconomic status and access to resources.
- **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**: Implicitly calls for reform in police interrogation practices, advocating for techniques that minimize the risk of false confessions.  Raises awareness about the fallibility of memory and the impact of trauma and manipulation on confessions.
