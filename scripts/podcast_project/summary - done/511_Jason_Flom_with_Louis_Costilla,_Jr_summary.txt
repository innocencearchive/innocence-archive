### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode details the wrongful conviction of five young men in Toledo, Ohio – Louis Castilla, Joseph Ricard, Eric Mish, and Ismael and Lorenzo Vasquez – for the 1992 murder of Vernon Huggins. Initially investigated with leads pointing towards the victim's girlfriend's family, the case was reassigned, and detectives pursued an unsubstantiated theory involving a non-existent white supremacist gang. Based on coerced statements from unreliable witnesses (including a minor, Eric Mish, who immediately recanted) and ignoring contradictory evidence and a lack of physical proof, all five were convicted or took Alford pleas. Decades later, post-conviction efforts led by attorney Joanna Sanchez uncovered hidden police files from the original investigation and secured DNA testing, which excluded the five men and pointed to another suspect. These revelations ultimately led to the vacating of all five convictions, highlighting severe police misconduct, Brady violations, and the devastating personal cost of wrongful imprisonment.

**(1) Louis Castilla**

-   **Name**: Louis Castilla
-   **Case Location**: Toledo, Ohio, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1993
-   **Exoneration Year**: Conviction Vacated / Charges Dismissed (Nolle Prosequi) ~2022-2023 (Order signed after prosecution dropped appeal of new trial decision).
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder (Initially), Aggravated Murder, Aggravated Robbery (Re-indicted)
-   **Sentence**: 15 years to life (Original Murder conviction)
-   **Time Served**: Approx. 27 years (Convicted 1993, Paroled April 2020)
-   **Contributing Factors**: Police Misconduct (feeding info to witnesses, ignoring leads, hiding evidence), Prosecutorial Misconduct (hiding Berks' files/Brady violation), Coerced/False Witness Statements (Michelle Parker, Joey Hall, John Urbina), False Confession (Eric Mish's coerced statement used against group), Tunnel Vision, Lack of Physical Evidence.
-   **Legal Status**: Conviction Vacated, Charges Dismissed. Initially remained on parole supervision after dismissal, which was later terminated.
-   **Organizations Involved**: Ohio Public Defender's Office (Wrongful Conviction Project - primarily initiated by Eric Mish's case).
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Don Malarcik (Post-conviction attorney for Louis Castilla, Partner with Ohio Defense Firm). Joanna Sanchez (Ohio Public Defender's Office, led effort for Eric Mish, uncovered hidden files, fought for DNA testing, secured new trial order).
-   **Cross-Reference**: Unknown based on transcript.
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Vernon Huggins, 31, was found beaten to death in Woodrow Wilson Park on the north side of Toledo, OH, on the morning of August 4, 1992.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**: Testimony of Michelle Parker (claiming presence at Urbina's house when group bragged about beating a Black man, inconsistent dates/times provided, later recanted citing police threats). Testimony of Joey Hall (claiming to witness the beating, inconsistent statements, couldn't identify crime scene location at trial). Eric Mish's coerced/recanted confession (used implicitly against others). A wooden club presented by Rosemary Nell (allegedly from CJ Ricard), noted as *not* the murder weapon but an *example* of what *might* have been used. Explicitly noted *lack* of any physical evidence linking the accused to the crime.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Castilla felt his attorney presented little defense, relying on the belief that the state hadn't proven its case. Attorneys were described as "fighting with two hands tied behind their back" due to the hidden exculpatory evidence (Berks' files).
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**: Discovery (in 2018) of original Detective Berks' 114 pages of hidden investigation files from Aug-Dec 1992, detailing alternate suspects (victim's girlfriend Emma's family, motive related to violent relationship), victim seen alive later than state's timeline (after 3 AM on East Side), confrontation with different suspects (4 Black males w/ chair leg, gray station wagon). Recantation affidavit (2016) from Michelle Parker detailing police threats and coercion. DNA testing results from a bloody bottle found near the victim excluded the five convicted men and identified an unknown local male with a felony record who knew the victim. Eric Mish's successful motion for a new trial based on this new evidence. Prosecution ultimately dropping appeal and filing motion to Nolle Prosequi (dismiss) charges for all five.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Unknown. Original investigation (Berks) pointed strongly toward the victim's girlfriend's (Emma's) brothers/family due to a volatile relationship, recent violent altercations, and threats made against Huggins shortly before his death. One brother was reportedly seen with a club-like weapon. Separately, DNA from the crime scene bottle matched a different local man with a felony record who knew Huggins. The actual killer(s) remain unidentified, and the prosecution showed no motivation to reinvestigate after vacating the convictions.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    *   Police Misconduct (Coercion of witnesses, feeding information, threats, ignoring viable leads, potential evidence planting/manipulation - e.g., suggesting the Nova).
    *   Prosecutorial Misconduct (Brady violations - hiding exculpatory evidence from Detective Berks' initial investigation).
    *   Coerced False Confessions (Especially from minors like 16-year-old Eric Mish).
    *   Tunnel Vision (Focusing solely on the unsupported white supremacist gang theory).
    *   Reliance on Unreliable Witnesses/Informants (Individuals with credibility issues like Urbina, Hall, Parker, possibly motivated by deals or avoiding charges).
    *   Lack of Accountability (Detectives/prosecutors facing no apparent consequences for misconduct).
    *   Systemic Self-Protection (Justice system prioritizing upholding convictions over correcting errors, fighting exoneration efforts).
    *   Difficulties with Re-entry (Finding jobs, dealing with trauma/PTSD, reconnecting with family).
    *   Post-Exoneration Bureaucratic Issues (Remaining under parole supervision even after conviction vacated).
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    *   Implicit calls for greater police and prosecutor accountability.
    *   Need for proper procedures during interrogations, especially with minors, to prevent coercion.
    *   Importance of open-file discovery laws to prevent Brady violations.
    *   Urging those within the system (judges, prosecutors, police) to act with integrity and prioritize truth over conviction rates ("make the right fucking decisions").
    *   A call for people to speak out against injustice when they see it ("Raise your voice, say something when you see something").
-   **Personal Narrative/Impact**: Louis Castilla described a difficult youth, running the streets but denying involvement in violence or supremacist groups. He felt targeted by police due to his name. He expressed shock and disbelief upon arrest, initially trusting the system. Prison involved adapting to survive, losing appeals, dealing with the parole board repeatedly ("flops"). He highlighted the crucial emotional support he received from a woman named Fabiola for 25 years, and later from his current wife (whom he initially met as a CO). Re-entry involved struggles finding work, dealing with PTSD-like symptoms (noise sensitivity, needing to confront uncomfortable situations), and rebuilding relationships with his children. He expressed immense frustration with the injustice, the decades lost, and the lack of accountability for the officials involved, but also gratitude for the support system that helped him survive and eventually gain freedom. His final message was one of hope for others wrongly convicted and underscored the far-reaching impact of wrongful convictions on families.
