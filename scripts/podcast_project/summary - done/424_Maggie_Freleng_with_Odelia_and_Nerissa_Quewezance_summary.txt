### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Maggie Freeling" tells the story of sisters Odilia and Narissa Qzonz, two Indigenous women wrongfully convicted of second-degree murder in Canada in 1994.  The sisters, victims of systemic racism and the residential school system, were interrogated for days without counsel and coerced into unrecorded confessions. Despite a confession from their cousin Jason Kishane, who admitted to being the sole perpetrator and testifying to their innocence at trial, they were convicted by an all-white jury.  They served nearly 30 years before being released on bail in 2023, awaiting ministerial review for potential exoneration. The podcast highlights the systemic issues faced by Indigenous people in the Canadian justice system, including false confessions, racism within the RCMP and parole system, and the lasting trauma of residential schools. The case underscores the urgent need for systemic reform and reconciliation.

    -   Legal aid and culturally competent defense for Indigenous defendants.
    -   Parole system reform to address bias and improve reintegration for Indigenous individuals.
