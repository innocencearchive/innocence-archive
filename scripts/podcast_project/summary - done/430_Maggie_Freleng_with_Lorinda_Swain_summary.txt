### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Maggie Freeling" tells the harrowing story of Lorenda Swain, a single mother wrongfully convicted of first-degree criminal sexual conduct against her son, Ronnie.  In 2002, based solely on Ronnie's initial accusation, Lorenda was sentenced to 25 to 50 years in prison. Despite Ronnie's multiple recantations, her appeals were unsuccessful until the Michigan Innocence Clinic took her case.  The clinic uncovered crucial evidence from a neighbor boy, a school bus driver, and Lorenda's ex-boyfriend, Dennis Book, proving the allegations were fabricated, influenced by Ronnie's stepmother.  After years of legal battles and a retrial ordered by the Michigan Supreme Court, the prosecution finally dropped all charges in 2016, exonerating Lorenda after she served years in prison for a crime she never committed.  Her story highlights the devastating impact of false accusations and the importance of organizations like Innocence Clinics.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Reliance on Child Testimony: The case highlights the dangers of relying solely on child testimony, especially in emotionally charged situations, without thorough investigation and corroboration.
    -   Ineffective Defense Counsel:  Lorenda's initial lawyer's failure to investigate and present readily available exculpatory evidence significantly contributed to her wrongful conviction.
    -   Prosecutorial Overzealousness: The prosecutor continued to fight against Lorenda's exoneration for years, even after significant evidence of her innocence emerged, demonstrating potential prosecutorial bias or unwillingness to admit error.
    -   Brady Violation: The withholding of Dennis Book’s exculpatory statement by Detective Pickett represents a serious breach of legal and ethical obligations.
