### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features Alan Newton, who recounts his harrowing 22-year ordeal of wrongful imprisonment for rape in the Bronx, starting in 1984.  Despite a solid alibi and lack of concrete evidence, Newton was convicted based on shaky eyewitness identifications from the victim and a store clerk, with serological evidence being ignored or not presented at trial. The jury delivered a confusing verdict, convicting him for assault in an abandoned building but not for a prior assault in a park, despite the victim stating it was the same assailant.  For years, his appeals for DNA testing were denied, with authorities claiming the rape kit was lost.  However, the Innocence Project intervened, and the kit was found exactly where it was supposed to be, leading to DNA testing that definitively exonerated Newton in 2006.  Newton’s story underscores systemic failures, the devastating impact of wrongful convictions, and his ongoing advocacy for justice and other exonerees.
