### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features the story of Marvin Anderson, wrongfully convicted of rape, sodomy, robbery, and abduction in 1982 in Hanover County, Virginia.  Despite having an alibi and community knowledge of the actual perpetrator, Otis Lincoln, Anderson was convicted based on a suggestive photo array and live lineup identification by the victim. He was sentenced to an astounding 210 years.  His trial was further compromised by ineffective counsel with a conflict of interest.  Although Lincoln confessed in 1988, it was dismissed.  Marvin Anderson was finally exonerated in 2001 through DNA testing of evidence preserved by a forensic analyst, which identified Lincoln as the perpetrator.  The episode underscores issues of racial bias, eyewitness misidentification, and the long fight for justice for the wrongfully convicted.

    - Lack of accountability for misconduct in wrongful conviction cases.
    - Initial dismissal of confessions by actual perpetrators without forensic corroboration.
