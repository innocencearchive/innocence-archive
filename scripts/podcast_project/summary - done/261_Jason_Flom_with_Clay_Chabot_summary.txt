### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of the Wrongful Conviction podcast features the story of Clay Chabot, who was wrongfully convicted of rape and murder in Garland, Texas in 1986. Despite substantial evidence pointing towards his brother-in-law, Jerry Pabs, including bloody clothing and pawned stolen items, police fixated on Clay, a biker with a prior drug dealing background.  Using Pabs as a star witness, and ignoring inconsistent statements and physical evidence, Clay was convicted and sentenced to life.  After serving 23 years, DNA testing proved Pabs' semen was in the rape kit and his DNA on the victim's hands and fingernails, leading to Clay's exoneration.  However, in a shocking turn, the state attempted to re-prosecute Clay, forcing him to take a plea deal for time served to secure his release, leaving the wrongful conviction on his record. The episode highlights profound failures within the justice system, including tunnel vision in investigations, reliance on biased witnesses, and the immense personal toll of wrongful convictions.
