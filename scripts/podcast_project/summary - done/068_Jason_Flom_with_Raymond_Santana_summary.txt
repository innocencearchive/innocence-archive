### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom" features Raymond Santana, one of the wrongfully convicted Central Park Five. Santana recounts his arrest at age 14, the coercive interrogation tactics leading to his false confession, and the subsequent trial and conviction despite lack of physical evidence. He describes the immense public pressure and racial prejudice surrounding the case, fueled by media sensationalism and figures like Donald Trump. Santana details his seven years of incarceration, the challenges faced post-release including difficulty finding employment and PTSD, and his eventual exoneration after Matthias Reyes, the actual perpetrator, confessed while in prison. The episode underscores the systemic issues within the criminal justice system that contribute to wrongful convictions, such as police coercion, false confessions, prosecutorial misconduct, and racial bias. Santana's story highlights the devastating personal impact of wrongful conviction and his journey toward healing and advocacy.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Police coercion and manipulative interrogation tactics (e.g., Reid method, good cop/bad cop).
    -   False confessions, especially from juveniles under duress.
    -   Racial bias in the justice system and media portrayal.
    -   Ineffective defense counsel (divorce lawyer).
    -   Prosecutorial misconduct (presenting false evidence, ignoring exculpatory evidence).
    -   Media sensationalism and trial by media.
    -   Public pressure on law enforcement to quickly solve high-profile cases.

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Support for the Innocence Project (Jason Flom encourages donations and involvement).
    -   Implied need for interrogation reform to prevent false confessions, especially for juveniles.
    -   Need for greater scrutiny of prosecutorial conduct and accountability for misconduct.
    -   Need for public awareness and education about wrongful convictions and systemic issues in the justice system.
