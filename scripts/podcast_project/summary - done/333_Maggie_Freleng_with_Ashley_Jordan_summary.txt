### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: Ashley Jordan recounts the horrific experience of being wrongfully convicted, along with her husband Albert, for the murder of their infant daughter Mackenzie.  Days after bringing Mackenzie home, she tragically passed away, leading to immediate suspicion from law enforcement. Despite a lack of evidence and maintaining their innocence, Ashley and Albert were interrogated, arrested, and ultimately convicted based on flawed medical testimony claiming blunt force trauma and the unreliable testimony of a jailhouse informant.  The podcast details the devastating trial, the misrepresentation of 'reasonable doubt' by the prosecution, and the subsequent years of wrongful imprisonment.  After serving over twelve years, their convictions were finally vacated due to ineffective assistance of counsel, highlighting the critical failures within the legal system and Ashley's journey towards healing and advocacy post-exoneration.

-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Ashley and Albert Doublebot's infant daughter, Mackenzie, died suddenly a few days after birth.  After noticing a lump on her head and breathing difficulties, they rushed her to the hospital where she passed away.  Medical professionals initially suspected foul play due to perceived injuries.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    -   Testimony of Dr. Laura Daris (medical examiner) stating Mackenzie died from blunt force trauma to the head with multiple skull fractures, concluding homicide.
    -   Testimony of jailhouse informant Melvin Tarver, claiming Albert confessed that Ashley harmed Mackenzie.
    -   Prosecution's portrayal of Ashley as emotionally cold and lacking appropriate grief.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**:
    -   **Ashley's Defense (Sandy Callahan)**: Initially intended to challenge medical evidence with expert John Plunkett on shaken baby syndrome but was hindered by lack of access to prosecution's medical evidence and denial of continuance. Ultimately presented a weak defense.
    -   **Albert's Defense (Bill Mason)**: Strategy was not to challenge the medical evidence presented by the prosecution.
    -   Both defense teams failed to object to significant misrepresentations and inaccuracies by the prosecution during closing arguments, including the mischaracterization of "reasonable doubt."
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    -   New medical expert testimonies presented in 2015 during post-conviction hearings, suggesting Mackenzie's injuries were likely pre-natal or occurred during birth, contradicting the original blunt force trauma diagnosis.
    -   Georgia Supreme Court vacated convictions in 2020 based on ineffective assistance of counsel, specifically the failure of trial counsel to adequately challenge the state's medical evidence.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Not applicable. The case was a wrongful conviction; no other perpetrator was identified.  The likely cause of Mackenzie's death was natural or due to pre-existing conditions, not homicide.

    -   **Junk Science in Court**: Reliance on initially flawed medical interpretations of infant injuries as definitive proof of abuse.
    -   **Ineffective Assistance of Counsel**: Deficient legal representation that failed to challenge key prosecution evidence, hindering a fair trial.
    -   **Prosecutorial Misconduct**: Misleading statements about the burden of proof and reasonable doubt, creating an unfair advantage for the prosecution.
    -   **Unreliable Jailhouse Informants**: Use of testimony from individuals seeking leniency, lacking credibility and potentially fabricating evidence.
    -   **Impact of Racial Bias**: Suggestion of racial undertones in the prosecution's strategy to portray Ashley negatively.
    -   **Voting Rights Disenfranchisement**:  Ex-offenders in Georgia unable to vote, even after completing sentences and paying taxes, highlighting systemic civic exclusion.
