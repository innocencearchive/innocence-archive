### Executive Summary

This episode of Wrongful Conviction details the story of Jermaine Archer, wrongfully convicted for a 1997 murder in Brooklyn, New York. The conviction heavily relied on the shifting testimony of Carlos Bethune, the driver of the car in which the victim, Patrick Niles, was shot. Despite Bethune initially stating he didn't see the shooter and physical evidence contradicting his later account implicating Archer, Jermaine was convicted, fueled partly by neighborhood tensions and alleged pressure from the victim's vengeful brother, Ronaldo. Archer served 22 years, during which he pursued education, co-founded the "Voices from Within" program, and participated in "Rehabilitation Through the Arts." His conviction was ultimately overturned in 2023 following a hearing that proved the prosecution committed a Brady violation by failing to disclose payments and benefits given to Bethune for his testimony, which both Bethune and the ADA denied at trial. Jermaine is now paroled but continues to fight for full exoneration.
