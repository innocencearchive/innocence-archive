### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features Leon Benson, who was wrongfully convicted of murder in Indianapolis in 1999 and served 25 years in prison before his exoneration in 2023. The episode details the events leading to his arrest, focusing on the unreliable eyewitness identification by Christie Schmidt, and the failure of his defense attorney.  It highlights prosecutorial misconduct during the second trial, where the prosecutor introduced extraneous and prejudicial information.  Laura Basilan, from the University of San Francisco School of Law, explains how her team, along with the Marion County Prosecutor's Conviction Integrity Unit, uncovered suppressed evidence, specifically police files pointing to another suspect, Joseph Webster. This suppressed evidence was crucial in vacating Leon's conviction. Leon shares his personal experience of incarceration, his resilience, and his advocacy for justice and at-risk youth since his release. The episode also touches upon the potential homophobic context of the original crime, emphasizing the victim Casey Shane's story.

A newspaper delivery woman, Christie Schmidt, witnessed the shooting from a distance. The podcast suggests Casey Shane was likely in the area after visiting a gay bar and may have been meeting someone when he was murdered, implying a possible hate crime element, although the motive was not definitively established in the transcript.
- **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**: Eyewitness testimony from Christie Schmidt who identified Leon Benson in a photo array and live lineup and again at trial as the shooter.  Circumstantial evidence of Leon being in the area, though his presence at the scene of the crime was disputed by him and alibi witnesses.
- **Trial Defender Notes**: Original defense lawyer, Timothy Miller, failed to adequately challenge the eyewitness identification, did not call key alibi witness Timothy Gaither in the second trial, and crucially, did not call Decaria Fulton, who identified Joseph Webster as the shooter. Miller also did not object to significant prosecutorial misconduct during the second trial.
- **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:  Undisclosed police files revealed that multiple crime stopper tips and a detective's note pointed to Joseph Webster as the shooter.  Detective Ellen Jones admitted to withholding this exculpatory evidence, claiming it was "work product," which constituted a Brady violation (withholding of exculpatory evidence).
- **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Joseph Webster was identified by Decaria Fulton as the shooter and was named in multiple crime stopper tips and police notes as the perpetrator.  While not explicitly stated he was the actual perpetrator in the podcast, the evidence strongly suggests this and was the basis for exonerating Leon Benson due to the suppressed evidence pointing to Webster.
