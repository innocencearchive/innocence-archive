### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features the story of Kiara Newsom, wrongfully convicted for a gang-related murder she did not commit.  Kiara recounts her challenging upbringing in South Central LA, the tragic murder of her boyfriend, and the subsequent events leading to her wrongful conviction. Despite a solid alibi of being in a lockdown school classroom at the time of the crime, coerced eyewitness testimony and gang-related biases led to her conviction and a 60-years-to-life sentence.  The episode details the efforts of the California Innocence Project and the Juvenile Innocence and Fair Sentencing Clinic at Loyola Law School, particularly Chris Hawthorne, in securing her commutation after nearly 19 years of incarceration.  Kiara and Chris discuss the systemic issues contributing to wrongful convictions, including gang evidence, witness intimidation, and ineffective legal defense. The episode concludes with a call to action to support justice reform and organizations like the Juvenile Innocence and Fair Sentencing Clinic.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Racial bias in the justice system, particularly through the use of gang evidence.
    -   Coerced and unreliable eyewitness testimony, especially in gang-related cases.
    -   Ineffective investigation by police, focusing on a suspect early without thoroughly checking alibis.
    -   Challenges in proving innocence in habeas corpus petitions.
    -   Over-sentencing of juveniles.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Support conviction integrity units within District Attorney's offices to review wrongful conviction cases.
    -   Reform the use of gang evidence in trials to prevent racial bias.
    -   Advocate for the exoneration of Kiara Newsom.
    -   Support organizations like the Juvenile Innocence and Fair Sentencing Clinic and the Innocence Project.
    -   Encourage jury duty participants to carefully consider all evidence, especially in cases involving gang affiliations and witness testimonies.
