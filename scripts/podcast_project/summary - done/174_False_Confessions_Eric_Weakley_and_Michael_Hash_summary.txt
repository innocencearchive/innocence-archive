### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction, False Confessions, hosted by Lauren Nyreiter and Steve Drizen, tells the story of Eric Weekly and Michael Hash, childhood friends in Culpepper County, Virginia, who were wrongly accused of the 1996 murder of Thelma Scroggins.  Police coerced a false confession from Eric Weekly, who then implicated Michael Hash. Despite a lack of evidence and Jason Klobe, a third accused individual, being found not guilty due to an alibi, Michael Hash was convicted based on Eric’s coerced testimony and false testimony from a jailhouse informant.  Michael's mother, Pam Hash, tirelessly fought for his freedom, uncovering the truth about a deal with the jailhouse informant and eventually leading to Eric Weekly recanting his false confession. After nearly 12 years, Michael Hash was exonerated, highlighting the devastating impact of false confessions, prosecutorial misconduct, and the unwavering dedication of a mother to her son's innocence. The episode also calls for a pardon for Eric Weekly, who remains convicted of second-degree murder despite his innocence and recantation.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   False confessions due to coercive interrogation tactics (prolonged interrogations, minimization, maximization, false promises).
    -   Use of jailhouse informants and prosecutorial misconduct (withholding deals with informants, lying to the jury).
    -   Ineffective assistance of counsel (perhaps in Eric Weekly's case, leading to guilty plea).
    -   Tunnel vision in investigations – focusing on initial suspects despite lack of evidence and exculpatory information (Jason Klobe's alibi).
    -   Difficulty in recanting confessions and being believed by the courts.
    -   Impact of wrongful conviction on exonerees and those who falsely confessed and remain convicted.

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Support Eric Weekly's pardon petition to the governor of Virginia.
    -   Raise awareness about coercive interrogation tactics and false confessions.
    -   Reform interrogation practices to prevent false confessions (e.g., mandatory recording of entire interrogations, limiting interrogation length, prohibiting deceptive tactics).
    -   Address the issue of jailhouse informant testimony and implement safeguards to ensure reliability and transparency.
    -   Advocate for compensation and support services for exonerees and those wrongly convicted.
