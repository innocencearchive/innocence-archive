### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features Pete Williams, who was wrongfully convicted of rape in 1985 and served 22 years in prison before being exonerated by DNA evidence in 2007.  The episode details the circumstances leading to his wrongful conviction, highlighting eyewitness misidentification and a flawed composite sketch.  It also introduces Drew Findling, now president of the National Association of Criminal Defense Lawyers, who was a young public defender during Pete's trial and was deeply impacted by the injustice.  The discussion also touches upon the role of unethical prosecutors, systemic issues within the justice system, and the importance of criminal justice reform. The case is used to illustrate the devastating consequences of wrongful convictions and the need for systemic change, including ethical prosecution, proper evidence preservation, and informed voting in local prosecutor elections. Pete and Drew emphasize the personal impact of wrongful convictions and the broader implications for justice and public safety.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Pete Williams
-   **Case Location**: Atlanta, Georgia, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1985
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2007
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Rape, aggravated assault, aggravated sodomy (initially one rape charge, later expanded to two)
-   **Sentence**: 45 years to life
-   **Time Served**: Nearly 22 years
-   **Contributing Factors**:
    -   Eyewitness misidentification (by two victims based on a composite sketch)
    -   Resemblance to a composite sketch of the actual perpetrator
    -   Lack of ethical prosecution and investigation
-   **Legal Status**: Exonerated
-   **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project, Georgia Innocence Project
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Not explicitly detailed in the transcript, but the Innocence Project and Georgia Innocence Project are mentioned as assisting with his case and DNA testing. Drew Findling, while not his lawyer for exoneration, was a witness to the trial and was deeply affected by the case.
-   **Cross-Reference**: Not specified in the transcript if he appears in a specific exoneree database.
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Two rapes occurred in April 1985 in parking lots north of Atlanta. The perpetrator approached women asking for "Carol" before forcing them into their cars and assaulting them. Pete Williams was arrested because he resembled a composite sketch created based on the first victim's description. He was wrongly identified by two victims as the perpetrator of the first two attacks.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    -   Eyewitness identification by two victims.
    -   Pete Williams resembled the composite sketch.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Not detailed in the transcript, but implied to be inadequate as per Drew Findling's observations and the wrongful conviction itself.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    -   DNA evidence from the original rape kit, which was preserved by the Georgia Bureau of Investigation (GBI).
    -   DNA testing excluded Pete Williams and matched Kenneth Wicker, who had already pleaded guilty to three subsequent similar attacks.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**:
    -   **Name**: Kenneth Wicker
    -   Responsible for all five attacks along Roswell Road.
    -   Arrested after his last victim took down his license plate number.
    -   Pled guilty to three attacks, but not initially charged for the first two attacks that Pete Williams was convicted of.
    -   After Pete's exoneration, Kenneth Wicker was arrested on February 9, 2007, for the April 5th and 10th incidents (the ones Pete was wrongly convicted for).
