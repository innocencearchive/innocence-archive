### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction: False Confessions" tells the story of Peter Riley, a teenager wrongfully convicted of murdering his mother in 1973 due to a false confession coerced by police interrogation tactics.  Despite no physical evidence and community support, Peter was convicted based on his confession.  The case gained national attention through figures like Arthur Miller, who helped fund his defense and raise public awareness about false confessions.  After years of appeals and the discovery of exculpatory evidence, Peter was exonerated in 1976.  His case became a landmark example of the dangers of false confessions and contributed to the movement for interrogation reform, including mandatory recording of interrogations. Peter Riley's story highlights the human tragedy of wrongful convictions and the importance of fighting for justice and systemic change.

    -   Steve Drizen: "Peter Riley's case was my baptism in the world of false confessions. -   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Coercive police interrogation tactics leading to false confessions.
    -   Belief in polygraph infallibility influencing suspect's self-doubt.
    -   Failure to record interrogations (at the time).
    -   Prosecutorial misconduct (withholding exculpatory evidence).
    -   System's unwillingness to admit error.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Mandatory electronic recording of interrogations.
    -   Reform of police interrogation techniques to avoid coercion and false confessions.
    -   Establishment and support of Conviction Integrity Units in prosecutor's offices.
