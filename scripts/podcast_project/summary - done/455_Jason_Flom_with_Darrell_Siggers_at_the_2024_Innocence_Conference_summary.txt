### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features Daryl Singers, who spent 34 years in prison for a murder he did not commit in Detroit in 1984.  The episode details the flawed investigation and trial that led to his wrongful conviction, highlighting issues such as unreliable eyewitness testimony from survivors of the shooting, fabricated ballistics evidence presented by a police sergeant falsely claiming expertise, and coercive interrogation tactics that led to Daryl jumping out of a window to avoid confessing.  The podcast emphasizes the dehumanizing conditions of Wayne County Jail and the inadequate legal representation Daryl initially received.  It further describes Daryl's decades-long fight for freedom, his self-education in law within prison, and the crucial role of the University of Michigan law students and the Wayne County Conviction Integrity Unit in uncovering the truth.  Ultimately, Daryl was exonerated in 2018, showcasing the systemic failures within the justice system and the importance of organizations dedicated to rectifying wrongful convictions.  The episode concludes with reflections on the lasting impact of wrongful imprisonment and the need for police accountability and judicial reform.
