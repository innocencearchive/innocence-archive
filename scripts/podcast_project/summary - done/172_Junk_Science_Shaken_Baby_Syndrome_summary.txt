### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction: Junk Science" explores the problematic use of "Shaken Baby Syndrome" (SBS) diagnosis in the justice system, leading to wrongful convictions.  The podcast uses the story of Stephanie Spurgeon, who was wrongly convicted based on SBS evidence and later exonerated, as a central example.  It details the origins of the SBS hypothesis, its reliance on the "classic triad" of symptoms, and how these symptoms can be caused by conditions other than abuse.  The episode features an interview with lawyer Kate Judson, who discusses the lack of scientific rigor behind SBS diagnoses used in court and the biases inherent in the system.  Host Josh Dubin argues that SBS has become "junk science" and highlights the devastating impact of wrongful convictions on individuals and the justice system's integrity. The podcast calls for more rigorous medical testing in child death cases and encourages listeners to advocate for justice reform.

    -   Over-reliance on "Shaken Baby Syndrome" diagnosis as definitive proof of abuse in legal settings.
