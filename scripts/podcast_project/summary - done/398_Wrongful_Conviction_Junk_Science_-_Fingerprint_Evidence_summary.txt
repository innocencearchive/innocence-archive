### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction: Junk Science, hosted by Kate Judson and Josh Dubin, examines the fallibility of fingerprint evidence, a forensic method often perceived as infallible by the public.  The episode highlights the subjective nature of fingerprint analysis through the ACE-V methodology, emphasizing issues with analysis, comparison, evaluation, and verification.  The hosts discuss the historical context of fingerprint evidence in courts, starting with the Thomas Jennings case in 1910, and present the case of Brandon Mayfield, a victim of fingerprint misidentification after the Madrid bombings.  Mary Moriarty, Chief Public Defender, provides expert commentary, explaining the lack of scientific rigor and the influence of popular culture on the perception of fingerprint evidence. The episode concludes with a call to action, urging listeners to educate judges and policymakers about the limitations of fingerprint analysis to prevent wrongful convictions.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Over-reliance on fingerprint evidence due to public perception and popular culture influence.
    -   Subjectivity and lack of scientific validation in fingerprint analysis methods (ACE-V).
    -   Confirmation bias in verification processes within fingerprint analysis.
    -   Courts' deference to precedent and slow adoption of scientific advancements regarding forensic limitations.
    -   Lack of documentation and replicability in fingerprint analysis processes.
    -   Non-scientific backgrounds of many fingerprint examiners historically.

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:

    -   Encourage public awareness and action to pressure for change in the criminal justice system regarding junk science.
