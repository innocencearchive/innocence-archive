### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom details the harrowing story of Jared Adams and Demetri Henley, two men wrongfully convicted of sexual assault in 1998.  Along with a third man, Rovon Hill, they were accused after a consensual encounter was misconstrued following a roommate's intervention.  The podcast highlights the profound racial bias that permeated their case, leading to convictions despite a lack of force or threats, and the accuser's own inconsistent statements.  Shockingly, the three men faced vastly different legal outcomes: Rovon's charges were dismissed, Jared was eventually exonerated after nearly a decade, and Demetri remains convicted, even though all three were involved in the same incident with identical evidence.  The episode underscores systemic failures, ineffective legal counsel, and the devastating and lasting impacts of wrongful convictions, particularly on individuals of color. It also showcases the crucial work of innocence projects and the ongoing fight for justice and reform.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Racial bias in the criminal justice system.
    -   Ineffective assistance of counsel for indigent defendants.
    -   Procedural barriers to post-conviction relief (AEDPA, limitations on state court authority).
    -   Lack of accountability for prosecutors or police misconduct (withholding evidence).
    -   Disparate outcomes in the justice system even with identical facts and evidence.
    -   Lack of compensation for wrongfully convicted individuals.
    -   Stigma and ongoing consequences for those convicted, even after release (sex offender registry).

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Systemic reform of the criminal justice system to address racial bias.
    -   Improved standards and oversight for defense counsel, especially in indigent cases.
    -   Reform of AEDPA and other procedural barriers to post-conviction relief.
    -   Accountability for prosecutorial and police misconduct, including withholding evidence.
    -   Compensation for wrongfully convicted individuals.
    -   Support for organizations like Innocence Projects and Center for Integrity in Forensic Sciences.
    -   Legislative action to address unjust laws and court decisions (like the Wisconsin Supreme Court decision in Demetri's case).
