### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom details the case of Kevin Dykes, wrongfully convicted in 1986 for murder and attempted murder in Compton, California. Dykes, already vulnerable due to a past accidental manslaughter and a recent injury leaving him in a wheelchair, was caught between drug dealers Slim and Hondo and a series of violent events. Despite being an eyewitness and initially cooperating with police, Dykes was convicted based solely on the testimony of three jailhouse snitches, all of whom had incentives to lie.  The podcast features interviews with Dykes and his attorney, Stephen K. Houser, highlighting the lack of physical evidence, recanted snitch testimonies, and the ongoing struggle for exoneration despite legal changes and new district attorneys.  Dykes' case exemplifies the dangers of jailhouse snitch testimony and the systemic injustices within the legal system.  Despite decades of wrongful imprisonment, Kevin Dykes expresses resilience and hope for eventual justice and parole.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    - **Jailhouse Snitch System**: The podcast heavily emphasizes the systemic problem of using incentivized jailhouse snitch testimony, which is shown to be unreliable and prone to fabrication.
    - **Lack of Retroactivity in Law Reform**:  The new law requiring corroboration of snitch testimony is not retroactive, failing to help individuals like Kevin Dykes who were convicted before the law changed.
    - **Prosecutorial Reluctance to Admit Error**:  Multiple district attorneys were presented with evidence of innocence and recanted snitch testimony, yet the office remained resistant to overturning the conviction, citing Kevin's initial statement as proof of guilt despite lack of intent.
    - **Inadequate Legal Standard at the Time**:  The legal system in 1986 allowed for convictions based solely on uncorroborated snitch testimony, leading to wrongful convictions like Kevin's.
