### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Maggie Freeling" tells the story of Butch Martin, who was wrongfully convicted of arson and the murder of his girlfriend and her two children in 1998.  The prosecution argued Butch intentionally set fire to his home due to relationship issues, using shifting forensic testimony and questionable arson science.  Despite a rigorous defense, Butch was convicted and sentenced to life.  The Innocence Project of Texas took on his case in 2017, highlighting flaws in the arson investigation and the discredited testimony of the original pathologist. New evidence and re-evaluation of the forensic science led to the potential for his exoneration, showcasing systemic issues within the justice system and the devastating impact of wrongful convictions.  Butch's story emphasizes the importance of reliable forensic science and the fight for justice for the wrongfully convicted.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Garland "Butch" Leon Martin
-   **Case Location**: Midland, Texas, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1999
-   **Exoneration Year**: Not yet exonerated as of the podcast episode, but a new hearing was held in May 2022 with strong indications of potential exoneration.
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Capital Murder (three counts) and Arson
-   **Sentence**: Three concurrent life sentences
-   **Time Served**: Approximately 24 years (as of the podcast release, incarcerated since August 1999)
-   **Contributing Factors**:
    - Faulty forensic science (arson investigation and interpretation of burn patterns, misidentification of accelerants).
    - Shifting and unreliable forensic pathology testimony (original pathologist changing cause of death findings).
    - Prosecutorial focus on a narrative of guilt, potentially overlooking exculpatory evidence.
-   **Legal Status**:  Still incarcerated at the time of the podcast, but with a pending legal process for potential exoneration based on new evidence presented at a hearing in May 2022.
-   **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project of Texas
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Allison Clayton, Deputy Director of the Innocence Project of Texas and Adjunct Professor of the Innocence Clinic at Texas Tech University School of Law, is leading Butch Martin's case.
-   **Cross-Reference**: Not explicitly mentioned in the transcript if he is in an exoneree database, but likely given the involvement of the Innocence Project of Texas.
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: A fire broke out at Butch Martin's home in February 1998, resulting in the deaths of his girlfriend, Marcia Poole, and her two children, Brady and Kristen. Butch was seen approaching the burning house after returning from a brief trip with a friend.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - Testimony from Marcia's mother suggesting a tumultuous relationship and potential motive.
    - Testimony from Butch's friend, Stacey, about Butch's supposedly suspicious statement ("I hope they're not going to my house").
    - Changed testimony from the original pathologist, Dr. Hoblett, claiming blunt force trauma to Brady and Marcia, contradicting his initial report of death by smoke inhalation.
    - Testimony from fire experts Dale Little and John Corn, claiming evidence of accelerants and pour patterns indicating arson.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Butch hired private attorneys Clifford Hardwick and John Cook, who presented a rigorous defense. They brought in their own fire experts and a forensic pathologist who refuted the prosecution's claims. They argued the fire could have started from a faulty extension cord on the back porch and that brain swelling was consistent with smoke inhalation, not blunt force trauma.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - Discrediting of "pour pattern" arson science and the understanding that chemicals found at the scene are common household products.
    - The State Fire Marshal's opinion (prior to Innocence Project involvement) stating that the fire's cause was undetermined and arson could not be confirmed.
    - Discrediting of the original pathologist, Dr. Hoblett, due to his arrest and loss of medical license for running a pain pill mill, undermining his revised testimony of blunt force trauma.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Not discussed in the transcript. The focus is on the lack of evidence against Butch and the flaws in the prosecution's case.
