### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction Junk Science, hosted by Josh Dubin, delves into the psychological tactics behind false confessions and their roots in junk science.  The episode uses a psychology experiment by Saul Cassin to illustrate how pressure and false evidence can lead individuals to confess to acts they did not commit, even in low-stakes scenarios. It then connects this to high-stakes police interrogations, particularly the Reid Technique, highlighting its flaws and potential to elicit false confessions from innocent individuals.  The podcast features criminal defense attorney David Rudolph, who explains the Reid Technique, its reliance on intuition and arrogance, and the dangers of confirmation bias.  The episode contrasts the Reid Technique with the PEACE method used in Great Britain and discusses the unique allowance of police deception in US interrogations.  Finally, it emphasizes the need for systemic change and encourages listeners to be aware of these issues and advocate for reform, including jury duty and public discussion.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Use of Reid Technique interrogation methods that are psychologically coercive and can lead to false confessions.
    -   Lack of scientific basis for detecting deception through verbal and nonverbal cues by interrogators.
    -   Legal permissibility of police deception about evidence in the US.
    -   Juror misunderstanding of false confessions and the presumption of innocence.
    -   Pressure on law enforcement to secure confessions, especially when other evidence is lacking.

-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Stop using the Reid Technique of interrogation.
    -   Adopt the PEACE method of interviewing, as used in Great Britain.
    -   Change laws to prevent law enforcement from lying to suspects about evidence.
    -   Consider implementing a "not proven" verdict option for juries, similar to Scotland, to better reflect the burden of proof and presumption of innocence.
    -   Encourage public awareness and discussion about false confessions.
    -   Advise individuals to always request a lawyer if interrogated, regardless of innocence.
