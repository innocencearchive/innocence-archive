### Executive Summary

This episode of Wrongful Conviction features Stephanie Spurgeon, a daycare provider wrongfully convicted of manslaughter in the death of a child in her care.  The episode details how the junk science of shaken baby syndrome, later morphed into a "soft impact theory," led to Stephanie's conviction despite a lack of external injuries on the child.  Experts initially misdiagnosed the child's symptoms, ignoring signs of a diabetic medical emergency.  Stephanie spent nine years in prison before being exonerated with the help of the Innocence Project of Florida and other legal teams.  The podcast highlights the failures of her original defense attorney, the dangers of relying on biased medical testimony, and the devastating impact of wrongful convictions on individuals and families. The episode underscores the systemic issues within the justice system and calls for medical and legal reform to prevent similar tragedies.  It also features Allison Miller, a state attorney candidate, who discusses the need for prosecutorial reform to prevent wrongful convictions.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Overreliance on flawed or outdated medical theories like shaken baby syndrome.
    -   Bias and tunnel vision in child death investigations, leading to premature accusations of abuse.
    -   Lack of understanding of complex medical evidence by jurors.
    -   "Cottage industry of medical fabrication" where certain medical experts consistently provide biased testimony.
    -   Ineffective assistance of counsel as a significant factor in wrongful convictions.
    -   Challenges in overturning wrongful convictions even with strong evidence of innocence.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Medical community needs to update training and recognize the limitations of shaken baby syndrome diagnosis and consider alternative medical explanations.
    -   Law enforcement and medical professionals should be more diligent and avoid assumptions of abuse in child injury cases.
    -   Need for prosecutorial reform to ensure reliable forensic evidence is used and biased expert testimony is challenged.
    -   Importance of supporting innocence projects and organizations working on criminal justice reform.
