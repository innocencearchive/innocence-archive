### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom features the case of Anthony Sims, who was wrongfully convicted of murder in 1998 and sentenced to 25 years to life.  The episode details the flawed investigation and trial, highlighting the role of a false narrative created by the actual perpetrator, Julius Graves, and supported by coerced witnesses and ignored exculpatory evidence. Key issues include Brady violations, ineffective defense, and systemic failures within the justice system.  After serving 24 years, Sims was released on parole, largely due to the efforts of attorneys Jonathan Hiles and Thomas Hoffman. The podcast emphasizes the ongoing fight for full exoneration and the devastating personal impact of wrongful convictions on individuals and their families.  The episode also touches on broader systemic issues within the criminal justice system and calls for reform and greater accountability.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Anthony Sims
-   **Case Location**: Brooklyn, New York, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1999
-   **Exoneration Year**: Not yet fully exonerated, released on parole in 2022.
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder in the second degree (intentional and depraved indifference), criminal possession of a weapon in the fourth degree.
-   **Sentence**: 25 years to life
-   **Time Served**: 24 years
-   **Contributing Factors**: False accusation, perjury by Julius Graves and his associates, coerced witness testimony (Shalima), Brady violations (withholding of Rachel's testimony and inconsistencies in Dorothy Bolding's statements), ineffective defense counsel, prosecutorial misconduct (Mark Hale).
-   **Legal Status**: Released on parole, seeking full exoneration.
-   **Organizations Involved**:  Attorneys Jonathan Hiles and Thomas Hoffman (pro bono), Brooklyn Conviction Review Unit (initially obstructive, later potentially relevant with change in leadership). Lava for Good podcasts, Wrongful Conviction podcast for raising awareness.
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Jonathan Hiles and Thomas Hoffman are criminal defense attorneys who took on Anthony Sims' case pro bono. They reinvestigated the case, uncovered crucial Brady violations, and presented new evidence leading to his release on parole.
-   **Cross-Reference**: Yes, should be added to exoneree database.
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Lee Run Chen was shot and killed at Mr. Hings Chinese restaurant in Brooklyn on May 18, 1998. Anthony Sims was accused of being the shooter.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**: Testimony of Julius Graves, Derek Dallop, Louis Cologne, Dorothy Bolding (all friends or family of Graves) implicating Anthony Sims as the shooter.  Ambiguous testimony from Shalima who claimed to see a taller man running with a gun.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**:  Defense lawyer did not call any witnesses, including Anthony Sims himself.  Relied on the perceived weakness of the prosecution's case, which proved to be insufficient.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**: Recantation (partial) from Julius Graves.  Affidavit from Rachel, the independent witness who saw Julius Graves running from the restaurant with a shotgun and was threatened by Graves. Statement from Shalima recanting her pressured testimony. Discovery of Brady violations (withholding Rachel's statement, inconsistencies in Bolding's statements, benefits given to Graves for testimony, Graves' probation violation). Testimony from Mr. Hings cook describing shooter as "not black," inconsistent with Anthony Sims' complexion.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Julius Graves, friend of Anthony Sims. Motive was revenge for Lee Run Chen allegedly touching his fiance's hand inappropriately.  Graves confessed to wiping the gun clean and hiding it. Rachel witnessed Graves running from the scene with a shotgun.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Brady violations and lack of accountability for prosecutors who commit them.
    -   Coerced witness testimony and unreliable eyewitness identification.
    -   Ineffective assistance of counsel.
    -   Tunnel vision in police investigations focused on a quick conviction rather than finding the truth.
    -   Racial bias and devaluing of Black lives within the justice system.
    -   Difficulty in overturning wrongful convictions even with compelling new evidence.
    -   Obstruction by conviction review units, especially when conflicted.
-   **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    -   Increased accountability for Brady violations.
    -   Reform of eyewitness identification procedures.
    -   Improved access to effective defense counsel.
    -   Independent and truly impartial conviction review units, free from conflicts of interest.
    -   Greater emphasis on truth-seeking and justice rather than just securing convictions.
    -   Support for organizations working on wrongful conviction cases.
-   **Personal Narrative/Impact**: Anthony Sims' personal narrative highlights the devastating impact of wrongful conviction. He lost 24 years of his life, was separated from his children, and suffered immense emotional and psychological trauma. Despite this, he demonstrates incredible resilience, forgiveness, and a commitment to helping others. His story underscores the human cost of failures within the justice system and the long-lasting scars of wrongful imprisonment. His wife Keisha's unwavering support and love are also a central part of the narrative, showcasing the strength of family bonds in the face of injustice.
