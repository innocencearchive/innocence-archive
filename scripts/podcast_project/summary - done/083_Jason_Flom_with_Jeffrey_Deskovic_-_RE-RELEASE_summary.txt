### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: In this episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom, Jeff Deskavic recounts his horrific experience of being wrongfully convicted for the rape and murder of Angela Correa at age 16. Despite DNA evidence proving his innocence being available at his trial, Deskavic was convicted based on a coerced false confession obtained through manipulative police interrogation, compounded by prosecutorial misconduct and an inept defense lawyer. He spent 16 years in maximum-security prisons enduring unimaginable conditions. Exonerated in 2006 after new DNA technology identified the actual perpetrator, Stephen Cunningham, who also committed a second murder, Deskavic now dedicates his life to fighting wrongful convictions through his foundation. He discusses the systemic failures that led to his wrongful conviction and the ongoing challenges faced by exonerees post-release, urging listeners to support his foundation and criminal justice reform.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   **Coercive Interrogation Tactics**: Use of deception, false promises, threats, and psychological manipulation to elicit false confessions from vulnerable individuals, particularly juveniles.
    -   **Prosecutorial Misconduct**:  Fraudulent use of medical examiner testimony to undermine exculpatory DNA evidence and maintain a false narrative of guilt. Withholding exculpatory witness statements.
    -   **Ineffective Defense Counsel**: Failure of public defenders to adequately represent clients due to workload, lack of resources, or conflict of interest, leading to inadequate challenges to prosecution evidence and missed opportunities to present a strong defense.
    -   **Confirmation Bias and Tunnel Vision**: Law enforcement and prosecutors becoming fixated on a suspect early in the investigation and ignoring or dismissing exculpatory evidence.
    -   **Lack of Accountability**: Failure of the justice system to correct errors even when presented with clear evidence of innocence, such as DNA. Lack of consequences for misconduct by police and prosecutors.
    -   **Impact of Public Pressure**: High-profile cases, especially those involving children, create immense public pressure on law enforcement to secure a conviction, potentially leading to compromised investigations and prosecutions.
