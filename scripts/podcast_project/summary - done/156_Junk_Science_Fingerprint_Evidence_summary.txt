- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    - **Subjectivity of Fingerprint Analysis**: The process is not based on objective scientific standards but on the subjective interpretation of examiners.
    - **Lack of Scientific Validation**: Fingerprint analysis lacks rigorous scientific validation, population statistics, and documented error rates.
    - **Flawed Methodology (ACE-V)**: The ACE-V process relies on subjective judgment at each step, lacking standardization and objectivity.
    - **Confirmation Bias**:  Verification process in fingerprint analysis is often not blind, leading to confirmation bias and reinforcing initial subjective interpretations.
    - **Overreliance on Precedent**: Courts often rely on historical precedent and are resistant to challenging long-accepted forensic methods like fingerprinting, despite scientific critiques.
    - **Public Misconception**: Public perception, influenced by popular culture, incorrectly assumes fingerprint evidence is infallible and scientifically sound.
    - **Lack of Transparency and Documentation**: Insufficient documentation of fingerprint analysis processes hinders independent review and replication.
    - **Non-Scientific Background of Examiners**: Many fingerprint examiners lack formal scientific training, relying on apprenticeship rather than scientific expertise.

- **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    - **Educate Judges**: Send the NAS report on forensic science to local criminal court judges, highlighting the section on fingerprint analysis, to educate them about its limitations.

    - **Demand Transparency and Scientific Rigor**: Advocate for greater transparency, documentation, and scientific validation in all forensic disciplines, including fingerprint analysis.
    - **Promote Blind Verification**: Push for blind verification processes in forensic analysis to mitigate confirmation bias.
