### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction recounts the harrowing story of Howard Dudley, wrongfully convicted of child sexual abuse in 1991 based on false allegations from his young daughter, Amy.  The case unfolded during a period of intense child sex abuse hysteria, where any child's claim was readily believed. Suggestive questioning by Amy's babysitter, Lydia Mae Starkey, fueled the accusations, leading to Howard's arrest despite a guardian ad-litem finding Amy's story untrue.  Tragically, Amy recanted immediately after the trial, yet Howard endured nearly 25 years of imprisonment. The podcast emphasizes systemic failures, including ineffective defense counsel, prosecutorial withholding of crucial evidence, and the devastating impact of societal hysteria.  Ultimately, Teresa Newman and the Duke Law Wrongful Conviction Clinic intervened, leading to Howard's exoneration in 2016 and highlighting the resilience of the wrongfully convicted and the crucial role of legal advocacy.

-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**: The primary evidence was the testimony of nine-year-old Amy, who made inconsistent and eventually recanted allegations of sexual abuse. The prosecution presented Amy's testimony, despite its inconsistencies and implausibility, as credible evidence of Howard's guilt.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Howard's trial defense lawyer was described as inexperienced (practicing for only about a year) and ineffective. He devoted minimal time to the case (around 30 hours), failed to conduct any independent investigation, did not file pretrial motions, and did not adequately challenge Amy's competency or the inconsistencies in her statements.  He also failed to obtain or utilize the DSS reports and the guardian ad-litem's findings.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**: Amy's immediate recantation after the trial,  evidence of Amy's suggestibility and cognitive limitations, the discovery and presentation of withheld DSS reports and the guardian ad-litem's report which deemed Amy's claims untrue, and the argument of ineffective assistance of counsel. Judge Parsons deemed Amy's recantation credible and cited ineffective assistance of counsel and prosecutorial disclosure failures as grounds for vacating the conviction.
