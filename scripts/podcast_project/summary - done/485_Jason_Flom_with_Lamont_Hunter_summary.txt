### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction tells the harrowing story of Lamont Hunter, who was wrongfully convicted and sentenced to death in Ohio for the death of his girlfriend's son, Tristan. The case hinged on the now-discredited Shaken Baby Syndrome and allegations of sexual assault.  Despite maintaining his innocence and evidence suggesting Tristan's injuries were from a fall and medical intervention, Lamont spent 16 years incarcerated, many on death row.  His trial was marred by ineffective counsel and the prosecution presented flawed medical testimony and withheld crucial evidence.  After years of legal battles and new evidence uncovered by his post-conviction legal team, including a recantation from the coroner and biomechanical evidence supporting a fall, Lamont was finally released after taking a plea for time served in 2023.  The episode highlights systemic issues within the justice system, the dangers of junk science, and the long-lasting impact of wrongful convictions on individuals and their families.

She is described as taking time to explain the legal process to Lamont and his family, building trust and a strong working relationship.

-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Lamont Hunter was accused of causing fatal injuries to Tristan Milda, his girlfriend's three-year-old son, while Tristan was in his care.  The prosecution alleged that Tristan's injuries, including subdural hematoma, retinal hemorrhages, and cerebral edema, were indicative of violent shaking and impact, consistent with Shaken Baby Syndrome.  They also alleged sexual assault based on anal/rectal injuries.  Lamont maintained that Tristan fell down the stairs.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - Medical expert testimony (child abuse pediatrician and coroner initially) attributing Tristan's injuries to abusive head trauma (Shaken Baby Syndrome) and not a fall.
    - Testimony suggesting anal/rectal injuries were consistent with sexual assault by an adult.
    - Past incident of Tristan's broken tibia in 2004, and 2004 hospital visit for unexplained injuries, presented to suggest a pattern of abuse, despite Lamont not being implicated in the 2004 incidents.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Lamont's trial attorney, Clyde Bennett, failed to:
    - Consult or hire medical experts to challenge the state's Shaken Baby Syndrome theory.
    - Adequately investigate medical records.
    - Effectively cross-examine state experts, relying on irrelevant lines of questioning.
    - Advise Lamont against a bench trial despite judicial warning of likely loss.
    - He was also distracted by his own federal criminal investigation during Lamont's trial.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - Deposition testimony from the coroner, Dr. Stevens, recanting her initial opinion after reviewing photographs of the staircase, biomechanical expert analysis, and medical records revealing rectal thermometer attempts. She changed her opinion to accidental death and medically inflicted rectal injuries.
    - Biomechanical expert testimony demonstrating that a fall down the stairs could generate forces sufficient to cause Tristan's injuries.
    - Medical records showing hospital staff's unsuccessful attempts to take Tristan's temperature rectally and subsequent notation of blood in rectum, providing innocent explanation for anal/rectal injuries.
    - Evidence of Brady violation (withheld evidence of coroner's consideration of temperature probe as cause of rectal injuries).
