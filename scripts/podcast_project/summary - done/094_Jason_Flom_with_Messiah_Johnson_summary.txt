### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features Messiah Johnson, who recounts his wrongful conviction for armed robbery in Norfolk, Virginia in 1997.  Despite alibis and no physical evidence, Johnson was identified in a suggestive show-up and faced incompetent legal representation, leading to a 132-year sentence. He details the harsh interrogation, inadequate defense, and the devastating trial.  After serving 20 years, the University of Virginia Innocence Project, particularly Deirdre Enright, reinvestigated his case, secured a confession from the actual perpetrator, and ultimately helped him obtain a conditional pardon.  Johnson emphasizes the systemic issues in Virginia's justice system, the importance of post-conviction investigation, and his commitment to helping others through his non-profit, the Lion Initiative.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   Suggestive police show-ups and eyewitness misidentification.
    -   Ineffective assistance of counsel, including overworked public defenders and incompetent attorneys.
    -   Malicious prosecution and vindictive sentencing after exercising right to trial.
    -   Harsh sentencing laws in Virginia, including abolition of parole and 85% rule.
    -   Procedural barriers to post-conviction relief, especially the one-year deadline in AEDPA.
    -   Racial bias and disproportionate impact on disenfranchised communities.
    -   Lack of adequate resources for defense attorneys, especially public defenders.

    -   Improving the quality of legal representation for indigent defendants.
