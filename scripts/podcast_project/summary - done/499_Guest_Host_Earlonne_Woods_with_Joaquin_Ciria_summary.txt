### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features the story of Joaquin Syria, who was wrongfully convicted of murder in San Francisco in 1990 and exonerated after nearly 32 years. Guest host Erlan Woods interviews Joaquin and Paige Kanep, supervising attorney at the Northern California Innocence Project. The episode details Joaquin's difficult upbringing in Cuba, his immigration to the US, and the events leading to his wrongful conviction based on flawed eyewitness identification and a coerced statement from a young acquaintance.  The podcast highlights systemic issues within the San Francisco Police Department in the 1990s,  the impact of wrongful convictions on individuals and families, and calls for accountability and reform within the justice system to prevent future tragedies and re-examine past cases from this era.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Joaquin Syria
-   **Case Location**: San Francisco, California, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1990
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2022 (April 18, 2022)
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: First-degree murder
-   **Sentence**: 31 years to life
-   **Time Served**: Almost 32 years
-   **Contributing Factors**:
    -   Eyewitness misidentification (flawed and suggestive police procedures)
    -   Coerced false statement (from George Varela)
    -   Ineffective assistance of counsel (failure to present alibi and context of Varela's statement)
    -   Rumors and initial police focus on Joaquin without sufficient evidence
-   **Legal Status**: Exonerated, charges dismissed
-   **Organizations Involved**: Northern California Innocence Project, Innocence Commission, Pro Bono Attorneys (Ellen Egger)
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Paige Kanep, Supervising Attorney at the Northern California Innocence Project, and Ellen Egger, pro bono attorney, played key roles in investigating and litigating Joaquin's case post-conviction, uncovering new evidence and challenging the original conviction.
-   **Cross-Reference**: Likely appears in exoneree database (needs to be verified)
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Felix Bostarica was shot and killed in an alley in San Francisco's Mission District on March 24, 1990. Witnesses reported seeing a man argue with Felix before shooting him and fleeing in a white Monte Carlo with a driver.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    -   Eyewitness identification (initially weak, strengthened after repeated exposure to Joaquin's photo and suggestive procedures).
    -   Coerced statement from George Varela claiming Joaquin was the shooter.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Joaquin's attorney highlighted discrepancies in eyewitness descriptions and Varela's timeline, but failed to present alibi witnesses (Joaquin's girlfriend and roommate) and the coercive nature of Varela's statement (that police suggested Joaquin's name first and pressured Varela).
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    -   Retraction of George Varela's false statement (through statements to his sister and a family friend).
    -   Confession from Roberto Socorro, an eyewitness who saw Condido Diaz shoot Felix Bostarica from his motel room and identified Condido as the perpetrator.
    -   Evidence of Condido Diaz's motive and matching the original suspect description (Afro, trench coat, feud with victim).
    -   Expert analysis of eyewitness identification unreliability in the case.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Condido Diaz, a Cuban man known to have a feud with Felix Bostarica and fitting the initial eyewitness descriptions of the shooter. He was seen by Roberto Socorro committing the crime.
