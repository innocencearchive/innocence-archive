### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom features DiMarco Carpenter and Malcolm Scott, two men wrongfully convicted of murder in Tulsa, Oklahoma in 1994 and exonerated after serving 22 years.  The episode details their upbringing in a tough neighborhood, the flawed police investigation that focused on them despite evidence pointing to Michael Wilson (the actual killer), and the failures of their public defenders.  It highlights the racial injustices within the justice system, the impact of police misconduct and the devastating consequences of wrongful convictions on the individuals and society.  The exoneration came after years of appeals, witness recantations, and ultimately, a confession from Michael Wilson before his execution for a different crime. Carpenter and Scott share their harrowing experiences in prison, the importance of family support and faith, and their efforts to rebuild their lives post-exoneration, advocating for justice and reform.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    - Police misconduct: Fabricating evidence, coercing witnesses, ignoring evidence pointing to the actual perpetrator.
    - Prosecutorial misconduct: Pursuing a case despite weak evidence and obvious alternative suspect, lack of accountability.
    - Ineffective defense counsel: Public defenders failing to adequately represent clients, investigate alibis, and challenge prosecution's case.
    - Racial bias: Case occurring in Tulsa with historical racial tensions, death penalty disparities mentioned, and the victims being black and white in different crimes influencing the case.
    - Lack of compensation and support for exonerees: Inadequate financial compensation and lack of support for reintegration into society.
    - Jury selection issues: Juror who volunteered for police service was on the jury.
