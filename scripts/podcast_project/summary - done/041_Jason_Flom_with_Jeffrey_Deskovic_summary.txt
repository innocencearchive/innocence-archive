### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Jason Flom" features Jeff Deskavic, who recounts his harrowing experience of being wrongfully convicted of rape and murder as a 16-year-old.  Despite DNA evidence excluding him, Deskavic was coerced into a false confession during a grueling police interrogation and subsequently convicted in 1990.  The podcast details the egregious failures of the justice system, including prosecutorial misconduct, an ineffective defense attorney, and the dismissal of crucial DNA evidence at trial.  Deskavic served 16 years before being exonerated in 2006 thanks to the Innocence Project.  The episode highlights the devastating personal impact of wrongful conviction, the systemic issues that contribute to such injustices, and Deskavic's inspiring journey to freedom and his current work advocating for criminal justice reform and helping other exonerees through his foundation.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

-   **Name**: Jeffrey Deskavic
-   **Case Location**: White Plains, Westchester County, New York, USA
-   **Conviction Year**: 1990
-   **Exoneration Year**: 2006
-   **Crime Convicted Of**: Rape and murder of Angela Correa
-   **Sentence**: 15 years to life
-   **Time Served**: 16 years
-   **Contributing Factors**: False confession, prosecutorial misconduct, ineffective defense counsel, ignoring DNA evidence, pressure on law enforcement to solve high-profile case.
-   **Legal Status**: Exonerated
-   **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project, Jeffrey Daskovic Foundation for Justice
-   **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Innocence Project lawyers convinced prosecutors to re-examine DNA evidence in 2006, leading to exoneration. Initial trial lawyer was a public defender from legal aid, described as "inept" and "terrible."
-   **Cross-Reference**: Yes (should be in exoneree database)
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: 15-year-old Angela Correa was brutally beaten, raped, and strangled after going out alone to take photographs for a school assignment.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - False confession obtained after a lengthy and coercive interrogation, including deceptive tactics, threats, and false promises.
    - Fraudulent testimony from the medical examiner to suggest the victim may have had consensual sex to explain away the lack of Deskavic's DNA at the crime scene.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**:
    - Public defender was ineffective and did not adequately defend Deskavic.
    - Failed to highlight the significance of the DNA evidence not matching Deskavic.
    - Did not effectively cross-examine the medical examiner regarding the fraudulent testimony.
    - Did not present Deskavic's alibi or allow Deskavic to testify about the coerced confession at a pretrial hearing.
    - Had a conflict of interest due to representing another youth also implicated by the prosecution's theories.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - New DNA testing technology in January 2006 allowed for re-examination of evidence.
    - This new testing definitively excluded Deskavic and identified Stephen Cunningham as the actual perpetrator through DNA match.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**:
    - Stephen Cunningham was identified as the actual perpetrator through DNA evidence.
    - Cunningham was later caught and incarcerated for a second murder (Patricia Morrison, 3.5 years after Angela Correa).
    - Cunningham confessed to both murders, including Angela Correa's rape and murder, on video.
    - Cunningham's crimes were committed while Deskavic was wrongfully imprisoned for his crime.
