### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction, False Confessions, tells the tragic story of Hugh Burton, who at 16 years old, was coerced into falsely confessing to the murder of his mother in 1989.  Despite inconsistencies in his confession and the emergence of a more likely suspect, his downstairs neighbor Emmanuel Green, Hugh was convicted and spent almost two decades in prison.  The podcast details the flawed police investigation, the pressure tactics used during interrogation, and the cultural context of the crack cocaine epidemic that influenced the narrative of Hugh's confession.  Years later, the Center on Wrongful Convictions of Youth, along with the Innocence Project, took up Hugh's case.  Through persistent investigation and collaboration with the Bronx Conviction Integrity Unit, new evidence and the exposure of police misconduct led to Hugh's exoneration in 2019, after a 30-year ordeal.  The story underscores the dangers of false confessions, especially from vulnerable youth, and the importance of conviction integrity efforts.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

- **Name**: Hugh Burton
- **Case Location**: Bronx, New York, USA
- **Conviction Year**: 1989
- **Exoneration Year**: 2019
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Second-degree murder of his mother, Kazaya Burton
- **Sentence**: Life in prison
- **Time Served**: Almost 20 years
- **Contributing Factors**: False confession (coerced through threats of statutory rape charges and promises of leniency), tunnel vision by police investigators, suggestive interrogation techniques.
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated
- **Organizations Involved**: Innocence Project, Center on Wrongful Convictions of Youth, Bronx Defender, Bronx Conviction Integrity Unit.
- **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Steve Drizen and Laura Cohen (Center on Wrongful Convictions of Youth), Susan Friedman and Barry Shek (Innocence Project).
- **Narrative of Original Crime**: Kazaya Burton was found murdered in her home, stabbed with a knife. Police initially suspected robbery but focused on Hugh, her son. The prosecution's narrative was that Hugh, driven by a crack cocaine addiction and a $200 drug debt, murdered his mother in a drug-fueled rage.
- **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**: Hugh's confession to the murder, despite its inconsistencies and likely coercion.
- **Trial Defender Notes**: Not detailed in the transcript, but implied to be inadequate as they failed to prevent the conviction based on a flawed confession and emerging alternative suspect.
- **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - Autopsy findings contradicted Hugh's confession: the murder weapon was a smooth-edged blade, not the serrated steak knife mentioned in his confession.
    - Discovery of another false confession coerced by the same detectives in a separate case (Dennis Koss).
    - Retraction by Hugh's teacher who initially misreported his attendance on the day of the murder, confirming he was in school.
    - Emmanuel Green, the downstairs neighbor, was found with the victim's car and gave a confession that also contained inconsistencies but pointed to his involvement.
- **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Emmanuel Green, a 22-year-old neighbor with a criminal history of rape and robbery. He lived in the apartment below the Burtons.  He was found driving Kazaya Burton's stolen car. He confessed to being involved after Hugh allegedly confessed to him, but his confession was also considered suspicious and potentially coerced by the same detectives.  He was never charged with Kazaya Burton's murder and died before Hugh's exoneration.
