### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features the story of Evan King, who was wrongfully convicted of the rape and murder of Crystal Hudson in 1994 and served almost 25 years in prison.  Despite DNA evidence excluding him and a lack of physical evidence linking him to the crime, he was convicted based on circumstantial presence at the scene and a premonition presented as evidence.  False testimony from the coroner's office regarding sperm deposition further misled the jury.  The Ohio Innocence Project eventually took on his case, and after years of legal battles and new DNA testing on fingernail scrapings that matched the semen DNA, prosecutors finally agreed to his release in 2017. The episode highlights the failures of the justice system, inadequate defense counsel, and the long fight for exoneration.

-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: Crystal Hudson was found dead in her apartment closet, having been raped and strangled. Evan King visited her apartment while her body was already there, unaware of her death.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    -   Evan King was present in the apartment where the body was found.
    -   Crystal Hudson's mother's "premonition" that Evan would kill her daughter.
    -   False testimony from the coroner's office suggesting the sperm DNA evidence could be unrelated to the murder, deposited days before.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Rocco Russo, Evan King's court-appointed attorney, was described as ineffective. He did not object to evidence, appeared to be falling asleep in court, and presented no defense.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    -   DNA testing on fingernail scrapings from Crystal Hudson matched the DNA from the rape kit (semen), confirming the rapist was also the murderer and it was not Evan King.
    -   Expert testimony debunking the initial coroner's office testimony about sperm deposition timing, proving the sperm was deposited at the time of death.
    -   The prosecutor's office eventually reviewed the case and agreed with the Ohio Innocence Project's findings, leading to Evan King's release.
