### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features the harrowing story of Keith Bush, wrongfully convicted in 1975 for the murder of Cherise Watson.  Despite over 20 alibi witnesses placing him elsewhere, and compelling alternate suspects, Bush was subjected to brutal police interrogation leading to a false confession. The prosecution relied on fabricated forensic evidence, including a planted hair pick and junk science about puncture wounds.  Exculpatory evidence regarding another suspect, John Jones, was concealed for decades. Bush served over 20 years in prison, consistently maintaining his innocence and facing repeated parole denials due to his refusal to admit guilt.  After a 44-year fight, involving DNA testing, recanted witness testimony, and the exposure of prosecutorial misconduct, Keith Bush was finally exonerated in 2019. His story exemplifies the profound systemic failures within the criminal justice system, highlighting issues of police coercion, prosecutorial dishonesty, and the devastating impact of wrongful convictions.

She is described as "one of the real greats in this field." She helped get DNA testing allowed, and presented forensic evidence to prove the confession was false, and exposed the Brady violation related to John Jones.
-   **Cross-Reference**: Yes (Exoneree Database - to be checked)
-   **Narrative of Original Crime**: 14-year-old Cherise Watson was found strangled to death in a field near a house party she attended. Her pants were partially unzipped, and she had puncture wounds on her back.  Police investigated Keith Bush after witness statements placed him as the last person seen with her.
-   **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - False confession from Keith Bush, stating he stabbed Cherise with a hair pick and strangled her after she refused a sexual advance.
    - Testimony from Maxine Bell claiming to have seen Keith Bush and Cherise leaving the party together "hugged up." (Later recanted).
    - Medical examiner's opinion that puncture wounds were consistent with a hair pick.
    - Detective testimony claiming fibers from Cherise's fingernails matched Keith Bush's jacket.
    - Planted metal hair pick presented as the murder weapon.
-   **Trial Defender Notes**: Harold Seligman, Keith Bush's paid attorney, lacked knowledge of the John Jones statement and alternate suspect.  When attempting to question Detective Rafferty about other suspects, the prosecutor Gerald Sullivan intervened, preventing this line of questioning by suggesting it would open the door to damaging raw evidence.
-   **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - DNA testing excluded Keith Bush from fingernail scrapings found on Cherise Watson.
    - Dr. Michael Baden's forensic analysis demonstrated the impossibility of the hair pick causing the puncture wounds as described in the false confession and by the medical examiner.
    - Maxine Bell's 2016 affidavit recanting her trial testimony and admitting it was made out of fear of police.
    - Discovery of John Jones' 1975 statement admitting to being at the crime scene and that the hair pick found was his (Brady violation).
    - Suffolk County Conviction Integrity Bureau investigation agreed with Dr. Baden's findings, deemed fiber evidence unsubstantiated, and identified John Jones as the most viable suspect.
-   **Actual Perpetrator Details**: John Jones, identified as a viable suspect by the Suffolk County CIB.  In May 1975, he gave a statement admitting to being at the crime scene, stumbling over Cherise's body and dropping his hair pick. He was interviewed by Detective Rafferty and took a polygraph.  This information was concealed for decades. Jones was later arrested for rape in 1976 and died in 2006.
