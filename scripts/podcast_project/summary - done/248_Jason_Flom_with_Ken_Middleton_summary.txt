### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of the Wrongful Conviction podcast features the harrowing story of Ken Middleton, wrongfully convicted in 1991 for the murder of his wife, Kathy. The podcast details the events of February 12, 1990, where Kathy tragically died from a gunshot wound in their home. Ken maintains it was an accident, triggered by Kathy's distress over news of a past affair. Despite evidence suggesting an accidental shooting and significant mishandling of evidence by law enforcement - including a potentially altered gunshot residue test and a staged crime scene - Ken was convicted and sentenced to life plus 200 years.  The episode highlights egregious issues such as ineffective defense counsel, false expert testimony, prosecutorial conflicts of interest, and jurisdictional loopholes that have perpetuated Ken's unjust imprisonment even after his conviction was overturned in 2004.  Ken and his son Cliff join host Jason Pham to plead for public awareness and action, urging the current prosecutor to utilize a new Missouri statute to finally grant Ken his freedom after over three decades of wrongful incarceration.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

- **Name**: Ken Middleton
- **Case Location**: Blue Springs, Missouri, USA
- **Conviction Year**: 1991
- **Exoneration Year**: 2004 (conviction overturned, but not fully exonerated due to jurisdictional issues)
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder of Kathy Middleton (wife)
- **Sentence**: Life plus 200 years
- **Time Served**: Over 30 years (as of podcast recording in transcript)
- **Contributing Factors**:
    - Ineffective Assistance of Counsel (Bob Duncan)
    - False Expert Testimony (Blood Spatter Expert)
    - Evidence Tampering (Altered gunshot residue test document)
    - Incompetent Investigation (Mishandled crime scene, restaged photos, failure to properly collect and preserve evidence)
    - Prosecutorial Misconduct & Conflicts of Interest (Prosecutor's father's law firm involved in civil suit against Ken)
    - Tunnel Vision (Police immediately focused on Ken as suspect)
- **Legal Status**: Conviction overturned in 2004, but still incarcerated due to jurisdictional technicality. Awaiting action from the current prosecutor to utilize a new Missouri law to resolve the issue and potentially grant release or new trial.
- **Organizations Involved**:
    - Silver Creek Entertainment (Larry Garrison - Newsbreaker who brought the case to the podcast)
    - AIG pro bono program (mentioned in podcast advertisement, not directly involved in Ken's case as per transcript)
- **Innocence Lawyer Details**:
    - **Bob Duncan (Trial Attorney)**:  Described as ineffective and unprepared. Failed to investigate evidence, didn't consult experts, didn't make opening statement, and was generally negligent in defending Ken. Later admitted to his failures in affidavits.
    - **Gerald Handley (2915 Appeal Attorney)**:  Also ineffective, described as disinterested. Procedurally defaulted Ken's appeal issues by filing a deficient motion, preventing the merits of the case from being heard.
    - **Jonathan Lawrence (Post-Conviction Attorney after 2001)**: Effective attorney who took on the case after new law allowed for re-evaluation. Filed an 81-page motion, presented expert testimony (Chuck Gay, Bob Trestle), and secured the overturning of Ken's conviction in 2004.
- **Narrative of Original Crime**: Ken was accused of intentionally shooting his wife, Kathy, during a domestic dispute. The prosecution argued Ken held Kathy against a wall and shot her at close range in the face.
- **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - Blood spatter "expert" testimony claiming Kathy was shot at close range while against the wall, implying a struggle and intentional act by Ken.
    - Alleged boot print on the wall, claimed to be evidence of Ken holding Kathy against the wall (later shown to be impossible and pre-existing).
    - Implication that Ken was trying to wash away gunshot residue by splashing water on his face and touching a potted plant.
    - Sister of Kathy gave testimony suggesting Ken had secret assets in Arkansas as a motive (later proven false and sister admitted perjury).
- **Trial Defender Notes**:
    - Bob Duncan was woefully inadequate. He failed to:
        - Investigate the physical evidence, including the gun and gunshot residue tests.
        - Consult with or present expert witnesses to counter the prosecution's claims.
        - Interview key witnesses.
        - Make an opening statement.
        - Address the altered gunshot residue document or conflicting evidence.
        - Effectively challenge the prosecution's narrative.
- **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - **Ballistics Expert Testimony (Bob Trestle)**: Demonstrated that the prosecution's scenario of Ken shooting Kathy was physically impossible based on bullet trajectory and angles. Showed that a shooter in the position described by the prosecution would have to be "under the table."
    - **Crime Scene Investigation Expert Testimony (Chuck Gay)**:  Highlighted the gross mishandling of the crime scene by police, including restaging photos, improper evidence collection (unclothing Kathy at the scene), and the altered gunshot residue document.
    - **Analysis of Altered Gunshot Residue Document**:  Revealed clear evidence of tampering with the document, specifically "white-out" covering up the testing of Kathy's left hand, which would have been crucial evidence supporting accidental self-inflicted gunshot.
    - **Testimony of Former Missouri Governor Joseph P. Tisdale**: Condemned the "suspect conduct of the prosecutors" and "ineffective performance of the defense council," stating it was the worst case of constitutional violations he had witnessed and he would have pardoned Ken.
    - **Judge Messina's 2004 Ruling**: Overturned Ken's conviction due to eight points of ineffective assistance of counsel by Bob Duncan, based on the evidence presented at the evidentiary hearing.
    - **New Missouri Law (2021)**:  Removed jurisdictional barriers, allowing prosecutors to file motions to reconsider cases of wrongful conviction, even outside of the 2915 process. This law gives the current prosecutor, Gene Peters Baker, the ability to act in Ken's case.
- **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Not applicable. The defense argues Kathy's death was a tragic accident, a self-inflicted gunshot wound.
