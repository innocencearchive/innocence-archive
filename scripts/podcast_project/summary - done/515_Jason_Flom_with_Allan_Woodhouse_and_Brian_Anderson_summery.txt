### Executive Summary

This episode of Wrongful Conviction details the 1974 wrongful convictions of four young Indigenous men, focusing on guests Brian Anderson and Alan Woodhouse, for the 1973 murder of Ting Fong Chan in Winnipeg, Manitoba. Host Jason Flom interviews Brian, Alan, and their Innocence Canada lawyer Bobbi-Jo Sody, exposing a case built entirely on coerced false confessions obtained despite language barriers (Ojibwe Salto primarily for Brian) and police brutality against Alan. All collected forensic evidence excluded the accused, yet they were convicted, with Brian, Alan, and Clarence Woodhouse receiving life sentences, and Russell Woodhouse receiving 10 years for manslaughter. Brian served 10 years and Alan 17 before parole, but both continue to face restrictive parole conditions decades later. The episode highlights systemic racism, police and prosecutorial misconduct (notably by George Dangerfield), and the arduous, ongoing 50-year fight for official exoneration through Innocence Canada and the ministerial review process.

### Podcast Research Notes

-   **Key Quotes/Moments**:
    -   Bobbi-Jo Sody: "We started off knowing that this was a 1973 case. It involved the Winnipeg police service for young indigenous men and George Dangerfield. And as far as Innocence Canada was concerned, that is a recipe for wrongful conviction."
    -   Bobbi-Jo Sody on the confessions: "All four of the confessions... start off with the exact same sentence. All four of them say on Monday night, I was."
    -   Alan Woodhouse on interrogation: "They were really rough. I mean, they were, they're hitting me. I mean, I was all bloody. So after about four hours, they wrote up the statement, told me to sign it. And then you can go. So I signed it... After they signed it, they called me."
    -   Brian Anderson on signing confession: "They got me to sign a piece of paper, which I did. And I didn't know that was the confession... What they do is they make you empty your pockets and then they put stuff aside and you have to sign for them. And that's what I thought it was."
    -   Alan Woodhouse on confession inaccuracy: "The statement said that I had stabbed this person in the stomach a couple of times but there was no stab wounds in the stomach at all."
    -   Trial Judge sentencing comments (quoted by Bobbi-Jo): "This is not a jungle where we live... We are not still taking it from wild people."
    -   Brian Anderson on initial thoughts in prison: "I thought it was just do away with myself, kill myself... I thought hey I can't be doing this. I'll be helping those buggers. That's what they're trying to do to me. They're trying to kill me. Then I promised myself that I would keep going and I'm still here."
    -   Alan Woodhouse on parole decision: "...they said you know he said we can't base our decision based on what you say. We have to base our decision based on the fact that you were convicted even if you are innocent."
    -   Brian Anderson on parole officer: "He told me he was an ex cop. He was really after me like any little thing... And then he had said well like you know what we say and what the courts say are two different things. Don't bet on it he told me."
