### Basic Podcast Information

-   **Executive Summary**: This episode of Wrongful Conviction features James Soto and Robert Elmodovar, two men wrongfully convicted in Chicago. James Soto recounts his arrest and conviction for a double murder in 1981, despite being 30 miles away at the time of the crime and lacking physical evidence. He served 42 years before exoneration. Robert Elmodovar, also wrongfully convicted, became cellmates with Soto in prison. The episode explores the flawed evidence in Soto's case, including coerced witness statements and lack of physical evidence.  It highlights the devastating impact of wrongful convictions, the importance of prisoner advocacy, and the enduring friendship forged between Soto and Elmodovar during their incarceration.  Both men now advocate for criminal justice reform.

-   **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    -   **Flawed eyewitness testimony**: Highlighted in both Soto and Elmodovar's cases.
    -   **Coerced confessions/witness statements**: Implied in Soto's case with young witnesses and explicitly mentioned regarding Detective Guavera in Elmodovar's case.
    -   **Ineffective trial defense**: Suggested by Soto's initial confidence in his lawyer despite the guilty verdict and the mention of things his attorney "didn't do."
    -   **Police misconduct/tunnel vision**:  CPD's focus on gang affiliation and overlooking initial eyewitness accounts in Soto's case.  Detective Guavera's pattern of misconduct in Elmodovar's case.
    -   **Impact of pre-trial detention**:  Stress on Soto's girlfriend leading to miscarriage, loss of personal property.
    -   **Brutal prison conditions**: Violence, overcrowding, lack of resources in Illinois prisons in the 80s and 90s.
    -   **Lack of parole in Illinois**: Mentioned as a factor in Soto's life sentence and advocacy for its reinstatement.
