### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Maggie Freeling" tells the tragic story of Rosa Jimenez, a young immigrant woman wrongfully convicted of injury to a child and felony murder.  In 2003, while babysitting, a toddler in her care, Brian Gutierrez, choked on paper towels and died.  Despite maintaining her innocence and lacking English proficiency during a confusing police interrogation, Rosa was convicted based on flawed expert testimony and inadequate defense.  After serving 18 years, the Innocence Project took her case, highlighting medical evidence and prosecutorial missteps.  Rosa was finally exonerated in 2023, but faced immediate deportation proceedings due to her immigration status, which were ultimately halted.  The episode underscores systemic issues of racial bias, ineffective legal counsel, and the devastating impact of wrongful convictions, while also highlighting Rosa's resilience and the long-lasting personal consequences for exonerees and their families.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

- **Name**: Rosa Jimenez
- **Case Location**: Austin, Texas, USA
- **Conviction Year**: 2005
- **Exoneration Year**: 2023
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Injury to a child and felony murder
- **Sentence**: 99 years in prison
- **Time Served**: 18 years
- **Contributing Factors**:
    - Inadequate Legal Counsel:  Trial lawyer consulted with an unqualified expert and failed to effectively challenge prosecution's medical testimony.
    - Flawed Expert Testimony: Prosecution experts incorrectly stated that it was impossible for the child to have choked on the paper towels accidentally.
    - Language Barrier/Confusing Interrogation: Rosa, a Spanish speaker with limited English, was interrogated without a proper interpreter and may have felt pressured to say what police wanted to hear.
    - Possible Racial Bias:  Podcast suggests women of color in the jurisdiction were disproportionately prosecuted in accidental child injury cases.
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated
- **Organizations Involved**:
    - Innocence Project
    - Mexican Consulate
- **Innocence Lawyer Details**:
    - Leonard Martinez (original trial attorney - described as woefully inadequate)
    - Bryce Benjet (Innocence Project attorney, hired in 2018, instrumental in her exoneration)
    - Vanessa Potkin (Director of Special Litigation at the Innocence Project, heavily involved in Rosa's case and release)
- **Cross-Reference**: Yes (Appears in the exoneree database via Innocence Project involvement)
- **Narrative of Original Crime**: Rosa was babysitting two children, her daughter and Brian Gutierrez. Brian choked on a wad of paper towels and later died. Rosa was accused of intentionally forcing the paper towels down his throat.
- **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - Testimony from doctors and first responders who stated it was impossible for Brian to have swallowed the paper towels himself due to his gag reflex.
    - Detective Delos Santos's interrogation testimony, suggesting Rosa manipulated him and implied guilt during questioning.
- **Trial Defender Notes**:
    - Defense attorney called character witnesses who testified Rosa was peaceful.
    - Defense attorney hired an expert with questionable credentials and biased past, whose testimony backfired.
    - Overall, the defense was deemed "woefully inadequate".
- **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - New expert medical opinions from top pediatric ENT doctors who stated that it was medically plausible, and even more likely, that Brian's choking was accidental. They refuted the prosecution's claim that it was impossible for Brian to swallow the paper towels himself, stating the gag reflex could have drawn the wad further in.
    - Federal court review determined Rosa’s trial lawyer was deficient for failing to consult appropriate experts.
- **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Not applicable, as the exoneration was based on the conclusion that the death was accidental, not due to another perpetrator.

### Podcast Research Notes

- **Key Quotes/Moments**:

- **Systemic Issues Identified**:
    - Ineffective Assistance of Counsel: Rosa's trial lawyer provided inadequate defense and expert consultation.
    - Flawed Expert Testimony: Reliance on expert testimony not grounded in pediatric airway expertise led to a false narrative.
    - Coercive Interrogation Tactics:  Questioning a non-English speaker without proper support and potentially implying benefits for confession.
    - Racial and Ethnic Bias:  Possible disproportionate prosecution of women of color in accidental child injury cases.
    - Immigration Status Vulnerability:  Deportation proceedings added another layer of injustice and complexity post-exoneration.
    - Shackling of incarcerated pregnant women during childbirth:  Dehumanizing and unnecessary practice.

- **Policy Recommendations/Calls to Action**:
    - Support local Innocence Organizations (mentioned at the end of the podcast).
    - Advocate for better legal representation for indigent defendants, especially non-English speakers.
    - Reform interrogation practices to prevent coercive tactics and ensure proper interpretation services.
    - Address potential racial and ethnic biases within the criminal justice system.
    - End the practice of shackling incarcerated women during childbirth.

- **Personal Narrative/Impact**:
    - Rosa’s personal narrative is centered around her dream of motherhood and how wrongful conviction stole 18 years of her life and her relationship with her children.
    - The podcast highlights her resilience in prison, relying on faith, and her struggle to adjust to freedom and reconnect with her now-adult children.
    -  It shows the profound emotional and familial damage caused by wrongful conviction, extending beyond incarceration to impact relationships, personal identity, and future prospects even after exoneration.
    - Despite exoneration and a kidney transplant, Rosa faces the ongoing challenge of rebuilding her life and dealing with the irreparable loss of time and family connection.
