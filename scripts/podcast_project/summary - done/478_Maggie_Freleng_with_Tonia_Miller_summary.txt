### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This episode of "Wrongful Conviction with Maggie Freeling" tells the story of Tanya Miller, who was wrongfully convicted of second-degree murder in the death of her 11-week-old daughter, Alicia, due to a misdiagnosis of shaken baby syndrome.  Tanya was incarcerated for 18 years based on this now contested medical diagnosis. The podcast details Tanya's early life, the events leading to Alicia's death, the flawed trial where the defense didn't challenge the shaken baby diagnosis, and the eventual exoneration after the Michigan Innocence Clinic took her case.  Key evidence for her exoneration included the discovery of acute bronchial pneumonia in Alicia's autopsy slides, which was missed during the original trial and supported Tanya's long-held belief that her daughter was sick. The episode highlights the devastating impact of junk science in the courtroom and the systemic issues that lead to wrongful convictions, particularly in cases involving mothers accused of harming their children. Tanya's story underscores the importance of challenging medical dogma and seeking justice for the wrongfully convicted.

- **Narrative of Original Crime**: Tanya Miller was accused of shaking her 11-week-old daughter, Alicia, to death.  The prosecution argued that Alicia's brain swelling, retinal hemorrhages, and bleeding around the brain were classic signs of shaken baby syndrome, implying intentional abuse by Tanya.
- **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - Testimony of Dr. Brian Hunter, the medical examiner who performed the autopsy, stating the "classic triad" of symptoms (retinal hemorrhages, bleeding on the outer layers of the brain, and brain swelling) indicated shaken baby syndrome.
- **Trial Defender Notes**: Tanya's original defense attorney, Edwin Heddinger, did not contest the shaken baby syndrome diagnosis.  His defense strategy focused on suggesting that someone else, possibly Alicia's father or paternal grandparents, could have caused the injuries, attempting to create reasonable doubt rather than directly challenging the medical evidence. He did not effectively present Alicia's prior medical history or question the shaken baby syndrome diagnosis itself.
- **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - Discovery of acute bronchial pneumonia in Alicia's autopsy slides, confirmed by expert pediatric lung pathologist Dr. Francis Green. This evidence was overlooked in the original trial and provided a natural cause of death, contradicting the shaken baby syndrome diagnosis.
    - Expert testimony from multiple leading experts in shaken baby syndrome who refuted the biomechanical possibility of shaking causing the triad of symptoms without neck injuries and highlighted alternative medical explanations for Alicia's condition.
    - Re-evaluation of Alicia's medical history, including prior instances of breathing problems and collapses, which were dismissed by doctors at the time.
- **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Not applicable, Tanya Miller was exonerated, and the death was determined to be likely due to natural causes (pneumonia) and misdiagnosis.
