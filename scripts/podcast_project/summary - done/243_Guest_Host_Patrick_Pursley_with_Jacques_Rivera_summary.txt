### Basic Podcast Information

- **Executive Summary**: This special episode of Wrongful Conviction features Patrick Persley interviewing Jacques Rivera, both men having been wrongfully convicted and incarcerated in Illinois' Stateville penitentiary. Rivera recounts his case, highlighting the egregious misconduct of corrupt Detective Renando Guevera and the compromised Judge Michael Close.  Despite the victim's initial identification of other perpetrators, Guevera targeted Rivera based on a fabricated eyewitness account from a misled child.  Rivera was wrongly convicted and served 23 years before being exonerated thanks to the Center on Wrongful Convictions. The episode details the key evidence leading to his exoneration, including the eyewitness recanting his testimony and evidence of Guevera's pervasive corruption. Rivera's story underscores systemic issues within the justice system and emphasizes the importance of hope and support for the wrongfully convicted.

### Wrongfully Convicted Individuals Featured

- **Name**: Jacques Rivera
- **Case Location**: Chicago, Illinois, USA
- **Conviction Year**: 1990 (inferred, incarcerated June 1990)
- **Exoneration Year**: 2011
- **Crime Convicted Of**: Murder, Wootle, Heatius, UUW (Unlawful Use of Weapon)
- **Sentence**: 80 years (60 years for murder, 20 years for Wootle and Heatius, 5 years for UUW)
- **Time Served**: 23 years
- **Contributing Factors**:
    - Police Misconduct: Corrupt Detective Renando Guevera fabricated evidence and misled a child eyewitness.
    - Eyewitness Misidentification: 11-year-old Orlando Lopez was misled by Guevera to identify Rivera.
    - Judicial Misconduct/Ineffective Counsel: Judge Michael Close, under suspicion of corruption, allowed questionable testimony and prevented key defense evidence (victim's initial identification) from being presented. Trial lawyer opted for bench trial and did not effectively present alibi.
- **Legal Status**: Exonerated, Certificate of Innocence awarded.
- **Organizations Involved**:
    - Center on Wrongful Convictions (Northwestern University Pritzker School of Law)
    - The Spanish Coalition (initially for community center funding)
- **Innocence Lawyer Details**: Catherine Crawford from Northwestern Center on Wrongful Convictions investigated and represented Jacques Rivera, leading to his exoneration.
- **Narrative of Original Crime**: On August 27, 1988, 16-year-old Felix Valentin was shot in his car in an area of Chicago known for gang activity.  While initially identifying members of the Imperial Gangsters as the shooters to responding officers, Detective Guevera pursued Jacques Rivera of the Latin Kings, based on a coerced eyewitness account.
- **Key Evidence at Trial (Prosecution)**:
    - Testimony of 11-year-old Orlando Lopez, who identified Rivera as the shooter, claiming to see him from behind wearing black and gold with a blonde ponytail, and that the car went in the direction of Latin Kings territory.
    - False testimony from Detective Guevera claiming that Rivera had a blonde ponytail at the time of arrest and that the victim Felix Valentin identified Rivera in a photo lineup at the hospital.
- **Trial Defender Notes**:
    - Trial lawyer advised against a jury trial and opted for a bench trial with Judge Close.
    - Lawyer did not effectively present the initial dying declaration of Felix Valentin to Officer Letrich.
    - Lawyer did not effectively utilize the alibi witness (mother of Rivera's children), fearing bias claims.
- **Key Evidence for Exoneration/Change in Legal Status**:
    - Recantation of eyewitness Orlando Lopez, who testified in 2011 that his identification of Rivera was false and coerced by Detective Guevera.
    - Testimony from Dr. treating Felix Valentin, stating it was impossible for Valentin to make an identification to Guevera due to being in a coma.
    - Evidence of Detective Renando Guevera's pattern of corruption and numerous wrongful convictions.
- **Actual Perpetrator Details**: Felix Valentin initially identified Jose Rodriguez as the shooter and Felix Nevis as the driver, both members of the Imperial Gangsters. This initial identification was disregarded by Detective Guevera.
