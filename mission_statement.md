# The Innocence Archive: Stories of Wrongfully Convicted Americans

## Mission Statement

The Innocence Archive is dedicated to documenting, preserving, and disseminating the stories of wrongfully convicted individuals across America. Through comprehensive research and digital archiving, we aim to create a centralized repository of information that illuminates the human cost of judicial errors while providing crucial resources for journalists, researchers, advocates, and the public. Our mission is to amplify unheard voices, facilitate informed discourse, and contribute to a more just legal system by increasing awareness of wrongful convictions and their systemic causes.

## Project Description

The Innocence Archive serves as a digital repository and research initiative focused on wrongful convictions in the United States. The project systematically collects, organizes, and presents information from diverse media sources, legal databases, and advocacy organizations to create a comprehensive resource on wrongful convictions.

### Core Objectives

1. **Documentation and Preservation**
   - Compile and catalog media coverage of wrongful conviction cases, including podcasts, documentaries, books, news articles, and academic publications
   - Create a searchable database of wrongfully convicted individuals, with particular attention to underpublicized cases
   - Preserve testimonies, legal documents, and personal narratives that might otherwise remain scattered or inaccessible

2. **Information Dissemination**
   - Provide journalists, filmmakers, and content creators with verified information and leads for developing in-depth coverage of wrongful conviction cases
   - Offer the public accessible entry points to understand complex legal issues through human stories
   - Create data visualizations and analytical tools that reveal patterns and systemic issues in wrongful convictions

3. **Resource Networking**
   - Catalog and highlight organizations working to address wrongful convictions, including innocence projects, legal advocacy groups, and post-exoneration support services
   - Connect individuals with relevant resources based on geographic location, case type, or specific needs
   - Facilitate awareness of policy reform initiatives addressing the root causes of wrongful convictions

### Methodology and Scope

The Innocence Archive operates as a research and information aggregation project, not as a legal service or primary journalistic outlet. Our work involves:

- Leveraging artificial intelligence (AI) technology to research and collate information efficiently and comprehensively
- Conducting extensive internet research to identify and document wrongful conviction cases
- Cross-referencing information from multiple sources to ensure accuracy
- Organizing data in accessible formats that enable further research and analysis
- Highlighting cases that have received limited public attention to encourage journalistic coverage

We are transparent about our use of AI as a research tool that enhances our ability to process large volumes of information, identify patterns, and connect disparate sources. While AI assists in the discovery and organization of content, all information is undergoing human review to ensure accuracy, ethical consideration, and contextual understanding. Please be aware, however, that all AI information is subject to error and bias, and not all information in this project is considered 100% accurate as of yet.

By serving as an information bridge between primary sources and those who can take action, The Innocence Archive aims to ensure that no wrongfully convicted person remains forgotten and that the systemic issues leading to wrongful convictions receive the attention necessary for meaningful reform.
